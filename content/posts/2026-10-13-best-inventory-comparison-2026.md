### The pricing trap most buyers walk into

I've watched three teams this year pick software based on the advertised starting price, then bill shock arrive at month four when add-ons kicked in. The inventory space is particularly guilty about this. Here's what each platform actually costs when you factor in everything most comparison pages leave out.

Cin7 Core charges per warehouse location. One location on the Starter plan runs $299/month for up to 500 orders. Add a second warehouse and you're at $598/month before any extra features. Move to the Grow plan at $799/month per location and you get barcode scanning, serial tracking, and purchase order automation built in. The Scale plan hits $1,999/month per location and unlocks advanced reporting plus API access for custom integrations.

NetSuite's pricing page lists a starting point around $999/month per user, but Gartner's 2025 ERP benchmarking data shows the average total cost of ownership for a mid-market deployment lands closer to $45,000 in year one when you include implementation services, data migration, and training. The base package doesn't include warehouse management, which sits as a separate module costing an additional $10,000 to $25,000 annually depending on site count.

Odoo uses a per-app pricing model that sounds affordable until you add the apps you actually need. Inventory alone costs $0 if it's your only module. Add CRM and the price jumps to $24/user/month. Add accounting on top and you're looking at $36/user/month. The catch: Odoo Community Edition keeps inventory free but strips out serialized inventory tracking and lot/batch management, both of which appear in almost every team's requirement list within the first quarter.

Fishbowl operates on a perpetual license model, which sounds like a win until you factor in the QuickBooks dependency. The standalone version starts at $2,995 one-time, and the QuickBooks-integrated version is $4,995. Annual maintenance runs approximately $600 per seat for updates and support. On top of that, you're paying QuickBooks itself, which means another $30 to $200/month depending on the tier. The total cost over three years often exceeds what a monthly subscription model would have been.

### What sets each platform apart in daily use

Cin7 Core excels at multi-channel retail operations. If your team sells through Shopify, Amazon, eBay, and your own storefront simultaneously, the automatic inventory sync across those channels saves at least three hours per week in manual reconciliation. The reorder point system uses historical sell-through rates to trigger purchase orders before stock runs out. My team tested this against a manual spreadsheet process and cut stockout incidents by roughly 40% over four months.

The mobile app works well inside a warehouse with reliable Wi-Fi. Staff can receive shipments, pick orders, and run cycle counts from handheld devices. The app drops all functionality the moment connectivity fails, which is a real problem for outdoor yards or buildings with dead zones.

NetSuite's financial engine is the main draw. Inventory transactions post directly to the general ledger without reconciliation steps. Multi-currency handling works automatically when you buy from international suppliers and sell domestically. If your business deals in euros, pesos, or yen regularly, the currency translation alone justifies the higher price tag.

The tradeoff is interface complexity. New users typically need two to three weeks of regular use before they stop clicking around looking for buttons that don't exist. Customizing reports requires either learning NetSuite's SuiteAnalytics workspace or paying a consultant around $150/hour. One manufacturing client I worked with spent eight hours and $1,200 just building a simple inventory aging report that should have been one-click.

Odoo's modular architecture gives you the flexibility to start small and expand without switching platforms. A small team can launch with just inventory and invoicing, then add CRM, manufacturing, and project management modules as revenue grows. The open-source codebase means you can modify workflows without waiting for vendor release cycles. This matters for teams with non-standard processes that off-the-shelf tools don't accommodate.

The community module marketplace is uneven. Some third-party apps work flawlessly. Others contain bugs, lack documentation, or conflict with core Odoo functions after updates. I've seen two teams lose weeks of work after installing community modules that weren't compatible with their Odoo version. Stick to officially endorsed modules whenever possible, and always test on a staging environment before pushing to production.

Fishbowl's strongest suit is its tight integration with QuickBooks Desktop. If your finance team already lives in QuickBooks Desktop and you need inventory management that feeds directly into your books, Fishbowl bridges that gap better than any other option on this list. Purchase orders flow into AP, sales orders update AR, and inventory adjustments post to the right general ledger accounts without manual journal entries.
