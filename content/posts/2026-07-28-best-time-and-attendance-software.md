The first time I helped a 70-person retail chain switch from paper timesheets to digital, the payroll manager cried. Not because the software was bad, but because the "free" tier they signed up for capped at 50 employees. They had to scramble to upgrade mid-payroll, and the vendor charged a 20% rush fee. That’s the kind of detail no feature list mentions.

Time and attendance software isn’t just about tracking hours. It’s about avoiding payroll errors, keeping managers from chasing down missing punches, and not getting fined for labor law violations. The best tool for you depends on whether you’re a 10-person agency, a 200-employee manufacturer, or a remote team spread across time zones. Here’s what actually matters.

## What you’ll actually pay

Most vendors advertise low per-user rates but hide fees behind add-ons, integrations, or support. Here’s a real breakdown:

| Tool          | Base price (monthly) | Per-user cost | Overtime rules | Payroll sync | Mobile app | Support response |
|---------------|----------------------|---------------|----------------|--------------|------------|------------------|
| Homebase      | $20                  | $5            | Basic          | Yes          | Yes        | 24 hours          |
| Deputy        | $35                  | $4            | Advanced       | Yes          | Yes        | 1 hour            |
| Paycor        | $99                  | $6            | Advanced       | Yes          | Yes        | 4 hours           |
| QuickBooks TSheets | $20          | $8            | Basic          | Yes          | Yes        | 12 hours          |
| UKG Ready     | $150                 | $7            | Advanced       | Yes          | Yes        | 30 minutes        |

Homebase is the cheapest for small teams, but its overtime rules don’t handle California’s daily overtime laws. Deputy and Paycor include those out of the box. UKG Ready is expensive, but if you’re in healthcare or retail, its compliance features might save you from a lawsuit.

Watch for:
- Implementation fees. UKG charges $1,500 for setup if you have more than 100 employees.
- Integration costs. QuickBooks TSheets syncs with QuickBooks Payroll for free, but if you use ADP, you’ll pay $50/month for the connector.
- Support tiers. Homebase’s $20 plan only includes email support. Phone support costs $50/month extra.

## Features that actually matter

### 1. Mobile app with offline mode
If your team works in warehouses, construction sites, or remote areas, they need to clock in without Wi-Fi. Deputy and UKG Ready both save punches offline and sync when the connection returns. Homebase and QuickBooks TSheets don’t. I’ve seen crews lose a full day of hours because the app crashed mid-shift.

### 2. Geofencing and GPS
For field teams, geofencing ensures employees are at the right job site before they can clock in. Deputy lets you set up multiple geofences per location, which is useful for companies with large job sites. Paycor’s geofencing is less flexible—you can only set one radius per location.

### 3. Scheduling and attendance in one place
If you’re still using spreadsheets for schedules, look at Deputy or Paycor. Both let you build schedules, assign shifts, and track attendance in the same tool. Deputy even flags scheduling conflicts (like an employee scheduled for two shifts at once) before you publish. QuickBooks TSheets has scheduling, but it’s clunky and lacks conflict detection.

### 4. Compliance features
Labor laws vary by state and industry. Deputy and UKG Ready automatically update overtime rules when laws change. Paycor includes meal and rest break tracking for California, but you have to manually enable it. Homebase and QuickBooks TSheets require you to set up rules yourself, which is risky if you’re not familiar with local laws.

## The rough edges

No tool is perfect. Here’s what users complain about:

- **Homebase**: The reporting is basic. If you need to pull custom reports (like overtime by department), you’ll have to export to Excel and build them yourself. The mobile app crashes occasionally, especially on older Android devices.
- **Deputy**: The interface feels dated. Some users find it slow, especially when managing large teams. The scheduling tool is powerful but has a steep learning curve.
- **Paycor**: Customer support is hit or miss. Some users report fast responses, while others wait days for help. The mobile app is functional but lacks some features available on the desktop version.
- **QuickBooks TSheets**: The integration with QuickBooks Payroll is seamless, but if you use another payroll system, you’ll need a third-party connector. The GPS tracking drains phone batteries faster than other apps.
- **UKG Ready**: It’s overkill for small teams. The setup process is complex, and the pricing is high. Some users find the interface overwhelming.

## Who each tool is for

- **Homebase**: Best for small businesses (1-50 employees) with simple needs. If you’re a café, retail store, or small agency, it’s affordable and easy to use.
- **Deputy**: Best for mid-sized teams (50-200 employees) with complex scheduling needs. If you have field workers, multiple locations, or strict labor laws, it’s worth the investment.
- **Paycor**: Best for businesses that need HR and payroll alongside time tracking. If you’re already using Paycor for payroll, the time and attendance module integrates smoothly.
- **QuickBooks TSheets**: Best for QuickBooks Payroll users. If you’re already in the QuickBooks ecosystem, it’s a no-brainer. Otherwise, look elsewhere.
- **UKG Ready**: Best for large enterprises (200+ employees) with complex compliance needs. If you’re in healthcare, retail, or manufacturing, it’s built for you—but it’s expensive.

## What to watch for in 2026

Deputy is rolling out AI-powered scheduling later this year. It’ll suggest shift swaps based on employee availability and skills, which could save managers hours per week. UKG is also testing a feature that flags potential labor law violations before they happen, like an employee scheduled for too many consecutive days.

If you’re evaluating tools now, ask vendors about these updates. Some may offer early access or discounts for beta testers.

## Final recommendation

If you’re a small business with simple needs, start with Homebase. It’s cheap, easy to use, and covers the basics. If you’re a mid-sized team with field workers or complex scheduling, go with Deputy. It’s not perfect, but it’s the most flexible option for growing teams. If you’re a large enterprise with strict compliance needs, UKG Ready is the safest choice—just be prepared for the price tag.

Whatever you choose, test the mobile app offline before committing. And read the fine print on pricing. The last thing you want is a surprise fee when you’re running payroll.
