I helped a 70-person retail chain move from paper timesheets to digital. The payroll manager cried. The "free" tier they chose capped at 50 employees. They had to upgrade mid-payroll and the vendor charged a 20% rush fee. Feature lists rarely mention details like this.

Time and attendance software tracks hours, prevents payroll errors, and stops managers from chasing missing punches. It also helps avoid labor law fines. The right tool depends on whether you run a 10-person agency, a 200-employee manufacturer, or a remote team across time zones.

## What you'll actually pay

Vendors often advertise low per-user rates but hide fees in add-ons, integrations, or support tiers.

|---------------|----------------------|---------------|----------------|--------------|------------|------------------|
| Homebase      | $20                  | $5            | Basic          | Yes          | Yes        | 24 hours          |
| Deputy        | $35                  | $4            | Advanced       | Yes          | Yes        | 1 hour            |
| Paycor        | $99                  | $6            | Advanced       | Yes          | Yes        | 4 hours           |
| QuickBooks TSheets | $20          | $8            | Basic          | Yes          | Yes        | 12 hours          |
| UKG Ready     | $150                 | $7            | Advanced       | Yes          | Yes        | 30 minutes        |

Homebase is cheapest for small teams, but its overtime rules do not handle California's daily overtime laws. Deputy and Paycor include those rules by default. UKG Ready costs more, but its compliance features can help if you work in healthcare or retail.

Watch for these fees:

- Implementation fees. UKG charges $1,500 for setup if you have more than 100 employees.
- Integration costs. QuickBooks TSheets syncs with QuickBooks Payroll for free. If you use ADP, you'll pay $50/month for the connector.
- Support tiers. Homebase's $20 plan includes email support only. Phone support costs $50/month extra.

## Features that matter

### 1. Mobile app with offline mode

Teams in warehouses, construction sites, or remote areas need to clock in without Wi-Fi. Deputy and UKG Ready save punches offline and sync when the connection returns. Homebase and QuickBooks TSheets do not. I have seen crews lose a full day of hours because the app crashed mid-shift.

### 2. Geofencing and GPS

Geofencing ensures field teams are at the right job site before they clock in. Deputy allows multiple geofences per location, which helps companies with large sites. Paycor is less flexible: you can set only one radius per location.

### 3. Scheduling and attendance in one place

If you still use spreadsheets for schedules, look at Deputy or Paycor. Both let you build schedules, assign shifts, and track attendance in one tool. Deputy flags conflicts, such as an employee scheduled for two shifts at once, before you publish. QuickBooks TSheets has scheduling, but it is clunky and lacks conflict detection.

### 4. Compliance features

Labor laws vary by state and industry. Deputy and UKG Ready update overtime rules automatically when laws change. Paycor includes meal and rest break tracking for California, but you must enable it manually. Homebase and QuickBooks TSheets require you to set up rules yourself. This is risky if you are not familiar with local laws.

## The rough edges

No tool is perfect. Here is what users complain about:

- **Homebase**: Reporting is basic. To pull custom reports like overtime by department, you must export to Excel and build them yourself. The mobile app crashes occasionally, especially on older Android devices.
- **Deputy**: The interface feels dated. Some users find it slow when managing large teams. The scheduling tool is powerful but has a steep learning curve.
- **Paycor**: Customer support is inconsistent. Some users report fast responses, while others wait days for help. The mobile app is functional but lacks features available on the desktop version.
- **QuickBooks TSheets**: Integration with QuickBooks Payroll works well. If you use another payroll system, you need a third-party connector. The GPS tracking drains phone batteries faster than other apps.
- **UKG Ready**: It is overkill for small teams. The setup process is complex, and the price is high. Some users find the interface overwhelming.

## Who each tool is for

- **Homebase**: Best for small businesses (1-50 employees) with simple needs. It is affordable and easy to use for cafes, retail stores, or small agencies.
- **Deputy**: Best for mid-sized teams (50-200 employees) with complex scheduling needs. It is worth the investment if you have field workers, multiple locations, or strict labor laws.
- **Paycor**: Best for businesses that need HR and payroll alongside time tracking. The time and attendance module integrates smoothly if you already use Paycor for payroll.
- **QuickBooks TSheets**: Best for QuickBooks Payroll users. If you are already in the QuickBooks ecosystem, it is a natural fit. Otherwise, look elsewhere.
- **UKG Ready**: Best for large enterprises (200+ employees) with complex compliance needs. It is built for healthcare, retail, or manufacturing, but it is expensive.

## What to watch for in 2026

Deputy is rolling out AI-powered scheduling later this year. It will suggest shift swaps based on employee availability and skills, which could save managers hours per week. UKG is testing a feature that flags potential labor law violations before they happen, such as an employee scheduled for too many consecutive days.

If you are evaluating tools now, ask vendors about these updates. Some may offer early access or discounts for beta testers.

## Final recommendation

For a small business with simple needs, start with Homebase. It is cheap, easy to use, and covers the basics. For a mid-sized team with field workers or complex scheduling, go with Deputy. It is not perfect, but it is the most flexible option for growing teams. For a large enterprise with strict compliance needs, UKG Ready is the safest choice. Be prepared for the price.

Test the mobile app offline before committing. Read the fine print on pricing.
