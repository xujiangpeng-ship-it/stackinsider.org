## What you’ll actually pay

Most vendors advertise per-employee-per-month pricing. The fine print hides setup fees, year-end filings, and integrations.

| Tool          | Base fee | Per employee | Year-end fee | Multi-state payroll | Contract length |
|---------------|----------|--------------|--------------|---------------------|-----------------|
| Gusto         | $40      | $6           | $0           | $12/state           | Month-to-month  |
| Rippling      | $35      | $8           | $0           | Included            | 12 months       |
| ADP Run       | $59      | $10          | $99          | $25/state           | 24 months       |
| Paychex Flex  | $39      | $5           | $120         | $15/state           | 12 months       |
| OnPay         | $40      | $6           | $0           | $10/state           | Month-to-month  |

Gusto’s pricing hasn’t changed since 2023. Rippling raised its per-employee fee from $7 to $8 in January 2026. ADP Run still locks you into a two-year contract; early termination costs 50% of remaining fees.

Hidden costs to watch:
- Direct deposit cutoff times. Gusto and OnPay require payroll to be submitted by 2 p.m. local time two business days before payday. ADP and Paychex offer same-day deposit for an extra $1.50 per employee.
- Integration fees. Rippling charges $99 to connect to QuickBooks Desktop. Gusto’s native QuickBooks Online sync is free but drops class tracking.
- Year-end filings. Paychex charges $120 for W-2s and 1099s. Gusto and OnPay include them.

## Features that actually matter

### Payroll processing
All five tools handle basic payroll. The differences show up in edge cases.

- Multi-state payroll: Rippling and ADP include it. Gusto and OnPay charge per state. Paychex caps at five states on the base plan.
- Off-cycle payments: Gusto and OnPay let you run an unscheduled payroll without fees. ADP charges $25 per off-cycle run. Rippling requires a support ticket.
- Tip reporting: Only ADP and Paychex support Form 8027 for restaurants. Gusto and OnPay require manual entry.

### Compliance
- Local taxes: Gusto and Rippling support all 11,000+ U.S. tax jurisdictions. ADP and Paychex miss about 300 small localities. OnPay covers 95%.
- ACA reporting: Rippling and ADP include 1094/1095 forms. Gusto and OnPay charge $150. Paychex charges $250.
- Garnishments: ADP and Paychex handle court-ordered deductions automatically. Gusto and Rippling require manual setup. OnPay doesn’t support them.

### Employee experience
- Mobile apps: Gusto and OnPay let employees update direct deposit and tax forms. Rippling’s app only shows pay stubs. ADP and Paychex require a desktop browser.
- Onboarding: Rippling and ADP pre-fill I-9s with E-Verify. Gusto and OnPay make employees enter data manually.
- PTO tracking: Gusto and OnPay include it. Rippling charges $4 per employee. ADP and Paychex require the next pricing tier.

## Where each tool falls short

### Gusto
- No native time-tracking. You’ll need a separate tool like TSheets or Homebase.
- Customer support moved to chat-only in 2025. Phone support costs $100 per call.
- Reports lack custom fields. You can’t add a cost center or project code.

### Rippling
- Migration is slow. Expect three to five weeks for data transfer from another system.
- The mobile app doesn’t support offline mode. Employees in remote areas can’t access pay stubs.
- No built-in expense reimbursement. You’ll need Rippling Expenses ($5 per employee).

### ADP Run
- Two-year contract. Early termination costs 50% of remaining fees.
- The interface feels outdated. Navigation requires too many clicks.
- No free trial. You commit before testing.

### Paychex Flex
- Year-end fees add up. $120 for W-2s and 1099s.
- The base plan caps at 50 employees. Larger teams pay more.
- Customer support is inconsistent. Some users report long hold times.

### OnPay
- No garnishment support. You’ll need a separate tool or manual calculations.
- Limited integrations. No native sync with Xero or NetSuite.
- Reports are basic. No drill-down or custom fields.

## What sets Rippling apart

Rippling’s strength is unification. It combines payroll, HR, IT, and expenses in one system. For teams already using Rippling for device management or expense tracking, adding payroll saves time.

- Single sign-on for all tools. Employees access payroll, benefits, and IT from one dashboard.
- Automated onboarding. New hires get a laptop, email, and payroll setup in one flow.
- Global payroll. Rippling supports 100+ countries. Gusto and OnPay are U.S.-only.

The trade-off is complexity. Rippling’s interface has more menus and settings. Small teams without an HR person may find it overwhelming.

## What sets Gusto apart

Gusto’s simplicity is its advantage. The interface is clean, and the workflow is intuitive. For teams under 50 employees who want payroll without extra features, Gusto is the easiest choice.

- No contract. You can cancel anytime.
- Free year-end filings. No surprise fees.
- Good customer support. Chat is responsive, and phone support is available for $100 per call.

The trade-off is limited scalability. Gusto lacks advanced reporting and global payroll.

## Who each tool is for

- **Gusto**: Teams under 50 employees who want simple, affordable payroll.
- **Rippling**: Teams using Rippling for HR or IT who want unified workflows.
- **ADP Run**: Teams that need advanced compliance and don’t mind a long contract.
- **Paychex Flex**: Teams that want a full-service provider with dedicated support.
- **OnPay**: Teams under 100 employees who want low-cost payroll with good customer service.

## The rough edges

No tool is perfect. Here’s what users complain about most.

- **Gusto**: Chat-only support frustrates teams who prefer phone calls.
- **Rippling**: Migration takes longer than promised. Data transfer can be messy.
- **ADP Run**: The interface feels outdated. Navigation is clunky.
- **Paychex Flex**: Year-end fees add up. The base plan caps at 50 employees.
- **OnPay**: No garnishment support. Limited integrations.

## What to watch in 2027

- Gusto is testing a global payroll beta. If it launches, it could compete with Rippling.
- Rippling plans to add expense reimbursement to its core payroll product. The $5 per employee fee may disappear.
- ADP is updating its interface. A new design is expected in early 2027.

If you’re a small team that wants simple payroll, Gusto is still the best choice. If you’re using Rippling for HR or IT, adding payroll makes sense. For advanced compliance, ADP Run is the safest bet. Avoid Paychex Flex if you hate year-end fees. OnPay is a good low-cost option, but it lacks garnishment support.
