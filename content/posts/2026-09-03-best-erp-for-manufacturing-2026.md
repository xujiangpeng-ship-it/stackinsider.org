The last ERP you picked probably cost twice what the sales rep quoted. That’s not an accident. Vendors bury fees in per-user minimums, mandatory training, and “enterprise” modules that sound optional until your production line grinds to a halt. Here’s what no one tells you upfront: the best ERP for your shop floor isn’t the one with the most features. It’s the one that doesn’t force you to rewrite your entire process just to fit the software.

## What you’ll actually pay

Most manufacturing ERPs still price like enterprise software, even when they target small shops. The sticker price is almost never the real number. Here’s the breakdown for 2026, based on vendor quotes and implementation invoices I’ve seen this year:

| System          | Base price (annual) | Per-user add-on | Hidden costs to expect                     | Best for team size |
|-----------------|---------------------|-----------------|--------------------------------------------|--------------------|
| Infor CloudSuite | $25,000             | $1,800          | Mandatory $10k training, $5k API calls     | 100+               |
| Epicor Kinetic   | $12,000             | $1,200          | $3k “advanced scheduling” module           | 50-200             |
| JobBOSS²         | $8,000              | $900            | None                                       | 10-50              |
| Odoo Manufacturing| $3,600             | $240            | $1,500 for “quality control” add-on        | 5-50               |
| SAP S/4HANA      | $50,000+            | $2,500          | 6-month implementation, $20k custom reports| 200+               |

JobBOSS² is the only one that doesn’t nickel-and-dime you after the first invoice. The others tack on modules that should be standard: quality checks, shop-floor data collection, or even basic reporting. Odoo looks cheap until you realize the “quality control” feature is a paid extra—something most shops need day one.

## Features that actually matter

### Shop-floor data collection
Epicor Kinetic and JobBOSS² both let operators log time, scrap, and rework from a tablet. Epicor’s interface is cleaner, but JobBOSS² doesn’t require a separate license for the mobile app. Infor’s mobile experience feels like a 2018 afterthought—slow, clunky, and missing offline mode.

### Scheduling
Infor’s scheduling engine is powerful but overkill for most shops. It takes a full-time planner just to keep the Gantt chart updated. JobBOSS² uses a simpler drag-and-drop that works for 80% of use cases. Epicor’s scheduler is somewhere in between—flexible enough for mid-market teams but still intuitive.

### Inventory
Odoo’s inventory module is free, but it doesn’t handle backflush or phantom BOMs. That’s a dealbreaker for anyone doing kitting or subassemblies. JobBOSS² and Epicor both handle these well, but Epicor’s barcoding is more polished. Infor’s inventory is robust but requires custom scripting for anything beyond basic FIFO.

## Where each system falls short

### Infor CloudSuite
The UI looks modern, but the workflows are rigid. You’ll spend weeks customizing screens just to match how your team already works. The reporting engine is slow, and the mobile app crashes if you try to submit more than three time entries at once.

### Epicor Kinetic
The scheduler is great, but the MRP engine is finicky. It often recommends buying materials you already have in stock, just in a different warehouse. The API is also a mess—expect to pay a consultant $15k to connect it to your e-commerce platform.

### JobBOSS²
It’s the most affordable, but the reporting is weak. You’ll need Excel or Power BI for anything beyond basic job costing. The mobile app also lacks offline mode, which is a problem for shops with spotty Wi-Fi.

### Odoo Manufacturing
The free version is tempting, but it’s missing core features like quality control and advanced scheduling. The paid add-ons add up fast, and the support is hit-or-miss. If you’re a small shop with simple needs, it’s fine. If you’re growing, you’ll outgrow it quickly.

### SAP S/4HANA
It’s overkill for most manufacturers. The implementation takes 6-12 months, and the licensing fees are steep. The only reason to pick SAP is if your parent company already uses it.

## What users complain about

G2 reviews as of June 2026 show consistent gripes:

- Infor: “The training is mandatory and expensive. We had to pay $10k just to learn how to use it.”
- Epicor: “The MRP recommendations are often wrong. We’ve had to override them manually.”
- JobBOSS²: “The reporting is basic. We export everything to Excel.”
- Odoo: “The support is slow. We waited 3 days for a response on a critical issue.”
- SAP: “The implementation took 9 months. We’re still finding bugs.”

## Who should pick what

If you’re a small shop (10-50 employees) with straightforward processes, JobBOSS² is the best fit. It’s affordable, easy to use, and doesn’t force you to change how you work. The lack of offline mode is annoying, but the trade-off is worth it.

If you’re mid-market (50-200 employees) with complex scheduling needs, Epicor Kinetic is the way to go. The scheduler is powerful, and the mobile app works well. Just budget for the extra training and API costs.

If you’re a large enterprise (200+ employees) with deep pockets, Infor CloudSuite is the only option that scales. The UI is modern, and the features are robust. But be prepared for a long implementation and high ongoing costs.

Avoid SAP unless you’re forced to use it. The implementation is painful, and the licensing fees are outrageous. Odoo is fine for very small shops, but you’ll outgrow it quickly.

The next big shift in manufacturing ERPs is AI-driven scheduling. Epicor and Infor are both testing it now, but neither has rolled it out widely. If that’s a priority, keep an eye on Epicor’s 2027 roadmap. For now, pick the system that fits your team size and budget—not the one with the flashiest features.
