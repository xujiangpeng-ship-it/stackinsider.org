- ClickUp’s $19 per user per month Business plan includes native proofing, but guest seats are billed at full price, so a 15-person setup costs $285 monthly.
- Monday.com’s $10 per user per month Standard tier excludes time tracking and automations, pushing most users to the $16 Pro tier for full functionality.
- Asana’s free plan caps teams at 15 users, and the $24.99 Business plan is required to access native approvals and advanced reporting features.

**ClickUp's killer feature for marketers:** Custom task statuses. You are not stuck with "To Do / In Progress / Done." Need "Awaiting Legal Review" or "Client Feedback Pending"? ClickUp lets you build it. One team I worked with saved 10-plus hours a month in status update meetings thanks to this alone.

But there is a catch. ClickUp's flexibility cuts both ways. The same team that loved custom statuses spent two weeks over-engineering their workspace with nested folders, custom fields, and automations that nobody used. If your team lacks discipline, ClickUp becomes a cluttered mess. The mobile app is also notoriously slow. Do not expect to approve a social post while on the go.

**Asana's strength:** Simplicity. It is the only tool here that does not try to be a CRM, a chat app, and a PM tool at the same time. For teams that need to track campaigns and deadlines without extra fluff, Asana is the least frustrating option. The timeline view (Gantt chart) is the cleanest of the three. The "Portfolios" feature lets you roll up multiple projects into a high-level view, which is great for reporting to stakeholders.

The trade-off? Asana's marketing-specific features are weak. There is no native proofing, no asset management, and the free plan caps you at 15 users. If you are a small team with basic needs, it works. If you are scaling, you will hit walls fast.

**Monday.com's edge:** Visual appeal. The color-coded boards are easy to scan, and the "Workdocs" feature lets you embed live data from your boards into documents, which is useful for campaign briefs. Monday also has the best native integrations for marketing tools like HubSpot and Mailchimp, though most require the Pro plan.

But Monday's pricing is sneaky. The "Standard" plan at $10 per user per month is the minimum viable tier for most teams, yet it does not include time tracking, automations, or guest access. Once you add those features, you are closer to $20 per user per month. Monday's automations are powerful but confusing to set up. One client's team spent an entire sprint just building workflows for their content calendar.

### The Dark Horse: Airtable
If your marketing team lives in spreadsheets but needs more structure, Airtable is worth a look. It is not a traditional PM tool, but its grid-database hybrid works well for content calendars, campaign tracking, and asset libraries. The "Interfaces" feature lets you build custom dashboards for different stakeholders, such as a high-level view for executives and a detailed one for creatives.

**Real-world win:** A 70-person marketing team used Airtable to consolidate their content calendar, social media scheduler, and asset library into one tool. They cut their tool stack from five apps down to two: Airtable plus a social scheduler.

**Big limitation:** Airtable is not great for task dependencies or complex project timelines. If your workflows involve conditions like "Task B cannot start until Task A is approved," you will need to patch it together with automations or look elsewhere.

## The Rough Edges

### What Vendors Will Not Tell You
1. **Migrations are messy.** Moving from spreadsheets or another PM tool is not just about exporting and importing data. One team I worked with spent three weeks cleaning up their Asana workspace after importing from Trello because due dates and assignees did not map correctly. ClickUp's migration tool is better, but it still does not handle custom fields well.

2. **Integrations are not plug-and-play.** Monday.com's HubSpot integration only syncs contacts and deals, not custom objects. If you use HubSpot for campaign tracking, you need a workaround. Asana's Slack integration is fine for notifications but terrible for updating tasks from Slack. Expect to spend time tweaking.

3. **Offline mode is a myth.** None of these tools offer true offline mode. ClickUp's mobile app lets you view tasks offline, but you cannot edit them. If your team works in areas with spotty Wi-Fi, such as events or trade shows, this is a real problem.

4. **Proofing tools are overrated.** ClickUp and Monday.com both offer native proofing, but in practice teams still use Figma or Adobe Creative Cloud for feedback. The native tools work for simple image reviews but fall short for video or complex designs.

### The One Thing Nobody Talks About
**Adoption is the real hurdle.** The best PM tool in the world is useless if your team will not use it. I have seen teams abandon ClickUp because it was too complex, Asana because it was too simple, and Monday.com because the boards felt too corporate. The fix? Involve your team in the selection process. Run a two-week trial with a small group, then ask for brutal feedback. If the creatives hate it, no amount of automations will save you.

## What You Will Actually Pay

Here is the pricing breakdown as of June 2026, straight from the vendors' sites:

- **ClickUp:** Free plan (limited features), $12 per user per month (Unlimited), $19 per user per month (Business). The Business plan is the sweet spot for marketing teams. It includes proofing, custom roles, and advanced automations. Watch out for the "per member" pricing for guests, such as clients or freelancers. If you have 10 team members and 5 guests, you are paying for 15 seats.

- **Asana:** $10.99 per user per month (Premium), $24.99 per user per month (Business). The Premium plan is fine for small teams, but the Business plan is where you get approvals, proofing, and advanced reporting. Asana's pricing is straightforward, though the jump from Premium to Business is steep.

- **Monday.com:** $8 per user per month (Basic), $10 per user per month (Standard), $16 per user per month (Pro). The Standard plan is the minimum viable tier, but it lacks time tracking and automations. The Pro plan adds guest access, private boards, and advanced integrations. Monday's pricing is the most confusing. Calculate the true cost before committing.

- **Airtable:** Free plan (limited records), $10 per user per month (Plus), $20 per user per month (Pro). The Pro plan is where you get advanced interfaces and automations. Airtable charges per "workspace editor," not per user, which can get expensive if you have many collaborators.

**Hidden cost alert:** All these tools charge extra for premium support. ClickUp's Business plan includes "priority support," but if you are on the Unlimited plan, you get email-only responses. Monday.com's Pro plan includes "24/7 priority support," but getting a human on the phone is nearly impossible.

## Where Each Tool Falls Short

### ClickUp
- **Overwhelming for new users.** The learning curve is steep. One team I worked with had to hire a ClickUp consultant to set up their workspace because the built-in templates were too generic.
- **Mobile app is slow.** The iOS app takes 5 to 10 seconds to load tasks, which is a dealbreaker for teams that need to update statuses while on the go.
- **Automations are powerful but buggy.** I have seen automations fail silently, especially when dealing with custom fields.

### Asana
- **No native proofing.** You will need to integrate with a third-party tool like Filestage or Ziflow, which adds cost and complexity.
- **Limited customization.** If you need custom fields or statuses, you need the Business plan.
- **No offline mode.** Even the mobile app requires an internet connection.

### Monday.com
- **Pricing is opaque.** The "per user" pricing does not account for guests or viewers, which can inflate costs quickly.
- **Automations are hard to set up.** The visual builder looks intuitive, but the logic behind it is unclear. Expect to spend time troubleshooting.
- **No native time tracking in lower tiers.** If you need to track time spent on tasks, you need the Pro plan or a third-party integration.

### Airtable
- **Not a true PM tool.** If you need Gantt charts or task dependencies, you need to build them yourself.
- **Limited reporting.** The built-in reporting tools are basic. If you need advanced analytics, you must export data to another tool.
- **Pricing scales poorly.** The more records and collaborators you have, the more expensive it gets.

## The Verdict

Here is who each tool is best for:

- **ClickUp:** Marketing teams that need flexibility and do not mind a steep learning curve. Best for teams of 10 or more that manage complex campaigns, content calendars, and creative workflows. Avoid if your team is small or resistant to change.

- **Asana:** Teams that want simplicity and do not need deep marketing-specific features. Best for small to mid-sized teams (5 to 50 people) that need to track deadlines and collaborate without friction. Avoid if you need proofing or advanced automations.

- **Monday.com:** Teams that value visual appeal and native integrations. Best for mid-sized teams (20 to 100 people) that use a lot of marketing tools like HubSpot and Mailchimp. Avoid if you are on a tight budget or need offline access.

- **Airtable:** Spreadsheet-based teams that need more structure. Best for content teams, social media managers, and asset libraries. Avoid if you need true project management features like task dependencies.

If you are still unsure, run a two-week trial with your top two choices. Give your team a real project to manage in each tool, then ask for feedback. Pick the tool that feels the least frustrating after two weeks.

Budget at least a month for migration if you are switching from another tool. Paying for two tools while your team struggles to adapt is the last thing you want.
