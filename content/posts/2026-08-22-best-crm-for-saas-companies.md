Most SaaS teams pick a CRM because it promises "growth" or "scalability." Six months later, they’re stuck exporting spreadsheets to fix broken renewal workflows or paying for seats their engineers never use. The right CRM isn’t the one with the most features—it’s the one that fits how your team sells, supports, and retains customers without creating extra work.

Here’s what matters for SaaS: subscription tracking, billing sync, product usage data, and churn signals. Not every CRM handles these well. Some force you into expensive add-ons or custom development. Others look simple until you try to automate a drip campaign for trial users who hit a specific feature threshold.

I’ve helped teams migrate from HubSpot to Salesforce, from Pipedrive to Close, and from spreadsheets to Zoho. The biggest mistake? Assuming the CRM will adapt to your workflow. In reality, you’ll adapt to the CRM—so pick one that matches how your team already works, not how you wish they worked.

## What SaaS teams actually need from a CRM

Forget generic sales pipelines. SaaS teams need four things:

1. **Subscription visibility.** You should see MRR, plan type, and renewal date without clicking through five screens. If your CRM treats subscriptions like one-off deals, you’ll waste time reconciling reports.
2. **Product usage data.** Trial users who hit a feature three times convert at 4x the rate of those who don’t. If your CRM can’t pull this from Segment, Mixpanel, or your own database, you’re flying blind.
3. **Billing sync.** When a customer upgrades, their invoice should update automatically. If your CRM and billing tool (Stripe, Chargebee, etc.) don’t talk, your finance team will hate you.
4. **Churn signals.** A CRM should flag customers who haven’t logged in for 30 days or whose usage dropped 20% month-over-month. Most don’t—you’ll need a workaround.

If a CRM can’t do these out of the box, you’ll spend money on integrations or custom fields. That’s fine if you have a developer, but most SaaS teams don’t.

## The contenders: what you’ll actually pay

Here’s how the main options stack up for a 20-person SaaS team with 1,000 customers. Prices are annual, per user, and include the features SaaS teams need (not the vendor’s "starter" tier).

**HubSpot** is the default choice for SaaS, but it’s expensive. The "Subscription Management" add-on costs $50/user/month. Without it, you’re manually updating deal stages when a customer upgrades. The Stripe sync works, but product usage data requires a separate integration (like HubSpot Operations Hub, another $50/user/month). If you’re already using HubSpot for marketing, this might make sense—but if you’re starting from scratch, there are cheaper options.

**Salesforce** is overkill for most SaaS teams. The subscription tracking is solid, but product usage data requires Salesforce CPQ (another $75/user/month). The billing sync works, but the interface is cluttered. Salesforce is worth it if you have a dedicated sales ops team and need advanced forecasting. Otherwise, it’s like using a sledgehammer to hang a picture.

**Close** is built for sales teams, not SaaS. It’s great for outbound calls and sequences, but it doesn’t track subscriptions or product usage. If your sales team lives in the CRM all day, Close is a good fit. If you need to sync with billing or track churn, you’ll need integrations.

**Pipedrive** is simple and affordable, but it’s not built for SaaS. There’s no subscription tracking or billing sync. You can hack it with custom fields, but it’s not ideal. Pipedrive is best for teams that sell one-off deals, not recurring subscriptions.

**Zoho CRM** is the dark horse. It’s cheap, and it does everything SaaS teams need out of the box. Subscription tracking, billing sync (with Zoho Subscriptions or Stripe), and product usage data (via Zoho Analytics). The churn signals aren’t perfect, but they’re better than nothing. The downside? The interface feels dated, and the mobile app is slow. If you’re on a budget, Zoho is the best value.

**Copper** is Gmail-native, which is great if your team lives in Google Workspace. But it doesn’t track subscriptions or product usage. Copper is best for teams that want a simple CRM and don’t need SaaS-specific features.

## Where each CRM shines (and where it doesn’t)

### HubSpot: Best for teams already using HubSpot for marketing
HubSpot’s strength is its ecosystem. If you’re using HubSpot for marketing, the CRM integrates seamlessly. The Stripe sync is reliable, and the reporting is solid. The downside? It’s expensive. The "Subscription Management" add-on is a must for SaaS teams, and the product usage data requires another add-on. If you’re not already using HubSpot, there’s no reason to start.

**What works:**
- Stripe sync is reliable.
- Reporting is customizable.
- Integrates with HubSpot’s marketing tools.

**What doesn’t:**
- Subscription tracking costs extra.
- Product usage data requires another add-on.
- Expensive for what you get.

### Salesforce: Best for large teams with complex needs
Salesforce is the most powerful CRM on the market, but it’s overkill for most SaaS teams. The subscription tracking is solid, and the billing sync works well. The product usage data requires Salesforce CPQ, which is expensive. Salesforce is worth it if you have a dedicated sales ops team and need advanced forecasting. Otherwise, it’s too much.

**What works:**
- Subscription tracking is built-in.
- Billing sync is reliable.
- Advanced forecasting tools.

**What doesn’t:**
- Product usage data costs extra.
- Interface is cluttered.
- Expensive for small teams.

### Close: Best for outbound sales teams
Close is built for sales teams, not SaaS. It’s great for outbound calls and sequences, but it doesn’t track subscriptions or product usage. If your sales team lives in the CRM all day, Close is a good fit. If you need to sync with billing or track churn, you’ll need integrations.

**What works:**
- Built for outbound sales.
- Sequences and call tracking are excellent.
- Simple interface.

**What doesn’t:**
- No subscription tracking.
- No billing sync.
- No product usage data.

### Zoho CRM: Best for budget-conscious teams
Zoho CRM is the best value for SaaS teams. It’s cheap, and it does everything out of the box. Subscription tracking, billing sync, and product usage data are all included. The churn signals aren’t perfect, but they’re better than nothing. The downside? The interface feels dated, and the mobile app is slow.

**What works:**
- Subscription tracking is built-in.
- Billing sync works with Stripe and Zoho Subscriptions.
- Product usage data is included.
- Cheap.

**What doesn’t:**
- Interface feels dated.
- Mobile app is slow.
- Churn signals could be better.

## What to watch for in 2026

Two trends are shaping the CRM space for SaaS teams:

1. **Native product usage data.** More CRMs are adding this, but most still require integrations. Zoho CRM is the exception—it includes product usage data out of the box.
2. **Pricing transparency.** Vendors are starting to unbundle features, which means you’ll pay more for what you actually need. HubSpot’s subscription add-on is a good example—it’s a must for SaaS teams, but it’s not included in the base price.

If you’re evaluating CRMs, ask about these two things. If a vendor can’t give you a straight answer, move on.

## Who should pick what

- **Small SaaS teams (1-10 people):** Zoho CRM. It’s cheap, and it does everything you need. The interface isn’t great, but it’s the best value.
- **Mid-sized SaaS teams (11-50 people):** HubSpot. It’s expensive, but it integrates with your marketing tools. The subscription add-on is a must.
- **Large SaaS teams (50+ people):** Salesforce. It’s overkill for most teams, but if you have a dedicated sales ops team, it’s worth it.
- **Outbound sales teams:** Close. It’s not built for SaaS, but it’s the best CRM for outbound sales. You’ll need integrations for billing and product usage.

If you’re still unsure, start with a free trial. Most CRMs offer 14 days—enough time to test the features that matter for SaaS. Don’t sign a year-long contract until you’ve tried it with real data.
