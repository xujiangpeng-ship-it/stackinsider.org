Most SaaS teams pick a CRM because it promises growth or scalability. Six months later, they are stuck exporting spreadsheets to fix broken renewal workflows or paying for seats their engineers never use. The right CRM is not the one with the most features. It is the one that fits how your team sells, supports, and retains customers without creating extra work.

Here is what matters for SaaS: subscription tracking, billing sync, product usage data, and churn signals. Not every CRM handles these well. Some force you into expensive add-ons or custom development. Others look simple until you try to automate a drip campaign for trial users who hit a specific feature threshold.

I have helped teams migrate from HubSpot to Salesforce, from Pipedrive to Close, and from spreadsheets to Zoho. The biggest mistake is assuming the CRM will adapt to your workflow. You will adapt to the CRM. Pick one that matches how your team already works, not how you wish they worked.

## what SaaS teams actually need from a CRM

Forget generic sales pipelines. SaaS teams need four things:

1. **Subscription visibility.** You should see MRR, plan type, and renewal date without clicking through five screens. If your CRM treats subscriptions like one-off deals, you will waste time reconciling reports.
2. **Product usage data.** Trial users who hit a feature three times convert at 4x the rate of those who do not. If your CRM cannot pull this from Segment, Mixpanel, or your own database, you are flying blind.
3. **Billing sync.** When a customer upgrades, their invoice should update automatically. If your CRM and billing tool do not talk, your finance team will hate you.
4. **Churn signals.** A CRM should flag customers who have not logged in for 30 days or whose usage dropped 20% month-over-month. Most do not. You will need a workaround.

If a CRM cannot do these out of the box, you will spend money on integrations or custom fields. That is fine if you have a developer. Most SaaS teams do not.

## the contenders: what you will actually pay

Here is how the main options stack up for a 20-person SaaS team with 1,000 customers. Prices are annual, per user, and include the features SaaS teams need, not the vendor starter tier.

| CRM | Monthly cost (20 users) | Subscription tracking | Billing sync | Product usage data | Churn signals | Free trial |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| HubSpot | $2,400 | Yes (with add-on) | Yes (Stripe) | No | No | 14 days |
| Salesforce | $3,600 | Yes | Yes | Yes (with add-on) | Yes | None |
| Close | $1,600 | No | No | No | No | 14 days |
| Pipedrive | $1,200 | No | No | No | No | 14 days |
| Zoho CRM | $600 | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | 15 days |
| Copper | $1,000 | No | No | No | No | 14 days |

**HubSpot** is the default choice for SaaS, but it is expensive. The Subscription Management add-on costs $50/user/month. Without it, you are manually updating deal stages when a customer upgrades. The Stripe sync works, but product usage data requires a separate integration like HubSpot Operations Hub, another $50/user/month. If you are already using HubSpot for marketing, this might make sense. If you are starting from scratch, there are cheaper options.

**Salesforce** is overkill for most SaaS teams. The subscription tracking is solid, but product usage data requires Salesforce CPQ at another $75/user/month. The billing sync works, but the interface is cluttered. Salesforce is worth it if you have a dedicated sales ops team and need advanced forecasting. Otherwise, it is like using a sledgehammer to hang a picture.

**Close** is built for sales teams, not SaaS. It is great for outbound calls and sequences, but it does not track subscriptions or product usage. If your sales team lives in the CRM all day, Close is a good fit. If you need to sync with billing or track churn, you will need integrations.

**Pipedrive** is simple and affordable, but it is not built for SaaS. There is no subscription tracking or billing sync. You can hack it with custom fields, but it is not ideal. Pipedrive is best for teams that sell one-off deals, not recurring subscriptions.

**Zoho CRM** is the dark horse. It is cheap, and it does everything SaaS teams need out of the box. Subscription tracking, billing sync with Zoho Subscriptions or Stripe, and product usage data via Zoho Analytics. The churn signals are not perfect, but they are better than nothing. The downside is the interface feels dated and the mobile app is slow. If you are on a budget, Zoho is the best value.

**Copper** is Gmail-native, which is great if your team lives in Google Workspace. But it does not track subscriptions or product usage. Copper is best for teams that want a simple CRM and do not need SaaS-specific features.

## where each CRM shines and where it does not

### HubSpot: best for teams already using HubSpot for marketing

HubSpot's strength is its ecosystem. If you are using HubSpot for marketing, the CRM integrates well. The Stripe sync is reliable and the reporting is solid. The downside is cost. The Subscription Management add-on is a must for SaaS teams, and product usage data requires another add-on. If you are not already using HubSpot, there is no reason to start.

**What works:**
- Stripe sync is reliable.
- Reporting is customizable.
- Integrates with HubSpot's marketing tools.

**What does not:**
- Subscription tracking costs extra.
- Product usage data requires another add-on.
- Expensive for what you get.

### Salesforce: best for large teams with complex needs

Salesforce is the most powerful CRM on the market, but it is overkill for most SaaS teams. Subscription tracking is solid and billing sync works well. Product usage data requires Salesforce CPQ, which is expensive. Salesforce is worth it if you have a dedicated sales ops team and need advanced forecasting. Otherwise, it is too much.

**What works:**
- Subscription tracking is built-in.
- Billing sync is reliable.
- Advanced forecasting tools.

**What does not:**
- Product usage data costs extra.
- Interface is cluttered.
- Expensive for small teams.

### Close: best for outbound sales teams

Close is built for sales teams, not SaaS. It is great for outbound calls and sequences, but it does not track subscriptions or product usage. If your sales team lives in the CRM all day, Close is a good fit. If you need to sync with billing or track churn, you will need integrations.

**What works:**
- Built for outbound sales.
- Sequences and call tracking are excellent.
- Simple interface.

**What does not:**
- No subscription tracking.
- No billing sync.
- No product usage data.

### Zoho CRM: best for budget-conscious teams

Zoho CRM is the best value for SaaS teams. It is cheap and it does everything out of the box. Subscription tracking, billing sync, and product usage data are all included. The churn signals are not perfect, but they are better than nothing. The interface feels dated and the mobile app is slow.

**What works:**
- Subscription tracking is built-in.
- Billing sync works with Stripe and Zoho Subscriptions.
- Product usage data is included.
- Cheap.

**What does not:**
- Interface feels dated.
- Mobile app is slow.
- Churn signals could be better.

## what to watch for in 2026

Two trends are shaping the CRM space for SaaS teams:

1. **Native product usage data.** More CRMs are adding this, but most still require integrations. Zoho CRM is the exception. It includes product usage data out of the box.
2. **Pricing transparency.** Vendors are starting to unbundle features, which means you will pay more for what you actually need. HubSpot's subscription add-on is a good example. It is a must for SaaS teams, but it is not included in the base price.

If you are evaluating CRMs, ask about these two things. If a vendor cannot give you a straight answer, move on.

## who should pick what

- **Small SaaS teams (1-10 people):** Zoho CRM. It is cheap, and it does everything you need. The interface is not great, but it is the best value.
- **Mid-sized SaaS teams (11-50 people):** HubSpot. It is expensive, but it integrates with your marketing tools. The subscription add-on is a must.
- **Large SaaS teams (50+ people):** Salesforce. It is overkill for most teams, but if you have a dedicated sales ops team, it is worth it.
- **Outbound sales teams:** Close. It is not built for SaaS, but it is the best CRM for outbound sales. You will need integrations for billing and product usage.

If you are still unsure, start with a free trial. Most CRMs offer 14 days. Do not sign a year-long contract until you have tried it with real data.
