Less Annoying CRM costs $15 per user per month. There are no tiers, no upsells, and no annual contracts. That’s the entire pricing page. For small teams who’ve been burned by Salesforce’s $300/user/year quotes or HubSpot’s sudden $1,200 annual jumps, the simplicity feels like a trick. It’s not.

I’ve set this up for three different service businesses: a 5-person marketing agency, a 12-person HVAC contractor, and a 20-person nonprofit. Each time, the onboarding call lasted 20 minutes. The CRM rep walked through the pipeline stages, imported contacts from a CSV, and left the team to test the workflow. No sales pitch, no upsell script. That’s unusual enough to mention.

## What you’ll actually pay

$15 per user per month, billed monthly. No discounts for annual prepay. No per-contact fees. No storage limits. No add-ons.

The only extra cost is if you want phone support. The base plan includes email and live chat. Phone support costs $50 per month for the entire team, not per user. The HVAC team paid it for three months while they migrated from a spreadsheet. After that, they canceled it and haven’t needed it since.

There’s no free tier. The 30-day trial is full-featured, no credit card required. After the trial, you either pay or lose access. No downgrades, no grandfathered pricing.

## What sets it apart

The pipeline view is the simplest I’ve seen. You get one default pipeline with stages you can rename. No complex branching, no conditional logic. For the marketing agency, that was a relief. They’d tried Pipedrive and spent two weeks arguing over pipeline rules. With Less Annoying, they set four stages: Lead, Proposal, Won, Lost. Done.

The contact record is a single page. No tabs, no pop-ups. Everything—notes, tasks, emails, files—lives in one scroll. The HVAC team loved this. Their previous CRM, Insightly, buried contact history behind three clicks. Here, a technician could see the last service call, the customer’s preferred contact time, and the model number of their unit in one glance.

Search works like Google. Type a name, a phone number, or even a partial email. Results appear instantly. No filters, no dropdowns. The nonprofit team used this daily. They’d get a call from a donor, type the first name, and see the last donation amount and the notes from their last conversation.

## The rough edges

No native mobile app. The mobile site works, but it’s not an app. The HVAC team tested it on iPads in the field. It was usable, but slow. They ended up using the desktop site on their phones and tolerating the pinch-zoom.

No automation beyond basic reminders. You can set a task to recur, but you can’t auto-create tasks based on pipeline stage changes. The marketing agency wanted to auto-assign leads to reps based on region. They had to do it manually.

No API for custom integrations. There’s a Zapier integration, but it’s limited to basic triggers: new contact, new task, pipeline stage change. The nonprofit wanted to sync donations from their payment processor. They had to export CSV files weekly and import them.

No reporting beyond basic lists. You can export contacts, tasks, and pipeline data to CSV. That’s it. No dashboards, no visual reports. The HVAC team built their own reports in Google Sheets using the CSV exports.

## What users complain about

G2 reviews as of June 2026 show a 4.3-star average from 120 reviews. The top complaints:

- No bulk email. You can send individual emails from the CRM, but no mass email campaigns. The marketing agency switched to Mailchimp for emails and used Zapier to log sent emails in the CRM.
- No calendar sync. You can’t sync tasks or appointments with Google Calendar or Outlook. The nonprofit team worked around this by using the CRM’s task list as their to-do list and ignoring their calendars.
- No custom fields beyond basic text and dropdowns. The HVAC team wanted to track equipment models and service dates. They ended up using the notes field and searching for keywords.

## Who it’s right for

Teams under 25 people who need a contact manager, not a sales machine. If you’re tracking leads, managing follow-ups, and keeping notes on customers, this works. If you need automation, reporting, or integrations, you’ll hit walls.
