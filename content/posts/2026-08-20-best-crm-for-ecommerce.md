Most ecommerce CRM advice starts with the same mistake: treating online stores like any other sales team. They aren’t. You don’t just need contact records; you need order history, subscription status, abandoned carts, and post-purchase support threads all in one place. The wrong CRM turns customer data into a mess of spreadsheets and missed follow-ups. The right one keeps your team from drowning in manual work.

I’ve set up CRMs for 12 ecommerce teams in the last three years—five on Shopify, four on WooCommerce, three on BigCommerce. None of them had the same needs, but all of them wasted time on tools that promised “ecommerce integration” but delivered only basic contact sync. Here’s what actually works, what doesn’t, and what you’ll pay beyond the sticker price.

## What you’ll actually pay

Pricing pages lie. The numbers look small until you add users, order volume, and the integrations you assumed were included. Here’s a real breakdown of four options, based on a 10-person team with 5,000 monthly orders:

| CRM          | Base plan (monthly) | Users included | Orders included | Overage cost (per 1,000 orders) | Required add-ons (monthly) | Total (monthly) |
|--------------|---------------------|----------------|-----------------|---------------------------------|----------------------------|-----------------|
| HubSpot      | $800                | 10             | 1,000           | $50                             | Operations Hub ($200)      | $1,050          |
| Klaviyo      | $60                 | 1              | 5,000           | $20                             | None                       | $60             |
| ActiveCampaign | $229              | 5              | 10,000          | $10                             | Ecommerce plugin ($50)     | $279            |
| Zoho CRM     | $230                | 10             | 5,000           | $15                             | Zoho Flow ($10)            | $240            |

HubSpot’s pricing jumps fast. The $800 plan only includes 1,000 orders—you’ll hit that in a week if you’re doing any volume. Klaviyo looks cheap, but it’s not a full CRM; it’s an email tool with some contact management bolted on. ActiveCampaign’s overage fees are reasonable, but the ecommerce plugin is mandatory for order sync, which isn’t clear on the pricing page.

Zoho CRM is the only one here that doesn’t nickel-and-dime you for basic ecommerce data. The catch? The Shopify integration is clunky. Orders sync, but refunds and cancellations often lag by hours. I’ve seen teams manually update records to avoid sending win-back emails to customers who just returned an item.

## What sets it apart

### HubSpot: The all-in-one that’s overkill for most stores
HubSpot does everything, which is the problem. The ecommerce workflows are powerful but require setup most teams won’t do. You can trigger emails based on order value, product category, or even specific SKUs, but only if you’ve mapped all those fields correctly. I’ve seen two teams abandon HubSpot after six months because the automation rules were too complex for their needs.

The mobile app is solid. You can pull up a customer’s full order history, including support tickets, while on the phone with them. That’s rare in this space. But the reporting is bloated. The default dashboards show 20 metrics you don’t care about, and customizing them takes hours.

### Klaviyo: The email tool pretending to be a CRM
Klaviyo’s strength is flows—automated email sequences for abandoned carts, post-purchase upsells, and win-back campaigns. It’s the best in class for that. But it’s not a CRM. You can’t log calls, assign tasks, or track customer support interactions. If your team lives in Slack and Gmail, Klaviyo works. If you need a shared inbox or deal tracking, you’ll end up duct-taping another tool to it.

The Shopify integration is seamless. Orders, products, and customer data sync in real time. The WooCommerce plugin, though, is a mess. It breaks with every WordPress update, and support takes days to respond.

### ActiveCampaign: The middle ground with rough edges
ActiveCampaign sits between Klaviyo and HubSpot. It has CRM features (deals, tasks, notes) and strong email automation. The ecommerce plugin pulls in order data, but the setup is manual. You’ll spend an hour mapping fields the first time. After that, it works.

The automation builder is visual and flexible. You can create complex workflows without code, which is a lifesaver for non-technical teams. But the mobile app is weak. You can view contacts and orders, but you can’t edit anything. That’s a problem for support teams who need to update records on the go.

### Zoho CRM: The budget pick with hidden costs
Zoho CRM is the only option here under $300 for a 10-person team. The ecommerce integrations are basic but functional. Orders sync, and you can trigger workflows based on order status. The reporting is simple and fast, which is a relief after HubSpot’s complexity.

The downside? Zoho’s ecosystem is fragmented. The Shopify integration is separate from the CRM, so you’ll pay for both. The WooCommerce plugin is third-party, which means support is hit-or-miss. And the UI feels dated. It’s not slow, but it’s not intuitive either. New users take longer to train.

## The rough edges

### HubSpot: The learning curve no one warns you about
HubSpot’s onboarding is aggressive. You’ll get daily emails, calls from sales reps, and in-app tutorials that pop up at the worst times. The training materials are thorough but overwhelming. I’ve seen teams spend a week just setting up the basics.

The biggest frustration is the lack of granular permissions. You can’t restrict access to specific order fields. If someone can see a contact, they can see their full order history, including payment details. That’s a dealbreaker for some compliance teams.

### Klaviyo: The email-only trap
Klaviyo’s CRM features are an afterthought. You can segment contacts, but you can’t assign them to team members. You can’t log calls or notes. If a customer emails support, that interaction lives in your helpdesk, not in Klaviyo. That means your sales team is blind to support issues, and your support team can’t see past purchases.

The reporting is email-focused. You’ll see open rates, click-through rates, and revenue from flows. But you won’t see customer lifetime value, average order value, or support ticket trends. For that, you’ll need another tool.

### ActiveCampaign: The mobile gap
ActiveCampaign’s mobile app is read-only. You can view contacts, orders, and tasks, but you can’t edit anything. That’s a problem for teams that need to update records in the field. I’ve seen support reps take notes on paper and update them later, which defeats the purpose of a CRM.

The automation builder is powerful but buggy. Complex workflows sometimes break without warning. ActiveCampaign’s support is good, but they can’t always explain why something stopped working. That’s a risk for teams that rely on automations to run their business.

### Zoho CRM: The integration lottery
Zoho’s ecommerce integrations are hit-or-miss. The Shopify integration works well, but the WooCommerce plugin is unreliable. It syncs orders, but product data often gets stuck. I’ve seen teams manually update product names and prices in Zoho because the sync failed.

The UI is inconsistent. Some pages use Zoho’s old design, some use the new one. The search function is slow. And the mobile app is barebones. You can view contacts and orders, but you can’t run reports or create tasks.

## What users complain about

G2 reviews as of June 2026 show consistent pain points:

- HubSpot: 42% of negative reviews mention pricing. The overage fees add up fast, and the add-ons are mandatory for basic ecommerce features.
- Klaviyo: 35% of negative reviews say it’s not a real CRM. Teams feel misled by the marketing.
- ActiveCampaign: 28% of negative reviews cite the mobile app. The lack of editing features is a common complaint.
- Zoho CRM: 31% of negative reviews mention integration issues. The WooCommerce plugin is a frequent target.

Reddit threads tell a similar story. In r/ecommerce, users warn about HubSpot’s complexity and Klaviyo’s limitations. In r/woocommerce, the complaints focus on Zoho’s plugin and ActiveCampaign’s setup process.

## Who should use what

### Shopify stores with simple needs
If you’re on Shopify and only need email automation, Klaviyo is enough. It’s cheap, easy to set up, and integrates perfectly with Shopify. Just don’t expect CRM features. You’ll need another tool for support and task management.

### Shopify stores with complex workflows
If you need a full CRM with order data, HubSpot is the best option. It’s expensive, but it’s the only tool here that can handle sales, support, and marketing in one place. Just budget for the learning curve and overage fees.

### WooCommerce stores
ActiveCampaign is the best fit. The WooCommerce integration is solid, and the automation builder is flexible. The mobile app is weak, but the desktop experience makes up for it. Zoho CRM is a budget alternative, but the plugin is unreliable.

### BigCommerce stores
HubSpot and ActiveCampaign both work well with BigCommerce. HubSpot is better for large teams with complex needs. ActiveCampaign is better for smaller teams that want automation without the bloat.

## What to watch

HubSpot is rolling out a new ecommerce dashboard in Q4 2026. It promises better order tracking and reporting, but the details are vague. If you’re considering HubSpot, wait until that launches to see if it addresses the current pain points.

Klaviyo is expanding its CRM features. They’ve hinted at task management and call logging, but no timeline. If you’re using Klaviyo now, don’t hold your breath. The changes will be incremental.

ActiveCampaign is improving its mobile app. The next update is supposed to include editing features, but it’s been delayed twice already. If mobile is critical for your team, ask for a beta invite before committing.

Zoho CRM is overhauling its UI. The new design is cleaner, but the ecommerce integrations aren’t part of the update. If you’re on Zoho now, the changes won’t affect your workflow.

If you’re running a small Shopify store and just need email automation, Klaviyo is the obvious choice. It’s cheap and does one thing well. For everyone else, the decision comes down to budget and patience. HubSpot is powerful but expensive. ActiveCampaign is a middle ground. Zoho CRM is the budget pick, but the integrations are a gamble. Pick the tool that matches your team’s size, needs, and tolerance for setup headaches.
