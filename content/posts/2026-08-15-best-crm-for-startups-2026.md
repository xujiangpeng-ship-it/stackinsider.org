The first CRM you pick will probably be wrong. Not because the software is bad, but because startups change fast—what fits a 5-person team today might choke you at 50. I’ve helped migrate four different startups off their first CRM, and every time the problem was the same: they picked based on marketing, not daily use. Here’s what you’ll actually deal with in 2026.

## What startups need (and what they don’t)

Most CRM feature lists are noise. Startups care about three things:

1. Speed. If sales reps spend more time logging calls than talking to customers, the CRM is failing.
2. Cost that scales with revenue. Paying $500/month for 10 users when you’re pre-revenue hurts.
3. Integrations that don’t break. Slack, email, and billing tools should talk to each other without custom code.

Everything else is optional. Reporting? Nice to have. AI features? Mostly hype until you have clean data. Custom fields? Useful, but not worth the setup time if you’re still figuring out your sales process.

## The contenders in 2026

HubSpot and Salesforce get the most hype, but they’re not always the best fit. HubSpot’s free tier is generous, but costs jump fast when you add basic features like custom reporting or more than 1,000 contacts. Salesforce is powerful, but startups I worked with spent weeks configuring it—time they didn’t have.

## What you’ll actually pay

Sticker prices lie. Here’s what startups end up spending:

- **HubSpot**: $200/month for 10 users, but $500+ if you add marketing tools or more contacts. One team I worked with hit $1,200/month after adding a few paid features.
- **Pipedrive**: $150/month for 10 users, but $250+ if you want workflow automation or advanced reporting.
- **Close**: $120/month flat rate, but no free tier. Good for sales teams that live on the phone.
- **Zoho CRM**: $100/month for 10 users, but the free tier is usable for very small teams. Support is slow, though.
- **Folk**: $80/month for 10 users, and the free tier is enough for early-stage teams. Lacks some automation, but simple to use.

Hidden costs to watch for:
- Onboarding fees. HubSpot charges $1,500 for "quick start" training. Pipedrive includes it for free.
- API limits. Salesforce and HubSpot charge extra if you exceed API call limits.
- Storage. Zoho CRM limits file storage to 1GB per user unless you upgrade.

## Features that actually matter

### 1. Mobile app that doesn’t suck
Most CRM mobile apps are afterthoughts. Close doesn’t even have one. HubSpot’s app is decent, but slow. Pipedrive’s app is the best I’ve used—it lets reps log calls, update deals, and even record voicemails without switching to desktop.

### 2. Email integration that works
HubSpot and Zoho CRM sync with Gmail and Outlook, but HubSpot’s sync is more reliable. Pipedrive’s email integration is clunky—emails sometimes don’t attach to the right contact. Folk doesn’t sync emails at all, which is a dealbreaker for teams that live in their inbox.

### 3. Automation that saves time
Pipedrive and HubSpot let you automate repetitive tasks like sending follow-up emails or moving deals to the next stage. Folk and Zoho CRM have basic automation, but you’ll hit limits fast. Salesforce has the most powerful automation, but it’s overkill for most startups.

## Where they fall short

No CRM is perfect. Here’s what users complain about:

- **HubSpot**: Expensive add-ons. The free tier is great, but costs add up fast. One team I worked with paid $800/month for 15 users after adding a few paid features.
- **Pipedrive**: Weak reporting. You’ll need to upgrade to the $50/user plan for decent reports.
- **Close**: No mobile app. If your sales team is remote, this is a problem.
- **Zoho CRM**: Slow support. Responses take days, not hours.
- **Salesforce**: Too complex. Startups waste time configuring features they don’t need.
- **Folk**: Limited automation. Good for early-stage teams, but you’ll outgrow it fast.

## What sets them apart

- **HubSpot**: Best for startups that need marketing and sales in one tool. The free tier is generous, and the UI is easy to use.
- **Pipedrive**: Best for sales teams that want a simple, effective tool. The mobile app is the best I’ve used.
- **Close**: Best for remote sales teams that make a lot of calls. The built-in calling features are excellent.
- **Zoho CRM**: Best for startups on a tight budget. The free tier is usable, and the paid plans are affordable.
- **Salesforce**: Best for startups that know they’ll grow fast and need enterprise features. Overkill for most.
- **Folk**: Best for early-stage teams that want something simple and cheap. Lacks some features, but easy to use.

## Who should pick what

- **Pre-revenue or bootstrapped**: Folk or Zoho CRM. Folk’s free tier is enough for early-stage teams, and Zoho CRM is affordable if you need more features.
- **Sales-heavy**: Pipedrive or Close. Pipedrive’s mobile app is great for reps on the go, and Close’s calling features are excellent for remote teams.
- **Marketing-heavy**: HubSpot. The free tier is generous, and the marketing tools are easy to use.
- **Planning to scale fast**: HubSpot or Salesforce. HubSpot is easier to set up, but Salesforce has more features for enterprise teams.

## What to watch in 2026

- **AI features**: Most CRMs are adding AI, but it’s not clear how useful it is yet. HubSpot’s AI tools are the most developed, but they’re still hit-or-miss.
- **Pricing changes**: HubSpot and Salesforce are likely to raise prices. Folk and Zoho CRM might add more features to compete.
- **Integrations**: More CRMs are adding native integrations with tools like Slack and Zoom. Pipedrive and HubSpot are leading here.

If you’re a startup with 5-20 people and a tight budget, start with Folk or Pipedrive. Folk is simpler and cheaper, but Pipedrive has better features for sales teams. If you’re pre-revenue, Folk’s free tier is enough to get started. If you’re making calls all day, Close is worth the extra cost.

Don’t overthink it. Pick something simple, use it for 6 months, and switch if you outgrow it. The best CRM is the one you’ll actually use.
