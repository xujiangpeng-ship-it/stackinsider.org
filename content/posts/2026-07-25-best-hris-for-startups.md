Startups don’t need HR software that looks good in a demo. They need something that doesn’t break when the first hire quits, the payroll runs late, or the CEO realizes they forgot to track PTO. Most HRIS tools are built for companies with dedicated HR teams, not founders who also handle onboarding between investor calls. Here’s what actually works when you’re moving fast and can’t afford surprises.

## What startups actually need from an HRIS

Most startups hit the same pain points in the same order.

First, you need to pay people without spending three hours in a spreadsheet. Then you realize you forgot to collect I-9s, and suddenly you’re printing forms at midnight. By the time you hit 20 employees, someone asks about benefits, and you realize your free tool doesn’t integrate with your payroll provider. The cycle repeats until you either switch tools or hire an HR person just to manage the software.

A good HRIS for startups should:
- Run payroll without requiring a dedicated ops person.
- Handle onboarding documents automatically (no more chasing signatures).
- Track time off without making employees email the CEO.
- Scale to at least 50 employees before you outgrow it.
- Not charge extra for basic compliance features.

Everything else is optional until you hit product-market fit.

## The real contenders (and who they’re for)

Here’s how the most common options stack up for startups under 100 employees. Pricing is based on a 25-person team as of July 2026.

| Tool          | Starting price (monthly) | Payroll included? | Onboarding docs | Time-off tracking | Best for                     | Biggest limitation                     |
|---------------|--------------------------|-------------------|-----------------|-------------------|------------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| Gusto         | $60 + $6/person          | Yes               | Yes             | Yes               | Founders who want one tool   | No advanced reporting                  |
| Rippling      | $8/person                | Yes (add-on)      | Yes             | Yes               | Tech startups with IT needs  | Overkill for non-tech teams            |
| BambooHR      | $6/person                | No                | Yes             | Yes               | Startups with HR staff       | Payroll costs extra                    |
| Deel          | $59 + $12/person         | Yes (global)      | Yes             | Yes               | Remote-first teams           | Weak for US-only companies             |
| Justworks     | $59 + $59/person         | Yes               | Yes             | Yes               | Startups with benefits needs | Expensive for lean teams               |

Gusto is the default choice for most startups because it handles payroll, onboarding, and time off in one place. The interface is simple enough that a founder can run payroll without training. The mobile app lets employees request time off without Slacking the CEO. At 25 people, you’ll pay around $210/month, which is cheap compared to hiring an HR person.

Rippling is popular with tech startups because it combines HR, IT, and payroll. If you’re issuing laptops and managing software logins, it saves time. But the pricing is confusing—payroll is an add-on, and the base product feels bloated if you don’t need IT features. I’ve seen teams drop it after realizing they were paying for functionality they didn’t use.

BambooHR is clean and easy to use, but it doesn’t include payroll. You’ll need to integrate it with another tool, which adds complexity. It’s a good choice if you have an HR person who wants more control, but founders often find it frustrating to manage two separate systems.

Deel is built for remote teams with global contractors. If you’re hiring outside the US, it’s one of the few tools that handles compliance and payments in multiple countries. But if you’re US-only, the pricing is higher than Gusto for similar features. The reporting is also weaker than competitors.

Justworks is a full-service PEO, which means it handles benefits and compliance for you. It’s expensive—expect to pay around $1,500/month for 25 people—but it’s worth it if you want to offer health insurance without hiring an HR team. The downside is that you’re locked into their benefits providers, which may not be the best fit for your team.

## What you’ll actually pay

Most HRIS tools advertise low starting prices, but the real cost includes add-ons, per-employee fees, and unexpected charges.

Gusto’s base plan is $60/month, but you’ll pay $6 per employee. At 25 people, that’s $210/month. If you add 401(k) or health insurance, the price jumps to $1,200+/month. They also charge $6/month per contractor, which adds up if you’re hiring freelancers.

Rippling’s pricing starts at $8/person, but payroll is an extra $8/person. If you want IT features, that’s another $8/person. A 25-person team with payroll and IT would pay around $600/month. The pricing is modular, so you can start small, but costs add up quickly.

BambooHR charges $6/person for the base plan, but payroll is a separate tool. If you integrate with Gusto for payroll, you’ll pay for both. At 25 people, that’s around $300/month for BambooHR plus $210/month for Gusto—totaling $510/month.

Deel’s pricing is $59/month plus $12/person. For 25 people, that’s $359/month. If you’re hiring globally, this is competitive, but for US-only teams, it’s more expensive than Gusto.

Justworks is the most expensive option. The base plan is $59/person, so 25 people cost $1,475/month. But this includes benefits and compliance, so it’s a different category than the others.

Hidden costs to watch for:
- Setup fees (some tools charge $500+ to migrate data).
- Per-payroll fees (Gusto charges $6 per contractor per month).
- Yearly contracts (some tools lock you in for 12 months).
- Support fees (Rippling charges extra for phone support).

## Where each tool falls short

No HRIS is perfect. Here’s what users complain about most.

Gusto’s reporting is weak. If you need custom reports for investors or board meetings, you’ll end up exporting data to Excel. The mobile app also lacks some features, like approving time-off requests.

Rippling’s onboarding is confusing. The product does a lot, but the interface feels cluttered. I’ve seen teams spend hours setting up workflows that should take minutes. The IT features are powerful, but if you don’t need them, they’re just noise.

BambooHR’s biggest issue is the lack of payroll. You’ll need to integrate with another tool, which means managing two logins and two sets of data. The time-off tracking is also less flexible than Gusto’s.

Deel’s US payroll features are limited. If you’re hiring globally, it’s great, but for US-only teams, it’s missing some compliance features. The customer support is also slower than competitors.

Justworks is expensive. The pricing is simple, but it’s hard to justify for lean startups. The benefits options are also limited—you’re stuck with their providers, which may not be the best fit for your team.

## What to do next

If you’re under 20 employees and just need payroll, onboarding, and time off, Gusto is the safest choice. It’s simple, affordable, and scales to around 50 people before you’ll need something more robust.

If you’re hiring globally or need IT features, Rippling is worth the extra cost. Just be prepared for a steeper learning curve.

If you have an HR person and want more control, BambooHR is a good option, but you’ll need to integrate it with a payroll tool.

If you’re remote-first and hiring outside the US, Deel is the best fit, but it’s overkill for US-only teams.

If you want to offer benefits without hiring an HR team, Justworks is the way to go, but it’s expensive for lean startups.

The biggest mistake I see is waiting too long to switch. Most startups outgrow their first HRIS within 18 months. Pick something that works now but can scale to at least 50 employees before you need to migrate again.
