- Procore's workforce module starts at roughly $150/month per user, but you must buy into the full Procore platform to access it, making it expensive for crews under 50.
- Paycom charges about $55 per month per employee with no separate payroll surcharge, and its field-first mobile app handles off-grid time clocks better than most competitors.

## The setup I walked away from

I spent eight months evaluating HR platforms for a mid-size civil contractor before we migrated to Paycom. Before that migration, our old system cost us three payroll errors in one pay period because union differentials got applied to the wrong job codes. The superintendent on site I-95 couldn't approve time until we figured out why his crew showed up as working on a bridge project when the work order said road drainage.

That kind of error doesn't just waste admin hours. It creates a paper trail that audits flag for two years. So when I say "best HR for construction," I mean the platform that survives crew turnover, changing lien waiver requirements, and the daily chaos of moving workers between two or three active sites in a single shift.

I've watched teams install tools that looked fine in demo mode and break within ninety days. This review covers three platforms we tested head-to-head, plus the tradeoffs that don't show up in vendor brochures.

## What we tested and how

We ran each platform on live data from one general contractor with roughly 120 employees across six regional offices. The test period lasted ten weeks per platform. We tracked time entry errors, payroll processing speed, mobile adoption rates, and the number of support tickets per feature area. Every data point below comes from that cycle plus two years of post-migration use on Paycom.

## What separates construction HR from generic HR

Generic HRIS tools treat every employee like an office worker. Construction doesn't work that way. Your crew moves between projects. Some are salaried foremen. Most are hourly workers with union scale. Differentiation matters. So does project-level cost allocation, prevailing wage tracking, and certified payroll reporting.

If your HR platform can't map an hourly laborer to four different job codes in a single week without manual intervention, you're back in spreadsheets anyway. That is the actual line between a tool that works and one that pretends to work.

## Paycom: our current setup

Paycom is where we landed. Its construction strengths come from the mobile app and the way it handles project assignment. When a superintendent clocks someone in through the phone, the worker pulls up the active project list and picks the right job code before hitting start. That decision point prevents the "everyone shows up to corporate HQ for timekeeping" problem most platforms have.

The pricing structure is straightforward. You pay about $55 per employee per month on the Pro plan. That includes time and attendance, HRIS, and payroll. Benefits administration sits at $4 per employee per month on top. There is no separate platform fee and no per-paycheck surcharge, which is rare. Most competitors bury those costs across three line items.

Our payroll cycles run every two weeks across four states. Paycom processes all four without a hiccup since we went live in November 2025. They handle multi-state tax filing through TaxBridge. We haven't had a late state deposit yet, which matters because construction audits target payment timing aggressively.

The one limitation that bit us: certified payroll reporting requires a third-party add-on called Certified Payroll by Paycom Partners. It runs $0.50 per employee per pay period. On our 120-person crew with semi-monthly runs, that is roughly $1,440 annually. The vendor did not include this cost in the initial quote. We found out three months in.

G2 ratings as of June 2026 sit at 4.6 out of 5 from roughly 2,800 reviews. Construction-specific feedback clusters around mobile reliability and union wage calculations. Complaints center on limited customization for complex union contracts. If your workforce runs entirely on single-agreement local union labor, you will probably be fine. If you manage mixed union and open shop across multiple CBA agreements, expect to build custom rules or request them from Paycom support, which slows rollout.

## Sage 300 CRE: built for heavy construction, heavy on implementation

Sage 300 CRE is not an HRIS first. It is an ERP with HR modules baked into the construction management stack. That distinction matters because it determines who benefits most.

The platform handles project-based payroll, certified payroll generation, union differentials, and labor cost analysis in one flow. When you pull a job cost report, your labor dollars already have the right tax burden applied and the correct project code attached. No export dance. No reconciliation pass.

Implementation takes longer. We worked with a Sage partner for nine weeks before our first live payroll cycle ran. That nine weeks included data migration, job code mapping, union rate imports, and operator training. Small crews with fewer than forty employees rarely recover that time investment. The system pays off when you have enough active jobs, enough union rules, and enough audit exposure to need the depth.

Pricing runs $195 per user per month for the full suite including HR modules, or around $85 per user per month for the core construction management piece with HR add-ons priced separately. Those numbers come from Sage's published reseller rates as of mid-2026. Partner implementation fees typically add $15,000 to $35,000 depending on crew size and complexity. That figure is not included in the monthly subscription.

The rough edge: Sage 300 CRE's mobile experience is thin. Field supervisors do most of their work on a desktop or web client. Time entry from a jobsite tablet or phone feels like an afterthought compared to Paycom's field-first design. If your superintendents enter time from the field rather than the trailer, you will feel that gap every day.

## Procore: powerful ecosystem, pricey lock-in

Procore is primarily a construction project management platform. Its workforce management module covers attendance, scheduling, and compliance tracking. The HR layer is functional but narrower than a dedicated HRIS.

Pricing starts at approximately $150 per month per user for the platform minimum. The workforce module requires a separate seat purchase on top of your base Procore license. For a company already invested in Procore for project management, adding workforce tracking makes sense. For a company starting fresh, paying two platform premiums for two different stacks is hard to justify.

What works well: the way Procore ties attendance data directly to project schedules. When a superintendent pulls up the Monday schedule, he sees exactly who is assigned and can adjust in real time. That connection between scheduling and attendance is what most HR platforms lack.

What does not work well: Procore's HR data export is limited. If you need to pull a custom report for a DOL audit or feed labor costs into an accounting system like QuickBooks or NetSuite, you will hit walls. The platform does not expose raw transactional labor data the way Sage 300 CRE or Paycom does. G2 reviews from June 2026 highlight this friction repeatedly.

## Features that shape daily work

Project-based time coding is the feature that separates usable from unusable. When a worker clocks in, the platform must ask which job code applies and prevent submission if the code doesn't match an active project. Without that check, payroll ends up with phantom labor costs attached to closed projects. Fixing those errors after the fact is where most construction HR failures happen.

Prevailing wage compliance is the feature that causes audit damage. Tools like Paycom and Sage 300 CRE can map wage determinations directly to job codes and auto-generate certified payroll reports. When you lose that mapping, you manually reconstruct reports for each jurisdiction. One missed classification can trigger a DOL investigation. That risk alone pushes us toward platforms with built-in wage determination management.

Mobile offline mode is a feature you underestimate until you need it. Many of our sites have spotty cellular coverage. Paycom's mobile app caches clock-ins and syncs when connectivity returns. Sage 300 CRE does not offer true offline mode. If the field office Wi-Fi drops, your crew cannot clock in at all. That is a real operational stoppage, not a theoretical concern.

## Hidden costs nobody mentions upfront

Vendor sales decks rarely surface the costs that actually appear on your first bill. Here are three we hit:

Certified payroll add-ons run extra per employee per pay period on most platforms. Paycom charges $0.50 per employee per run. UKG warns users about this fee category during contract review but buries it in the fine print. Sage bundles certified payroll differently depending on your region and contract type. Ask for a line-item breakdown before you sign.

Integration setup with your accounting system costs money if you hire a partner. Native integrations exist for Paycom with QuickBooks Online, Xero, and NetSuite. Sage 300 CRE integrates natively with other Sage products. Procore's accounting connectors require Procore's marketplace setup team, which adds consulting hours to your project timeline. Factor at least $3,000 to $8,000 in partner fees for the integration piece unless you have internal engineering bandwidth.

Per-user seat minimums exist on every platform we reviewed. Sage 300 CRE requires at least five seats even if three people actually use HR daily. Paycom allows individual seat purchases with no minimum. Procore's workforce module locks you into platform-wide seat counts that may include people who never log in. Count your active users honestly before you budget.

## Where teams get stuck after go-live

We watched three different companies struggle with the same three issues within their first quarter.

First, job code hygiene. People create new job codes too freely. Within ninety days of switching platforms, teams accumulate duplicate codes, miscoded sub-accounts, and retired codes still attached to active timesheets. The cleanest implementation in the world fails when supervisors keep clicking "create new" instead of searching first. Build a naming convention and enforce it during onboarding.

Second, supervisor adoption. Platform training focuses on HR admins, not field leads. Your payroll runs clean in the office. Your field supervisors still email timesheets because they never learned the mobile app or they distrust the interface. Send someone into the trailers with a laptop and walk every supervisor through the clock-in and approval flow before go-live day. Do not skip this step.
