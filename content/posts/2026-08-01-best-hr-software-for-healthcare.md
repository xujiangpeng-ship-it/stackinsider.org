Healthcare HR isn’t just payroll and timecards. You track certifications that expire, schedule shifts that can’t drop below minimum coverage, and handle compliance paperwork that changes every year. Most HR tools treat these as add-ons. The ones built for healthcare usually charge extra for them.

I’ve helped three mid-sized clinics migrate off spreadsheets and two hospital systems consolidate vendors. Here’s what I’ve learned about what actually works—and what doesn’t.

## What healthcare teams need from HR software

Most HR platforms list the same features: onboarding, time tracking, benefits. Healthcare adds three layers:

1. **Credential tracking**: Licenses, certifications, and competencies with expiration dates. You need alerts at 90, 60, and 30 days, plus a way to block shifts if credentials lapse.
2. **Shift compliance**: Overtime rules that differ by state, mandatory rest periods between shifts, and union-specific seniority rules. A generic time-tracking tool won’t flag these.
3. **Documentation for audits**: OSHA logs, HIPAA training records, and incident reports that need to be retrievable in 24 hours. If the software can’t pull these in a single export, you’ll scramble during an audit.

Generic HR tools often bolt these on as “healthcare modules” with separate pricing. Healthcare-specific tools include them but assume every clinic runs the same way—which they don’t.

### BambooHR: Simple but limited for shift work

BambooHR is clean and easy to use. The credential tracking works well—it sends alerts and blocks access if certifications expire. But it doesn’t handle shift compliance. If your state has daily overtime rules or mandatory rest periods, you’ll need to manually check schedules or export data to Excel.

The mobile app is solid for clocking in and out, but managers can’t approve timecards from it. For a small clinic with stable shifts, this might not matter. For a hospital with rotating schedules, it’s a dealbreaker.

### UKG Ready: Built for healthcare but expensive

UKG Ready includes credential tracking, shift compliance, and audit exports out of the box. It handles union rules and state-specific overtime without extra configuration. The scheduling tool lets you set minimum coverage requirements and flags violations before you publish shifts.

The downside is cost. At $12 per employee, it’s the most expensive option here. The interface feels dated, and the reporting tool requires training. I’ve seen teams spend weeks building custom reports for payroll and compliance.

### Paycor: Middle ground with some rough edges

Paycor sits between BambooHR and UKG. It includes credential tracking and shift compliance without add-on fees. The scheduling tool is simpler than UKG’s but covers the basics: minimum coverage, rest periods, and overtime rules.

The mobile app is better than UKG’s but still clunky. Approving timecards takes too many taps. The reporting is more flexible than BambooHR’s but not as powerful as UKG’s. For a mid-sized clinic, this is often the best balance of cost and features.

### ADP Workforce Now: Enterprise features with enterprise complexity

ADP handles credential tracking and some shift compliance, but the rules engine isn’t as flexible as UKG’s. You can set up alerts for certifications and block shifts, but overtime and rest period rules require custom configuration.

The audit exports are thorough, but the interface is overwhelming. ADP assumes you have a dedicated HR admin. For a small clinic, this is overkill. For a hospital system with multiple locations, it’s worth the learning curve.

## What you’ll actually pay

List prices are just the start. Here’s what adds up:

- **Implementation fees**: UKG and ADP charge $5,000–$15,000 for setup. Paycor and BambooHR include it in the first year.
- **Training**: UKG and ADP require paid training sessions ($1,500–$3,000). Paycor and BambooHR offer free webinars.
- **Support tiers**: BambooHR and Paycor include phone support. UKG and ADP charge extra for it ($1,000–$2,000/year).
- **Custom reports**: UKG and ADP charge $200–$500 per custom report. Paycor and BambooHR include a set number.

For a 100-employee clinic, first-year costs look like this:

- BambooHR: $9,228 (base) + $2,400 (healthcare add-on) = $11,628
- UKG Ready: $14,400 (base) + $10,000 (implementation) + $2,000 (training) = $26,400
- Paycor: $9,600 (base) + $0 (healthcare included) = $9,600
- ADP: $12,000 (base) + $3,600 (healthcare add-on) + $10,000 (implementation) = $25,600

These are rough estimates. Actual quotes vary based on contract length and negotiations.

## What sets healthcare HR software apart

The tools that work for healthcare share three traits:

1. **Credential tracking that blocks access**: If a certification expires, the system should prevent clock-ins until it’s renewed. BambooHR and UKG do this well. Paycor does it but with a 24-hour delay.
2. **Shift rules that match real workflows**: Minimum coverage, rest periods, and overtime rules should be configurable per location. UKG and Paycor handle this. BambooHR doesn’t.
3. **Audit exports that don’t require IT**: You should be able to pull OSHA logs, HIPAA training records, and incident reports in one click. UKG and ADP do this. Paycor requires a custom report.

## Who each tool is for

- **BambooHR**: Small clinics (10–50 employees) with simple shift patterns and no union rules.
- **UKG Ready**: Hospitals (200+ employees) with complex scheduling and compliance needs.
- **Paycor**: Mid-sized clinics (50–200 employees) that need shift compliance without enterprise complexity.
- **ADP**: Large hospital systems (500+ employees) with dedicated HR teams.

## One thing most reviews don’t mention

Most healthcare HR tools assume you’ll use their scheduling module. But many clinics and hospitals already have a scheduling tool they like. Integrating a new HR system with an existing scheduler is painful.

UKG and ADP have APIs, but they’re not plug-and-play. You’ll need a developer or a third-party integration tool like Zapier. Paycor and BambooHR don’t integrate with external schedulers at all. If you’re not ready to replace your scheduler, factor in the cost of custom integration or manual data entry.

## What to watch for in the next year

UKG is rolling out AI-powered shift recommendations. Early tests show it can reduce overtime by 10–15% by suggesting swaps between employees. Paycor is adding a credential marketplace where employees can sign up for renewal courses directly in the app. Neither is a must-have yet, but both could save time if they work as promised.

## The bottom line

If you’re a small clinic with simple needs, BambooHR is the easiest to use. If you’re a hospital with complex scheduling, UKG Ready is the most powerful. For everything in between, Paycor is the best balance of cost and features.

ADP is only worth the complexity if you’re a large system with dedicated HR staff. For everyone else, it’s overkill.

Pick the tool that matches your size and workflows today. You can always migrate later if you outgrow it. Just don’t assume the “healthcare” label means it will handle your specific compliance rules out of the box. Always test the credential tracking and shift compliance with your real data before signing a contract.
