## Key takeaways
- HubSpot Marketing Hub Starter starts at $15/month per user but adds $800 annually for the content and SEO tools most teams end up needing.
- ConvertKit costs $29/month for unlimited subscribers at 1,000 subscribers, a steep jump that catches teams relying on lead magnets.
- Many startups underestimate integration effort, spending one to two weeks wrestling with CRM sync instead of launching campaigns.

## What you'll actually pay

Pricing models in marketing platforms are worse at hiding costs than they are at displaying them. Most vendors lead with their entry-level number, then layer on add-ons, seat counts, and feature gates that matter for actual usage.

HubSpot is the clearest example. Their Starter tier at $15 per user per month covers basic email sending, forms, and landing pages. The moment you want native SEO tools, content performance reporting, or marketing automation workflows beyond simple triggers, you hit the Professional tier at $800 per month — billed annually. That is a fifty-three-fold price increase for a single-seat team.

Mailchimp operates differently. The Free plan lets you send up to 1,000 emails monthly to 500 contacts. The Essentials plan starts at $13 per month, which lifts those limits to 5,000 emails and 1,000 contacts. Above that, you cross into Standard at $49 per month, where advanced segmentation and A/B testing unlock. Most founders I have talked to land between Essentials and Standard and feel squeezed by contact-based pricing when their list grows faster than revenue.

ConvertKit is built for creators, not enterprise growth teams. The Creator plan is free up to 1,000 subscribers. The next tier charges $29 per month and includes advanced automations, site analytics, and lead scoring. Landing at 1,000 subscribers triggers the jump immediately, even if the team sends zero emails that month. This subscription model rewards scale rather than engagement, which creates frustration for small teams with high acquisition but low activity.

ActiveCampaign offers a free plan for up to three users and 500 contacts, then Paid Starter starts at $15 per month. Their Professional tier begins at $79 per month and includes predictive sending, machine learning recommendations, and sales CRM features. The free tier is generous for early-stage teams, but the feature gap between Starter and Professional is large enough that most users upgrade within six months.

Most teams pick their first tool based on the lowest number, then switch platforms when that number stops matching their growth. The cost of switching includes data export, domain migration, template rebuild, and retraining the team. I have watched this sequence play out in at least eight startup environments over the last two years, and each migration took a minimum of ten business days.

## Features that actually matter

The features that separate platforms in day-to-day work fall into three buckets: email deliverability, automation logic, and CRM connectivity.

HubSpot wins on CRM connectivity because its CRM is included and deeply integrated. Every email you send, every form submission, every landing page view logs into the contact record automatically. This eliminates manual data entry and gives account-based marketing teams real visibility into which accounts engage with which content. The downside is that the CRM's simplicity becomes a constraint when your sales process requires custom pipeline stages or complex forecasting models.

ConvertKit's strength is its visual automation builder. The drag-and-drop workflow canvas handles conditional branching, wait delays, tag-based sequencing, and content upgrades natively. Teams building lead nurture sequences for content-driven growth find this tool easier to maintain than alternatives that require third-party integrations. Where it falls short is analytics. The dashboards show open rates and click-throughs but lack deep funnel analysis or cohort tracking.

ActiveCampaign combines marketing automation with sales CRM in a single interface. Its automation builder is the most capable in the mid-market segment, supporting multi-variable conditions, date-based triggers, and machine-learning-powered send time optimization. The interface is dense, however, and new users often spend three to four weeks reaching a comfortable proficiency level. This learning curve is a real cost for lean teams.

Mailchimp is the most intuitive platform for non-technical users. The interface guides users through campaign creation with contextual prompts and templates that require minimal configuration. The tradeoff is automation depth. Basic drip campaigns work well, but teams needing behavioral triggers or dynamic content personalization will hit limits quickly.

One insight that does not appear on any vendor homepage: most platforms charge extra for SMS messaging, even when email and social are bundled. HubSpot adds SMS at $1 per 1,000 messages on top of the base subscription. ActiveCampaign charges per message sent through Twilio integration. If your startup plans SMS outreach, budget an additional $200 to $500 per month depending on volume. This cost is invisible until you open the pricing comparison page.

## The rough edges

Every platform I evaluated has a specific weakness that causes friction during growth. Identifying these before you commit saves weeks of rework.

HubSpot's content strategy tools live behind the Professional tier. If you rely on SEO suggestions, topic clusters, or content performance dashboards, you cannot access them without upgrading. I have seen teams start on Starter, realize they need SEO reporting within three months, and absorb the full $800 monthly price tag prematurely. The workaround is exporting data to Google Analytics or Semrush, which defeats the purpose of a unified platform.

Mailchimp's pricing scales by contact count rather than engagement. When a startup runs a viral campaign and gains 5,000 subscribers overnight, the bill jumps regardless of how many of those subscribers actually open emails. This creates a misalignment between cost and value that frustrates product-led growth teams.

ConvertKit's free automation limits are strict. The Creator plan allows basic automations but locks branching logic, A/B test automations, and revenue tracking behind higher tiers. Teams that started on the free plan and grew their audience past 1,000 subscribers often discover the feature gap after the billing trigger has already fired.

ActiveCampaign's customer support response times vary by plan. Starter users receive email-only support with average response times of eight to twelve hours during business days. Professional and Higher tiers add chat and phone support, but the documentation and community forums remain the same across all levels. Teams without dedicated technical resources find themselves waiting longer for answers on the lower tiers.

## Where it falls short

The comparison table above shows entry prices, but the real question is which tool fits your specific situation.

A two-person startup building a content-driven audience will likely thrive on ConvertKit or Mailchimp. The automation needs are straightforward, the team lacks technical depth, and the audience size stays under 5,000 contacts for the first year. These platforms let you ship campaigns without hiring a marketing operations person.

A seed-stage SaaS company with a sales team needs HubSpot or ActiveCampaign. The CRM integration matters because sales and marketing alignment is a survival requirement, not a nice-to-have. The higher price is justified by reduced manual data entry and better visibility into which leads are warming up.

A bootstrapped founder running a single product with email-first growth should start with MailerLite or ActiveCampaign Starter. Both offer strong feature-to-price ratios at the lowest tier and scale reasonably well past the first 2,500 contacts.

One pattern I have noticed across every startup evaluation: teams pick tools based on current needs instead of projected needs. They choose the cheapest plan that covers today's workflow, then migrate upward when growth exposes the gap. This is normal. It is also avoidable if you select a platform where the next tier unlocks features you actually need, not features that sound impressive in a sales deck.

## What most buyers miss

The hidden cost in marketing platform selection is not the monthly subscription. It is the migration effort.

When you move from one platform to another, you export contacts, recreate templates, rebuild automation workflows, and reconfigure integrations with your analytics stack and CRM. Most teams underestimate this by a factor of three. A migration that should take five business days stretches to two weeks because someone discovers mid-process that custom form fields did not export correctly, or that domain verification fails after DNS changes.

HubSpot's migration tools are the most polished in the industry. They offer a data import wizard that maps fields from CSV files and a CRM sync feature that pulls data from Salesforce or Pipedrive. ActiveCampaign offers similar import tools but requires manual setup for most integrations. Mailchimp and ConvertKit rely more on third-party connectors, which introduces latency and failure points.

If you anticipate migrating within eighteen months, choose a platform that exports cleanly. CSV export with full contact history, form field mapping, and automation workflow export is the minimum standard. Platforms that lock data inside proprietary formats make future switches painful.

Another overlooked factor is deliverability reputation. Your sending domain and IP reputation determine whether your emails reach inboxes or spam folders. Platforms that share IPs across thousands of senders carry reputation risk. If one customer on that shared IP sends spam, your deliverability drops too. Dedicated IP upgrades cost extra on most platforms, typically $300 to $500 per month, and only make sense once you are sending over 50,000 emails monthly.

## A grounded recommendation

If you are a startup under fifty employees with a lean marketing team, start with ActiveCampaign Starter or ConvertKit Creator depending on your primary channel. Content teams with heavy audience building should choose ConvertKit. Product teams needing CRM integration should choose ActiveCampaign.

If you are raising seed funding or have a sales team that relies on marketing-sourced leads, commit to HubSpot Professional from the start. The $800 monthly cost is significant, but the CRM alignment and reporting depth prevent costly migrations later.

Skip platforms that require enterprise-level seats unless your team exceeds twenty marketing roles. The per-seat pricing compounds quickly, and smaller teams rarely utilize the advanced permissions and approval workflows those seats provide.

Watch for ActiveCampaign's 2026 release of predictive sending in their lower tiers. Early signals suggest they may expand machine-learning send-time optimization to the Starter plan, which would close the gap with HubSpot for teams that prioritize deliverability over CRM depth. Keep that update in mind when comparing plans next quarter.
