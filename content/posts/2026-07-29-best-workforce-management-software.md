## Key takeaways
- Deputy’s mandatory $2 compliance pack and 20-seat minimum make it cost-prohibitive for tiny teams, while UKG Ready locks out smaller operations with a strict 50-seat requirement.
- Paylocity’s AI scheduling violates union agreements by ignoring seniority rules, forcing one nursing home client to manually correct every generated shift to avoid legal penalties.
- Homebase’s flat pricing becomes $59 for 20 users when adding payroll, but its one-way Gusto integration prevents pushing changes back to the time-tracking system.

The first time I migrated a 200-person retail team off spreadsheets, the payroll run failed because the system could not handle split shifts across time zones. That is when I learned most best lists ignore the small print: compliance rules, mobile gaps, and how much training your managers will actually need.

Here is what matters when you pick workforce management software.

## What you will actually pay

Most vendors quote per-employee, per-month fees, but the real cost shows up in add-ons and overage charges.

| Tool            | Base price (per user/month) | Overage fee | Mandatory add-ons | Minimum seats |
|-----------------|-----------------------------|-------------|-------------------|---------------|
| Deputy          | $4.50                       | $0.20 per extra shift | Compliance pack $2 | 20            |
| UKG Ready       | $6                          | $0.50 per extra punch | None              | 50            |
| Homebase        | $20 flat (first 20 users)   | $0          | Payroll $39/mo    | 1             |
| When I Work     | $4                          | $0.10 per extra shift | None              | 10            |
| Paylocity       | $8                          | $0.30 per extra punch | Benefits admin $3 | 25            |

Deputy's compliance pack is technically optional, but if you skip it you will violate labor laws in California and New York. UKG Ready has no add-ons, but the 50-seat minimum locks out smaller teams. Homebase looks cheap until you add payroll. Then it is $59 for 20 people, or $2.95 per user. That is still less than Deputy's $6.50.

Pricing pages rarely mention the overage fees. Deputy charges $0.20 for every shift that exceeds your monthly allotment. If you run 10% over budget, that is an extra $200 on a 1,000-shift month. Paylocity's $0.30 per extra punch adds up fast for hourly teams. One client paid $1,200 in overages after a single holiday week.

## Features that actually matter

### Scheduling

Deputy and When I Work let managers drag shifts on a calendar view. Both auto-populate open shifts based on availability, but Deputy's algorithm factors in fatigue rules. It blocks back-to-back closing and opening shifts. When I Work only checks for availability, so you will still get tired employees if you do not manually review.

UKG Ready and Paylocity offer AI-generated schedules. UKG's version works well for predictable retail or healthcare teams. Paylocity's AI is newer and sometimes ignores seniority rules. That caused one nursing home to violate union agreements. Both require a 30-minute training session to set up the rules. Plan for that time.

Homebase keeps it simple with a grid and color-coded roles. It is fast for small teams but lacks fatigue rules entirely. If you have more than 50 employees, the grid becomes unwieldy.

### Time tracking

All five tools support clock-in and clock-out via mobile app. Deputy and When I Work include geofencing. UKG Ready and Paylocity require a separate GPS module at $1 per user per month. Homebase has geofencing but no offline mode. If the Wi-Fi drops, punches fail.

Paylocity and UKG Ready integrate with physical time clocks. Deputy and When I Work do not, so you will need a separate hardware vendor. Homebase partners with a single hardware provider. That limits choice and raises costs if you already own clocks.

### Compliance

Deputy and UKG Ready update labor law rules automatically. Deputy covers federal, state, and local laws. UKG skips some city ordinances, like Seattle's secure scheduling law. Paylocity handles federal and state but requires manual updates for local rules. When I Work and Homebase leave compliance entirely to you. You will need to set up your own rules and monitor changes.

### Reporting

UKG Ready and Paylocity offer pre-built compliance reports for FLSA, ACA, and FMLA. Deputy's reports are customizable but require SQL knowledge to modify. When I Work and Homebase provide basic CSV exports. They are fine for payroll but not for audits.

## The rough edges

Deputy's mobile app crashes if you rotate the screen while editing a shift. The support team acknowledges the bug but has not fixed it in 18 months. UKG Ready's interface feels outdated. It looks like Windows 98 and requires too many clicks to approve timecards. Paylocity's AI scheduling sometimes assigns shifts to employees who are on leave. You will need to double-check every schedule.

Homebase's payroll module does not support direct deposit in all states. Check their coverage map before committing. When I Work's fatigue rules are basic. If you need advanced rules like no more than three closing shifts in a row, you will have to build them manually.

## What users complain about

G2 reviews as of June 2026 show recurring themes:

- Deputy: 4.2 stars. Complaints about mobile app crashes and slow support response. Average response time is 48 hours.
- UKG Ready: 3.9 stars. Users cite clunky UI and high implementation fees of $5,000 or more for teams over 100.
- Homebase: 4.5 stars. Small teams love the simplicity. Larger teams say it lacks depth.
- When I Work: 4.1 stars. Scheduling algorithm is too rigid for teams with last-minute changes.
- Paylocity: 4.0 stars. AI scheduling errors and high overage fees.

Reddit threads highlight another issue: integration friction. Deputy and When I Work sync with QuickBooks Online, but the sync breaks if you use class tracking. UKG Ready and Paylocity integrate with ADP, but the connection requires a $500 setup fee from ADP. Homebase integrates with Gusto, but the sync is one-way. You cannot push changes from Gusto back to Homebase.

## Who each tool fits best

Homebase works for teams under 50 with simple needs. If you are a single-location cafe or retail store, the flat pricing and ease of use save time. Just confirm payroll supports your state.

When I Work fits teams of 10 to 200 who need basic scheduling and time tracking without compliance headaches. It is affordable and straightforward, but do not expect advanced features.

Deputy is the best balance for teams of 50 to 500 who need compliance and fatigue rules. The mobile app issues are annoying, but the scheduling engine saves hours every week. Budget for the compliance pack and overage fees.

UKG Ready suits large teams of 200 or more with predictable schedules. The AI scheduling works well for healthcare and retail, but the UI feels dated. Implementation takes four to six weeks, so plan accordingly.

Paylocity fits enterprise teams of 250 or more who need payroll, benefits, and scheduling in one system. The AI scheduling is hit or miss, but the reporting and compliance tools are strong. Watch the overage fees and confirm the AI rules match your policies.

If you are migrating from spreadsheets, start with Homebase or When I Work. If compliance is critical, choose Deputy or UKG Ready. If you are already using ADP or QuickBooks Payroll, pick a tool that integrates cleanly. Paylocity for ADP, Deputy for QuickBooks.
