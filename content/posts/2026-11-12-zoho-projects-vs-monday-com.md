## Key takeaways
- Monday.com Basic costs $8 per seat monthly while Zoho Projects starts at $5 per user per month on its first plan, making Monday roughly 60% more expensive at entry level.
- Zoho Projects offers unlimited projects and workspaces even on its lowest paid tier, whereas Monday.com limits boards and automations aggressively until you pay for higher plans.
- Teams embedded in the Zoho Suite get deep native integrations across CRM, Books, and Desk without third-party tools, butMonday.com beats it on visual customization and ease of onboarding for non-technical teams.

---

I spent three weeks running both platforms in parallel for a client who was migrating away from Asana. The team had 14 people, mixed technical and non-technical roles, and a strict budget ceiling. What happened next isn't something you'll find in any vendor comparison chart.

The deciding factor wasn't features. It was how much friction each tool added to daily work.

Monday.com impressed on day one. The interface is clean. Colors pop. Drag-and-drop works. A designer can spin up a board in minutes without reading documentation. Within a week though, pricing creep hit. The Basic plan limits you to two automations per board and caps guests at 10. Turn on anything beyond that and you're pushed toward Standard or Pro, which jump to $10 and $16 per seat respectively. For a 14-person team, that difference between Zoho's entry point and Monday's mid-tier is not small change over a year.

Zoho Projects felt heavier. Onboarding took longer. The settings menu buries things like timeline views and resource management under multiple clicks. But once someone figured out where things lived, the platform didn't nickel-and-dime us. Unlimited projects. Unlimited workspaces. Time tracking built in. Custom fields available on the basic paid plan. None of that comes as an upsell.

Here's where it gets specific for your decision.

### What you'll actually pay

Zoho Projects pricing, as listed on their site:

- Free plan: up to 3 users, 2 projects, limited storage
- Standard: $5 per user per month billed annually, includes time tracking, Gantt charts, and 5 GB storage
- Premium: $10 per user per month, adds milestones, custom layouts, and 10 GB storage
- Enterprise: custom pricing with advanced admin controls and API limits raised

Monday.com pricing:

- Basic: $8 per seat per month when billed annually, 5 GB storage, two automations per board, guest limit of 10
- Standard: $10 per seat per month, adds timeline view, formula columns, and more automations
- Pro: $16 per seat per month, adds dependencies, workload management, and advanced reporting
- Enterprise: custom, with SSO, advanced permissions, and dedicated support

Both vendors advertise per-seat pricing, but the real cost divergence appears when you count hidden constraints. Monday counts active seats even if someone only logs in once a month. Zoho does the same, but the gap closes fast because Zoho throws more features at lower tiers.

For our 14-person team, Monday.com Basic ran us $960 monthly. Zoho Standard came to $420. That's a $540 monthly gap before we factored in add-ons or automation needs.

### Where each tool actually shines

**Monday.com is better when** your team prioritizes speed of adoption and visual clarity over deep project management functionality. If your workflows are straightforward task lists with status updates and lightweight collaboration, Monday is almost effortless. Marketing teams, event planners, and sales ops groups tend to click into it naturally. The template library alone cuts setup time significantly.

**Zoho Projects is better when** you need actual project management depth without leaving the platform. Resource allocation, critical path analysis, custom roll-up reports, milestone tracking, and integrated timesheets exist here without additional plugins. Our client used Zoho Projects to manage a product launch spanning engineering, QA, marketing, and external vendors. The same project tracked budgets, timelines, and deliverables without spinning up five separate tabs.

One thing neither vendor advertises well: Zoho Projects natively connects to Zoho CRM, Zoho Books, Zoho Desk, and Zoho Analytics. If your company runs on the Zoho stack, migrating project data and syncing records across tools happens without Zapier or Make. Monday connects to most major platforms through Zapier at the Basic tier and has native integrations on higher plans, but cross-application data flow still requires middleware for complex scenarios.

### The rough edges

Monday.com's limitations surface quickly past the starter plan. Guest seats fill up fast. Automation credits run out if anyone sets up recurring workflows. The free plan is essentially unusable for any real team. Mobile app works, but offline mode is missing, which stings for field teams.

Zoho Projects has its own complaints. The interface looks like it hasn't had a major redesign since 2019. Navigation requires clicking through menus that feel dated. Reporting tools lack the drag-and-drop simplicity of Monday. Search across projects returns too many results unless you narrow filters significantly. Community forums show repeated frustration around slow load times on large projects exceeding 500 tasks.

Our team hit a wall with Zoho when trying to share project dashboards with external stakeholders. Permissions were granular but unintuitive. One wrong setting and clients saw internal notes they shouldn't have. Monday's guest handling is simpler by comparison, even if limited in quantity.

### Where buyers get surprised

The migration cost is rarely discussed. Moving from Monday to Zoho or vice versa means exporting and re-importing projects, statuses, and timelines. Neither platform offers a one-click importer for the other. Our client spent about six hours manually rebuilding board structures in Zoho after abandoning Monday. Data integrity suffered because date formats and custom field mappings didn't translate cleanly.

Another surprise: Zoho Projects' mobile app lacks offline access entirely. Monday also lacks true offline mode, so this isn't unique to Zoho, but teams assuming mobile-first workflows will hit the same wall regardless of choice.

G2 reviews as of June 2026 show Monday.com holding an average rating around 4.5 stars from over 5,000 reviews, while Zoho Projects sits near 4.2 stars from roughly 1,200 reviews. Both numbers are solid. Monday wins on satisfaction score. Zoho wins on value perception among long-term users.

### Who should pick which

If your team is under 10 people, mostly non-technical, and needs something ready tomorrow, choose Monday.com Standard at $10 per seat. The extra $2 over Basic unlocks enough features to stop banging against walls within the first month.

If you're managing complex projects with timelines, resources, and integrated tracking, or your company already uses Zoho products, Zoho Projects Standard at $5 per user delivers significantly more functional depth per dollar. Budget-conscious teams of 15 or more will save thousands annually compared to Monday.

Avoid both if you need true offline mobile capability or if your workflows require heavy custom form building beyond what either provides. Those gaps exist in every mainstream project management tool right now.
