## Key takeaways
- Procore starts at $1,800/year per user on the Essentials plan, which prices out crews under 15 people unless they share one seat across roles.
- Jobber works best for trades doing residential or light commercial work where scheduling and invoicing live in the same tool, not for heavy civil projects.
- FieldWire lacks native accounting integration, so teams using it still pay $50-200/month extra for a separate books tool like QuickBooks or Xero.

I've watched three different general contractors waste six weeks each trying to force-field-task apps into becoming full project management systems. That's not a workflow problem. It's a tool-problem. The best workflow for construction isn't a single platform you install and forget. It's a stack where estimates, field tracking, and client billing actually talk to each other without someone manually copying numbers between screens.

Here is what I've learned from watching teams pick, implement, and sometimes abandon construction workflow software over the last few years.

## What you'll actually pay

Construction software pricing has shifted toward per-user tiers with feature gates that matter more than the sticker price. Procore Essentials runs about $1,800 per user annually, which sounds manageable until you need 20 field supervisors plus five office staff and realize you're already at $45,000 a year before add-ons. Procore also charges extra for document management beyond a certain storage threshold and for advanced financial features that sit behind the Project Management add-on.

Autodesk Build bundles into the Autodesk Construction Cloud at roughly $120 to $200 per user per month depending on whether you include Estimating and Safety modules. That bundle is denser out of the gate but locks you further into the Autodesk ecosystem, which matters if your subcontracts and suppliers already use Procore or PlanGrid.

Jobber charges $49 to $199 per month flat regardless of crew size on its core plans, which sounds cheap until you need multiple locations or want to add recurring maintenance scheduling at the $99 per month upgrade tier.

FieldWire offers a free tier for up to three projects and paid plans starting around $29 per user monthly, making it the lowest barrier entry point for small crews who mainly need punch lists and drawing markup.

## Features that actually matter on a jobsite

The feature most teams undervalue until they miss it is version-controlled drawing markups. When your superintendent circles a revised HVAC duct on a PDF and the electrician gets that updated sheet the same afternoon, rework drops fast. Procore and Autodesk Build both handle this natively. FieldWire does it well too, and it is cheaper for teams that do not need financial features baked in.

Photo documentation tied to a specific drawing sheet is another daily-use feature that separates tools worth keeping from tools gathering dust. The old way was texting photos to a group chat and hoping the right person saw them. Modern tools let you pin a photo directly to a floor plan, assign action items, and push notifications to the responsible subcontractor. I have seen framing crews catch a mislocated outlet box before drywall went up because the inspector's photo annotation sat on the same sheet the foreman pulled up on his phone.

RFI and submittal workflows are the features that either save or sink a project schedule. A tool that makes it easy to submit, route, track, and close these items prevents the classic two-week delay caused by a submittal that got lost in someone's inbox. Procore handles this with formal status tracking and deadline alerts. Jobber includes it for trade contractors managing client approvals. FieldWire supports basic RFI logging but does not enforce the same level of workflow discipline.

## Where the rough edges show

No construction workflow tool is clean. The ones I keep coming back to share the same categories of friction.

Mobile offline mode is inconsistent across the board. FieldWire lets you pull drawings and annotate them offline, but sync conflicts sometimes appear when two field people edit the same sheet and come back online at different times. Procore's mobile app works better offline but still occasionally misses syncing older attachments. This matters when you are on a basement pour or a remote job site with spotty cell service.

Change order tracking is where most teams hit walls. Procore has the strongest native workflow for this, but it requires buying the Financial Management add-on, which pushes the per-user cost higher. Teams that skip the add-on end up managing change orders in spreadsheets anyway, which defeats the whole point of having the software.

Integration gaps are the silent budget killer. None of these tools integrate natively with every accounting package. Procore connects to QuickBooks Online and Sage 100/300, but if your team uses Foundation Software or CMiC, you are looking at a custom integration or a manual export-import process. FieldWire integrates with QuickBooks and Xero through Zapier rather than natively, which means a third-party subscription and a point of failure outside your control.

## Hidden costs most buyers miss

The feature vendors do not advertise upfront is migration effort. Moving an active project from one platform to another is not as simple as exporting a CSV. Procore's data import tools require structured files and often need the vendor's professional services team to help, which runs $2,000 to $10,000 depending on project count and data complexity. I watched one mid-size GC spend three weeks and $6,500 in internal labor just importing subcontractor contact info and past project histories because their old system stored dates in a format Procore rejected.

Another hidden cost is training time. FieldWire is the easiest to learn, but even here, superintendents who are used to paper clipboards resist the habit change. One project manager told me it took six weeks before his crew consistently logged daily reports in-app instead of going back to the clipboard. During those six weeks, the tool delivered less value than he hoped, which made leadership question the investment before it had a chance to prove itself.

## Which tool fits which team

If you run a general contracting firm with 15 or more projects open and need financial management, change orders, and heavy drawing coordination in one place, Procore is the right call despite the price. The platform is mature, the integrations are real, and the support team understands construction workflows. You pay for that maturity.

If you are a residential trade contractor doing remodels and new builds with crews under 20, Jobber gives you scheduling, invoicing, and client communication in a single monthly fee that will not break your margin. You will outgrow it when you start managing multi-tenant commercial projects, but that is a good problem to have.

If you are a small crew that mainly needs to mark up drawings and track punch lists without wrestling with accounting modules, FieldWire costs almost nothing to start and gets the job done. Just budget separately for whatever tool you use to send invoices and track expenses.

The best workflow for construction is the one your crew actually uses daily, not the one with the most feature tabs. Pick the tool that matches your current project mix, budget the migration time, and give your team eight weeks before judging whether it is worth keeping.
