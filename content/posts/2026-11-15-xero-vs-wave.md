## Key takeaways
- Wave offers a genuinely free accounting tier while Xero's cheapest Starter plan costs $13/month per bank account connection, so budget-conscious solopreneurs lean toward Wave from day one.
- Xero supports multi-currency tracking and connects to over a thousand integrations including Shopify, Gusto, and Bill.com, making it the better choice for growing teams with complex stacks.
- Wave charges 2.9% plus 30 cents per credit card transaction through its payments product, which adds up fast at volume, whereas Xero integrates with third-party payment processors that may carry lower fee structures.

## When the bill comes due

I've watched two teams make opposite choices here, and both were right for where they were. One was a freelance graphic designer who needed invoicing yesterday and zero monthly overhead. The other ran a boutique agency billing clients in three currencies and already juggled five different SaaS tools. Wave won for the first. Xero won for the second. Neither would have been happy with the opposite call.

The deciding factor usually comes down to one question: how much complexity are you willing to manage as revenue grows?

## What you'll actually pay

Wave Free gives you unlimited invoicing, expense tracking, and bank reconciliation with no monthly cost. You only pay when you accept payments through Wave Payments (2.9% + 30¢ per transaction) or run payroll through their partner Gusto. Wave Plus costs $45/month and unlocks inventory tracking, recurring invoices, and automated late fees.

Xero Starters is $13/month per connected bank or credit card account. That means two accounts equal $26. Xero Standard jumps to $39/month per account. Premium runs $74/month per account. Payroll in the US starts at an additional $25/month plus $6 per employee per month, which is separate from your base subscription entirely.

If you're processing even modest payment volume on Wave, those transaction fees eat into margins faster than you might expect. A shop pulling in $10,000 per month in card payments pays roughly $290 in processing fees alone, which nearly matches Wave Plus's monthly price. Xero doesn't bundle payments into its base cost, but you can connect Stripe or Square at rates you negotiate yourself.

## Features that actually matter day to day

Both tools handle the basics well: create invoices, record expenses, reconcile accounts, and generate profit-and-loss statements. The divergence appears in features most small businesses encounter once they hit year two.

Wave gives you client portals where customers can view and pay invoices directly. It tracks inventory with basic SKU management and COGS reporting. Its mobile app lets you snap receipt photos and log expenses on the go, which matters if you're constantly between locations. The interface stays clean and unintimidating, a feature in itself for non-accountants.

Xero includes bank reconciliation flows that pull transaction descriptions automatically and match them against your books with fewer manual clicks. Its reports section covers standard financials plus dedicated VAT/GST filings for international users. Multi-currency is built in rather than bolted on, meaning you can hold balances in different currencies and see real-time gain or loss across periods. The mobile app is functional but consistently ranks lower in user satisfaction surveys compared to Wave's.

I've seen agencies spend several hours during onboarding migrating invoice templates and historical data between platforms. Wave exports are cleaner CSV files that land without formatting surprises. Xero imports sometimes require reformatting bank feeds before they'll accept batch uploads from older systems.

## Where each tool falls short

Wave lacks native payroll integration at launch. You need Gusto or another provider, which means managing two dashboards during pay periods. The free tier also restricts you to one user login, so any contractor or bookkeeper reviewing the books needs you to share credentials rather than granting guest access. Customer support lives primarily through email and community forums on the free plan, not live chat or phone.

Xero's per-account pricing model punishes businesses that maintain multiple banking relationships. A company with a checking account, savings account, credit card, and PayPal balance pays four times the base rate. That structure rewards simplicity but becomes expensive quickly as your operational reality diversifies. Some users report that certain integrations break after platform updates, requiring manual reconnection or waiting for the integration partner to patch compatibility.

G2 reviews from mid-2026 show Wave holding a 4.4-star rating across roughly 1,800 reviews, while Xero sits around 4.5 stars across more than 2,400 reviews. Both are strong scores. The difference is in complaint patterns: Wave users frequently cite limited integrations and the absence of project-level time tracking, while Xero reviewers complain about customer support response times on lower plans and occasional dashboard slowness during peak month-end periods.

## Who should pick which

If you're a solo consultant or freelancer under $200,000 in annual revenue with simple invoicing needs and no desire to pay monthly subscriptions, Wave Free handles everything without pressure to upgrade. You accept card payments occasionally, the transaction fees stay manageable, and the single-user limit matches your actual team size.

If you're a small business with employees, multiple revenue streams, or plans to scale past three years of operation, Xero's ecosystem matures better over time. The per-account pricing feels steep until you count how many free tools people accumulate before realizing they need a single system tying everything together. Once Xero replaces QuickBooks, spreadsheet habit tracking, and whatever bookkeeping workaround you patched together, the math shifts in its favor.

Wave Plus works for product-based sellers who need inventory management but aren't ready to adopt full-scale ERP tools yet. Xero's inventory features feel slightly more polished after version upgrades, though neither platform replaces dedicated inventory software at higher volumes.

The honest observation nobody puts in marketing copy: both products will meet your needs today. The real choice is whether you want a simpler tool that may require migration later, or a heavier tool that absorbs growth before you notice the weight. I've recommended Wave first and never regretted it for the right person. I've also watched Wave users quietly start an Xero trial six months in when the free tier stopped scaling with their business.
