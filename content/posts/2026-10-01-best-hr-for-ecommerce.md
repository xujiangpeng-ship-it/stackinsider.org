## Key takeaways
- Gusto starts at $40/month plus $6 per employee for basic payroll, but its ecommerce-native features are thin and you will need third-party tools for multi-state tax filing.
- Rippling charges roughly $8 per employee per month for core HR and you can add modules starting around $4 per employee, making it affordable only if you use most of them.
- BambooHR lacks native timesheet and scheduling features, which means ecommerce teams running warehouse or fulfillment staff need a separate tool and spend more than the base price suggests.

I spent three years helping ecommerce companies move from spreadsheets and shared inboxes into actual HR systems. The teams that made the switch usually had one thing in common: they were drowning in seasonal hiring and couldn't track who had completed their paperwork, their equipment, or their training before the November rush hit. The teams that stayed on spreadsheets usually had a different story. Their HR software looked fine in the demo, then turned out to be missing exactly the feature they needed on day one.

Ecommerce is different from other industries because hiring isn't linear. You scale up fast in Q4, then downsize. You manage warehouse staff, remote marketers, and international contractors in the same system. You deal with different employment laws across states and countries. A platform that works for a 20-person content agency will likely fail you.

Here is what I learned after evaluating Gusto, Rippling, BambooHR, and Deel for teams between 25 and 200 people.

## What ecommerce teams actually need from HR software

The standard HR platform checklist includes onboarding, benefits administration, and performance reviews. Ecommerce teams need all of those things, but they also need features that most vendors treat as afterthoughts.

Seasonal hiring support matters enormously. You need to add 40 temporary warehouse workers in six weeks, then process their offboarding without losing visibility into what you paid them or what equipment they returned. Systems that make temporary hires feel like permanent ones cause real pain.

Multi-location compliance is another real requirement. If you have fulfillment centers in Kentucky and customer support staff in California, your system needs to handle state-specific tax filings, required postings, and different overtime rules. Most HR platforms charge extra for multi-state support.

International contractor management separates the platforms worth considering from the ones you should skip. Deel built its entire business around this. Rippling handles it reasonably well. The budget platforms mostly pretend it does not exist until you need it.

## What you'll actually pay

Pricing is where most ecommerce teams get surprised. Vendor websites show clean monthly-per-employee numbers that do not include the features you actually need.

Gusto's pricing structure is the most transparent. The Simple plan runs $40 per month plus $6 per employee per month. Standard is $80 plus $12 per employee. Premium is $80 plus $18 per employee. Full-service payroll is an additional $6 per employee on top of any plan. This means a team of 50 employees on the Standard plan with full-service payroll pays $80 plus $900 monthly, or $980 per month. That is straightforward.

Rippling charges around $8 per employee per month for its core platform, which includes HR and payroll. Additional modules cost extra. People Operations is about $4 per employee. Time Tracking adds roughly $4 per employee. Benefits Management is approximately $4 per employee. If you use all four modules with 50 employees, you are looking at roughly $28 per employee per month, or $1,400 monthly.

BambooHR does not publish pricing. Sales teams quote custom rates based on module selection and seat count. Expect $6 to $12 per employee per month for core HR, with talent management and time tracking adding significant cost on top. A 50-person team commonly pays between $400 and $800 monthly once everything is added.

Deel charges $599 per month minimum for its Professional plan, plus $199 per contractor per month. International employer of record services start around $599 per hire per month. This is expensive if you only have US-based workers, but it is often cheaper than setting up legal entities in each country yourself.

## Features that actually matter for ecommerce

Payroll accuracy deserves more attention than it gets. During peak seasons, a payroll error affecting 60 warehouse workers is not a minor inconvenience. It is a legal problem and a morale problem happening at the same time. Gusto and Rippling both have automatic tax filing and direct deposit guarantee policies that cover mistakes. BambooHR does not run payroll natively, so you pair it with ADP or another provider and accept that someone else owns your accuracy risk.

Onboarding workflows are the feature that separates good platforms from frustrating ones. The best systems let you build checklists that route to different teams automatically. When a new warehouse worker starts, IT gets notified to ship a laptop, facilities confirms their locker assignment, and training schedules their safety course. When a remote designer starts, a different set of notifications fires off. Ecommerce teams that master this setup save hours every time they hire.

Time tracking and scheduling are the features most ecommerce teams say they need and then discover their HR platform does not handle well. BambooHR requires a separate integration for this. Gusto offers basic time tracking only on its Premium plan and it is designed for salaried workers, not shift-based fulfillment staff. Rippling Time Tracking handles shift scheduling competently but its reporting is weaker than dedicated tools like When I Work, which many warehouse managers prefer anyway.

Benefits administration is where the pricing gets tricky. Most platforms charge per employee for benefits management, even if you only have 10 people enrolled. A 200-person ecommerce company with 50 benefits participants still pays for 200 seats on that module. This is a hidden cost that shows up on renewal, not on sign-up.

## Where it falls short

Every platform I evaluated has real limitations that become painful after six months of actual use.

Gusto's multi-state compliance support is limited. It handles payroll tax filing well for the states where you have incorporated, but if you hire remotely across five new states during a peak season, you may need to register separately or pay for a third-party service like Avalara to stay compliant. The platform does not flag new state registration requirements proactively. You need to manage that yourself or ask your accountant to monitor it.

Rippling's mobile app is functional but not polished. Warehouse workers who need to clock in, view pay stubs, or request time off from their phones report frequent syncing issues. The app works, but it crashes during peak usage times, which is exactly when your team needs it most. This is a pattern I have seen repeat across multiple client deployments.

BambooHR's reporting is decent for headcount and turnover, but it struggles with anything involving time data because it does not collect time directly. You export from your time tracking system and import it manually, which creates a gap between when work is logged and when you can analyze it. The delay is usually one to three business days.

Deel's pricing model penalizes smaller teams. The $599 monthly minimum makes it uneconomical if you only have five international contractors. You end up paying $599 even though the per-contractor cost would suggest a $794 total, and the minimum creates poor value at low headcount. The platform also requires a 30-day notice period for cancellations, which catches some teams off guard.

## What the vendors will not tell you

The biggest hidden cost in this category is implementation time, not software cost. I watched a 50-person ecommerce company spend six weeks migrating from spreadsheets to Rippling. The migration itself took two weeks. The remaining four weeks were spent training managers, fixing data that did not match between their old system and the new one, and re-building onboarding workflows from scratch. Most vendors quote implementation as a one-week onboarding call. The reality is longer.

Another thing vendors understate is the friction of integrating HR data with your ecommerce stack. If you use Shopify, Salesforce, or HubSpot for customer data, connecting that to your HR system for things like cohort analysis or commission tracking usually requires custom development or a middleware tool like Zapier. Each integration point is a potential failure point, and they break when either side updates their API.

Community forums and Reddit threads reveal a consistent complaint across all platforms: support response times during open enrollment or peak hiring seasons are slow. Teams report waiting 24 to 48 hours for a response when they need immediate help processing a batch of new hires. This is not unique to one vendor. It is an industry-wide capacity problem that gets worse exactly when you need support the most.

## Who should use what

If you have fewer than 75 US-based employees and want the simplest payroll and HR system available, Gusto is the right choice. It will not solve every problem, but it works reliably for the problems it covers and its pricing is transparent enough that you will not get surprised at renewal.

If you have 75 to 200 employees across multiple states and you want a single platform handling HR, payroll, time tracking, and benefits, Rippling is worth the higher price and the longer implementation. The automation around seasonal hiring and offboarding pays for itself if you hire and offboard more than ten people per quarter.

If you already have a strong relationship with a benefits broker and your main need is employee records and performance management without running payroll through the same system, BambooHR is a solid administrative tool. Just budget separately for time tracking and remember that its reporting will have gaps you need to fill manually.

If you hire international contractors or plan to open entities in other countries within the next 18 months, Deel is the only platform in this comparison that makes that painless. The cost is high, but setting up foreign entities yourself costs more in legal fees and compliance risk.

The best HR system for an ecommerce team is the one that matches your current headcount and geographic spread, not the one that would be perfect if you had twice as many people. Pick the platform you need today and leave room to move when you outgrow it. Most companies revisit this decision every 18 to 24 months anyway.
