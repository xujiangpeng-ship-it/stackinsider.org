Agile CRM and Bitrix24 both promise to organize sales, marketing, and support in one place. But the way they handle daily tasks is different enough that picking the wrong one can slow your team down for months. I’ve set up both tools for small businesses and mid-sized teams, and the biggest frustration isn’t missing features—it’s the friction you don’t see until you’re already using it.

Here’s what actually matters when you compare them.

## What you’ll actually pay

Agile CRM starts at $15 per user per month for the Starter plan. That includes contact management, email tracking, and basic automation. The next tier jumps to $50 per user, which adds landing pages, telephony, and advanced reporting. Free plan covers 10 users but limits storage to 1,000 contacts and 1,000 emails per month.

Bitrix24 has a free tier for unlimited users, but it restricts you to 5 GB storage and basic CRM features. Paid plans begin at $49 per month for the entire team (not per user), which includes 24 GB storage, automation, and telephony. The next tier is $99 per month for 100 GB storage and advanced analytics. If you need more, you’ll pay $199 per month for 1,024 GB.

Hidden costs show up in both. Agile CRM charges extra for premium support and additional email campaigns. Bitrix24 charges for extra storage blocks and third-party integrations like Mailchimp or Zapier. If you have a large contact list, Agile CRM’s pricing can escalate quickly. Bitrix24’s team pricing looks attractive until you realize the storage limits force upgrades sooner than expected.

## Features that actually matter

Agile CRM focuses on sales and marketing automation. The interface is clean, with a Kanban-style deal pipeline that sales teams like. You can set up automated email sequences based on user behavior, and the built-in telephony works well for call logging. The mobile app is functional but lacks offline mode, which is a problem for field teams.

Bitrix24 is more of a collaboration suite with CRM bolted on. It includes task management, document storage, and a built-in chat. The CRM itself is solid but feels secondary—you’ll navigate through project boards and calendars to get to deals. The automation is powerful but requires more setup than Agile CRM. The mobile app has offline mode, which is useful for teams that travel.

One feature Agile CRM handles better is email tracking. You can see when a prospect opens an email or clicks a link, and it logs that activity directly in the contact record. Bitrix24 does this too, but the integration feels clunky—you have to switch between the CRM and the email module.

Bitrix24’s strength is its built-in collaboration tools. If your team already uses Slack or Trello, you might not need them. But if you’re looking for a single tool to replace multiple apps, Bitrix24 can do that. The downside is that the CRM features are spread across different sections, so training takes longer.

## Where it shines (and where it doesn’t)

Agile CRM is best for small sales teams that need automation without extra complexity. The learning curve is shallow, and you can start running campaigns within a day. The reporting is basic but covers the essentials—deal stages, email performance, and call logs.

Bitrix24 works better for teams that need project management alongside CRM. The task boards integrate with deals, so you can track both sales and delivery in one place. The downside is that the CRM isn’t as polished. For example, you can’t bulk-edit deal stages in the mobile app, which is a common complaint in user reviews.

Both tools have quirks. Agile CRM’s automation builder is flexible but can get messy if you have complex workflows. Bitrix24’s interface feels dated, and the navigation isn’t intuitive. Some users report that the search function is slow, especially with large contact lists.

## The rough edges

Agile CRM’s biggest limitation is scalability. The free plan is generous, but once you hit the storage or contact limits, the pricing jumps. The API is functional but lacks documentation for some endpoints, which makes custom integrations harder.

Bitrix24’s storage limits are the main issue. The free plan gives you 5 GB, which fills up fast if you store documents and emails. The paid plans increase storage, but the pricing isn’t linear—you’ll pay $99 for 100 GB, then $199 for 1,024 GB. If you need 200 GB, you’re stuck paying for the higher tier.

Another problem is support. Agile CRM offers premium support only on higher tiers. Bitrix24’s support is included, but response times vary. G2 reviews as of June 2026 show that users rate Agile CRM’s support 3.8/5 and Bitrix24’s 3.5/5.

## Who should pick which

Agile CRM is the better choice if:
- Your team is under 20 people.
- You focus on sales and marketing automation.
- You want a simple interface with quick setup.

Bitrix24 is the better choice if:
- You need project management alongside CRM.
- Your team is larger than 20 people.
- You want a single tool to replace multiple apps.

If you’re still unsure, both offer free trials. Agile CRM’s trial is 14 days, and Bitrix24’s is 30 days. Use that time to test the automation and mobile app—those are where most teams hit snags.
