The first CRM we installed for a mid-sized machine shop lasted six months. Sales reps loved the pipeline view. The shop floor ignored it. Parts kept shipping late because the CRM didn’t sync with the ERP, and nobody had time to enter the same job twice. That’s the gap most vendors won’t tell you about: a CRM for manufacturing isn’t just about contacts—it’s about whether the tool can talk to the machines, the schedules, and the people who actually build things.

## What you’re actually buying

Most CRMs marketed to manufacturers are repackaged sales tools. They assume you care about deal stages and email templates. You probably care more about:

- Work orders that flow from quote to production without copy-paste.
- Real-time inventory visibility so sales doesn’t promise parts you don’t have.
- Service ticket tracking that ties to warranty contracts and serial numbers.

If the CRM can’t show a sales rep the exact lead time for a custom part based on current shop load, it’s just another contact database.

## What you’ll actually pay

Pricing for manufacturing CRMs splits into two camps: generic sales CRMs with a few add-ons, and industry-specific tools that cost more upfront but save time later.

Hidden costs show up in three places:

1. ERP integration. Generic CRMs charge $10–20k for a one-time middleware setup. Industry-specific tools usually include it, but lock you into their ERP.
2. Shop-floor access. Most CRMs charge extra for “field service” or “manufacturing” user types. These users often just need read-only job status, not full CRM features.
3. Data migration. Moving from spreadsheets or an old system can add 20–30 hours of consulting at $150–200/hour.

Infor CRM includes a built-in MES module, but the interface feels like 2010. JobBOSS² has a modern UI but only works with its own ERP. HubSpot and Salesforce let you pick your ERP, but you’ll pay for the glue.

## Features that actually matter

Most “manufacturing CRM” checklists repeat the same items: contact management, quotes, orders. Here’s what teams actually use daily:

**Quote-to-cash automation**
A good system pulls live material costs and machine rates from the ERP. When steel prices jump, quotes update automatically. Salesforce and JobBOSS² do this well. HubSpot requires a custom integration that breaks every time the ERP updates.

**Serial number tracking**
If you build equipment with warranties, you need to tie service tickets to specific units. Infor CRM handles this out of the box. Generic CRMs force you to use custom objects, which slows down reporting.

**Shop-floor visibility**
The best tools show a Gantt chart of current jobs with color-coded delays. JobBOSS² does this in the CRM. Salesforce can do it, but you’ll need a third-party app and a full-time admin to keep it running.

**Mobile service app**
Field techs need to see service history, update tickets, and order parts from the road. JobBOSS² and Infor have dedicated mobile apps. HubSpot and Zoho use the same app as sales reps, which is cluttered with features techs don’t need.

## The rough edges

No tool is perfect. Here’s what users complain about:

- **JobBOSS²**: The CRM is strong, but the ERP side lacks modern APIs. If you need e-commerce or advanced analytics, you’ll hit limits.
- **Infor CRM**: The UI is dated and slow. Training takes longer because the workflows don’t match modern web apps.
- **Salesforce**: Flexible but expensive. The manufacturing cloud add-on costs $150/user/month, and you’ll still need a consultant to set it up.
- **HubSpot**: Cheaper, but the manufacturing features are bolt-ons. The mobile app doesn’t support offline mode, which is a problem for field techs.

G2 reviews as of June 2026 show JobBOSS² leads in user satisfaction for mid-sized shops (4.3/5), while Salesforce scores higher for large enterprises (4.1/5). Infor sits at 3.8/5, dragged down by UI complaints.

## What sets the good ones apart

The tools that work have two things in common:

1. They treat the shop floor as a first-class user, not an afterthought.
2. They include ERP integration in the base price, not as a $20k add-on.

JobBOSS² and Infor CRM do both. HubSpot and Salesforce do neither, but they’re easier to customize if you have a big IT team.

## Who should buy what

- **Small shops (10–50 employees, $5–20M revenue)**: Zoho CRM with a custom ERP connector. It’s cheap and flexible, but you’ll outgrow it in 3–4 years.
- **Mid-sized (50–200 employees, $20–100M revenue)**: JobBOSS² if you want an all-in-one system. HubSpot if you need to keep your existing ERP and can handle the integration work.
- **Large enterprises (200+ employees, $100M+ revenue)**: Salesforce Manufacturing Cloud. It’s expensive, but it scales and integrates with everything.

If you’re in a regulated industry (aerospace, medical), Infor CRM’s compliance features save time. If you’re not, the dated UI isn’t worth the hassle.

## What to watch

JobBOSS² is rolling out a cloud-native version in Q4 2026. Early previews show a cleaner UI and better mobile support. If you’re on the fence, wait for that update—it might close the gap with Salesforce on usability without the price tag.

Salesforce is pushing AI features, but most are still in beta. Don’t count on them to solve your workflow problems yet.

Infor CRM isn’t dead, but the roadmap is unclear. If you’re considering it, ask for a written commitment on future updates.

## The bottom line

A CRM for manufacturing isn’t about sales. It’s about whether the tool can bridge the gap between the front office and the shop floor. If it can’t, you’ll end up with two systems, double data entry, and frustrated teams. Pick the one that fits your size, budget, and willingness to customize. The rest is just noise.
