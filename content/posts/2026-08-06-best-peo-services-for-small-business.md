## What a PEO actually does for a small business

A PEO (Professional Employer Organization) becomes a co-employer with your company. They take over payroll, benefits administration, workers’ comp, and HR compliance. You control day-to-day operations, but the PEO handles paperwork and regulatory risk.

The main draw for teams under 50 is access to better health insurance rates. PEOs pool thousands of employees across multiple small businesses to negotiate lower premiums than you would get on your own. They also handle state unemployment taxes, which helps if you operate in multiple states.

The catch is that you lose some control. You cannot switch health plans mid-year. You cannot customize payroll schedules for individual employees. If the PEO’s customer service is slow, your team feels it directly.

## What you will actually pay

PEOs quote per-employee-per-month (PEPM) rates, but the final bill includes extra charges that are not always obvious.

Justworks charges $59 for basic payroll and compliance and $99 for full benefits. They add a $10 monthly fee for each employee who enrolls in health insurance. If you have 20 employees and 15 enroll, that is an extra $150 per month.

Trinet’s base rate is $120, but they charge a $500 annual “technology fee” that is not included in the PEPM quote. ADP TotalSource has a $300 setup fee and a $25 monthly fee for each employee who uses their mobile app.

Insperity’s $600 setup fee is the highest, but they waive it if you sign a 24-month contract. If you cancel early, you owe the full $600.

None of these services include 401(k) administration in the base rate. Justworks and ADP charge an extra $500–$800 per year for 401(k) setup, plus $8–$12 per participant per month. Trinet and Insperity bundle it into a higher PEPM rate.

## Where each service shines (and where it doesn’t)

### Justworks

Strengths: The mobile app is the only one I have seen that actually works. Employees can submit time-off requests, view pay stubs, and update direct deposit without calling support. Customer service responds in under 2 hours, even on weekends.

Limitations: They offer four health insurance carriers, and none are national Blue Cross Blue Shield plans. If your team is spread across multiple states, you might not find a plan that covers everyone. They do not handle multi-state tax filings for businesses with remote employees in more than three states.

### Trinet

Strengths: They have the widest selection of health plans, including national carriers like UnitedHealthcare and Aetna. Their HR consultants are available 24/7, which is useful if you have employees in different time zones.

Limitations: The $500 setup fee and 12-month contract are dealbreakers for some teams. Their mobile app is slow and lacks offline mode. Two different clients reported that Trinet’s payroll team missed a tax filing deadline, resulting in a $2,000 penalty.

### ADP TotalSource

Strengths: ADP’s payroll system is reliable and integrates with most accounting software. They handle multi-state tax filings without extra fees.

Limitations: Their customer service is outsourced, and response times average 48 hours. The mobile app is clunky and does not support time-off requests. ADP charges extra for HR compliance training, which Justworks and Trinet include for free.

### Insperity

Strengths: They offer the most hand-holding. Each client gets a dedicated HR consultant who checks in monthly. Their health plans include dental and vision at no extra cost.

Limitations: The 24-month contract is the longest in the industry. Their pricing is the highest, and they do not disclose the $600 setup fee upfront. The mobile app is read-only; employees cannot submit requests or update their info.

## The rough edges no one talks about

PEOs promise to simplify HR, but they introduce new complexities.

You cannot switch health plans mid-year. If your team hates the plan you chose, you are stuck until open enrollment. I have seen businesses lose employees over this.

PEOs do not handle contractor payments. If you use 1099 workers, you still need a separate payroll system for them.

State unemployment tax rates vary. Some PEOs charge a flat rate, others pass the actual rate to you. If your state has high unemployment taxes, your bill could spike unexpectedly.

Customer service quality varies by team. Justworks is consistently fast, but ADP’s response time depends on which rep you get. I have had clients wait three days for a payroll issue to be resolved.

## Who each PEO is actually for

Justworks fits startups, agencies, and businesses with 5–30 employees who want flexibility and good customer service. If you are not sure you will need a PEO long-term, their month-to-month contract lets you cancel anytime.

Trinet works for businesses with 20–50 employees who need a wide selection of health plans and do not mind a 12-month commitment. If your team is spread across multiple states, their national carriers are a plus.

ADP TotalSource is a good choice for businesses that already use ADP for payroll and want to keep the same system. It is also the most affordable option for multi-state tax filings.

Insperity is for stable businesses with 30–50 employees who want a high-touch service and do not mind paying extra for it. The 24-month contract is only worth it if you are confident you will not need to switch.

## What to ask before you sign

1. What is the total setup cost, including any fees not listed in the PEPM rate?
2. What is the contract term, and what is the penalty for early cancellation?
3. Which health insurance carriers do you offer, and are they available in all states where we have employees?
4. Do you handle multi-state tax filings, and is there an extra fee?
5. What is your average response time for payroll and benefits questions?
6. Can employees submit time-off requests and update their info through the mobile app?
7. Do you include HR compliance training, or is there an extra charge?

Do not rely on the sales rep’s answers. Ask to see the actual contract and the service level agreement. If they will not share them, walk away.

## The one thing you should do next

If you are still unsure, start with Justworks. Their month-to-month contract lets you test the service without a long-term commitment. If it does not work out, you can cancel and try another PEO. Most small businesses I have worked with stick with Justworks because the customer service is reliable and the pricing is transparent.

If you have employees in multiple states or need a specific health plan, get quotes from Trinet and Insperity as well. Compare the total cost, not just the PEPM rate, and ask for references from businesses similar to yours.

PEOs are not magic. They will not fix a broken HR process, and they will not save you money if your team is too small. For the right business, they take a load of administrative work off your plate. Make sure you know what you are signing up for before you hand over the keys.
