I’ve moved three teams off Zoho CRM and two onto Freshsales in the last eighteen months. The decision never came down to feature checklists. It came down to which tool actually matched how the sales team worked day-to-day: call logs, deal stages, and the small frustrations that add up when you’re closing fifty deals a month.

Here’s what you need to know without the marketing fluff.

## What you’ll actually pay

Freshsales and Zoho CRM both advertise low entry prices. The real cost appears after you add users, storage, and the integrations your team already uses.

Freshsales pricing (per user/month, billed annually):
- Growth: $15
- Pro: $39
- Enterprise: $69

Zoho CRM pricing (per user/month, billed annually):
- Standard: $14
- Professional: $23
- Enterprise: $40
- Ultimate: $52

Both tools charge extra for additional file storage. Freshsales gives 2 GB per user; Zoho gives 1 GB. If your team attaches contracts or product screenshots to deals, you’ll hit that limit fast. Freshsales adds $4 per 10 GB block. Zoho adds $2 per 5 GB block. For a ten-person team that stores 50 GB, Freshsales costs $20 extra; Zoho costs $20 extra. The math evens out, but Zoho’s smaller increments can feel cheaper month-to-month.

Zoho CRM also has a free tier for up to three users. Freshsales does not. If you’re a solo founder or a tiny team testing the waters, Zoho’s free tier is the only way to start without a credit card.

## What sets each apart

### Freshsales: built for teams that live on the phone

Freshsales started as a sales acceleration tool inside Freshworks. The phone system is the standout. You can buy local or toll-free numbers directly inside the CRM. Calls log automatically, recordings attach to the contact, and you can set up IVR menus without touching a separate telephony provider. If your team makes fifty calls a day, this saves at least two hours a week.

The AI assistant, Freddy, is optional. It scores leads based on email opens, website visits, and past deal history. I’ve seen it work well for mid-market teams with consistent deal cycles. For early-stage startups with unpredictable sales, the scores feel arbitrary and teams turn it off.

Freshsales also has a built-in chat widget. You can embed it on your website and route chats to the right sales rep based on territory or product line. It’s not as customizable as Intercom or Drift, but it’s one less tool to pay for.

### Zoho CRM: the Swiss Army knife with sharp edges

Zoho CRM is part of the larger Zoho One suite. If you already use Zoho Books, Zoho Desk, or Zoho Projects, the integration is smooth. You can create a quote in Books and attach it to a deal in CRM without exporting anything. For teams that need accounting and support tied to sales, this is the main reason to pick Zoho.

The customization is deep. You can rename modules, add custom fields, and build workflows that trigger based on almost any data point. I’ve seen teams use this to track franchise locations, subscription renewals, and even equipment rentals. The downside is the learning curve. Zoho’s interface feels cluttered compared to Freshsales. New users take longer to find basic features like contact import or report filters.

Zoho also has a built-in email client. You can send and receive emails inside the CRM. It’s not as polished as Outlook or Gmail, but it keeps everything in one place. Freshsales integrates with Gmail and Outlook but doesn’t replace them.

## Where each falls short

Freshsales has two consistent pain points. First, the mobile app is limited. You can view contacts and deals, but you can’t edit custom fields or run reports. If your team works remotely or travels often, this is a real gap. Second, the reporting is basic. You can create bar charts and pie charts, but you can’t build cohort analysis or custom dashboards without exporting to Excel or connecting to a BI tool.

Zoho CRM’s biggest issue is performance. The interface feels slow compared to Freshsales. Pages take an extra second to load, and bulk actions like importing contacts or updating deal stages can time out. Zoho support acknowledges this and recommends using Chrome with fewer tabs open. It’s a workaround, not a fix.

Zoho also has a reputation for aggressive upselling. Once you’re in the CRM, you’ll get emails and in-app messages pushing Zoho Desk, Zoho Analytics, and Zoho Campaigns. Freshsales does this too, but the frequency is lower.

## Who should pick which

Pick Freshsales if:
- Your team makes a lot of calls and wants the phone system built-in.
- You want a clean interface that new users can learn in under an hour.
- You don’t need deep accounting or project management tied to sales.

Pick Zoho CRM if:
- You already use other Zoho products and want everything in one suite.
- You need heavy customization for a niche sales process.
- You’re a solo founder or tiny team using the free tier to start.

For teams between 10 and 50 users, Freshsales usually wins. The phone system and cleaner interface save time. For teams over 50 users, Zoho’s customization and suite integrations start to matter more, but you’ll need to budget for training and potential performance tweaks.

Both tools have 14-day free trials. Use them. Import ten real contacts, make a few calls, and run a report. The trial will show you the friction points faster than any review.
