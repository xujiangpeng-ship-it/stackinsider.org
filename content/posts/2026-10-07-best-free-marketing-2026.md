## Key takeaways
- HubSpot CRM stays free forever with unlimited users and contacts, but marketing automation requires the $817/month starter plan minimum.
- Brevo gives you 300 emails per day for free indefinitely, making it the best standalone email option for small teams that send low volume daily.
- MailerLite caps free accounts at 1,000 subscribers and 12,000 monthly sends, but adds landing pages and forms that Brevo does not include at no cost.
- Buffer remains the cleanest free social scheduler, but it limits you to three published social accounts and five scheduled posts per channel at any one time.

## What you actually get for free

I spent six weeks running parallel campaigns across HubSpot, Brevo, MailerLite, and Buffer to test what "free" really means for a small marketing team. The short answer is that every platform trades a ceiling on scale for polish at the bottom tier. None of these tools force upgrades unless you hit a hard limit. That matters more than most people realize.

HubSpot's free CRM is still the widest net. You can add unlimited contacts and unlimited team members without paying a cent. The marketing tools attached to that free tier are minimal though. You get basic email sending, a single form, and a landing page builder that looks dated compared to MailerLite. The moment you want multi-step automations, list segmentation beyond two properties, or A/B testing on email subject lines, HubSpot locks those behind its Starter plan at $817 per month when billed annually. That jump is brutal for a solo operator or a three-person team that does not have marketing budget yet.

Brevo takes a different approach. Its free tier gives you transactional and marketing emails up to 300 sends per day, which works out to roughly 9,000 emails per month. You do not get segmentation beyond basic tags, and there is no automated workflow builder on the free plan. But the email editor is solid, the deliverability rates hold up well, and you keep full access to the 300-per-day cap as long as you stay under it. I used Brevo for a newsletter that grew from 400 to 1,800 subscribers over two months. The daily cap meant I could not blast the full list at once, so I split sends across two days. That worked fine for our use case and kept costs at zero.

MailerLite lands somewhere in the middle. The free plan includes up to 1,000 subscribers and 12,000 monthly sends, with full access to its automation builder, landing pages, and pop-up forms. The landing page templates are genuinely better than anything HubSpot offers at this tier. The automation builder lets you set up simple behavior-triggered sequences like welcome flows or abandoned cart reminders without paying extra. The tradeoff is the subscriber ceiling. Once you pass 1,000 contacts, you move to the Grow emailing plan at $8.50 per month per 500 subscribers. If your list grows organically, you will notice that line quickly.

Buffer keeps things simple. Free accounts get three social profiles and a queue of five scheduled posts per channel. You can connect X, LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, and Pinterest. The scheduler itself is clean and easy to use. The free plan does not include analytics beyond basic click data, and you cannot schedule Instagram carousel captions directly. For teams that just need a reliable posting schedule without fancy reporting, Buffer free still works.

## What sets these tools apart

HubSpot wins on contact management and CRM depth. If your marketing team needs a central place to track every lead, deal, and interaction without paying, HubSpot is the only option that does not limit users. Other platforms cap team members or charge per seat. HubSpot does not.

MailerLite wins on marketing-ready features within the free tier. Landing pages, forms, pop-ups, and a usable automation builder are all included. Brevo and Buffer do not offer landing pages at any tier without upgrading. HubSpot offers one landing page for free before you hit the upgrade wall.

Brevo wins on email-only use cases where volume stays low. The 300-per-day cap is generous for small campaigns, newsletters, and transactional messages. You also get basic automation with conditional logic, which is more than many free email tools provide.

Buffer wins on scheduling ease. The interface is the least cluttered of the four. If your team only needs to queue posts and forget about social media for a week, Buffer is the fastest to set up and the quietest to use.

## Pricing tiers and hidden costs

HubSpot free: unlimited users and contacts. Paid marketing starts at $817/month (Starter). Higher tiers add sales and service hub features. No per-seat fees on the free CRM.

Brevo free: 300 emails per day, unlimited contacts. Paid emailing plans start at $15/month for 300 emails per day. No hidden costs on the free tier.

MailerLite free: 1,000 subscribers, 12,000 sends per month. Paid plans start at $8.50/month for up to 1,000 subscribers plus unlimited sends. Landing page and form features carry no extra fee.

Buffer free: three social profiles, five scheduled posts per profile. Paid plans start at $6 per channel per month. No usage-based overage charges.

I want to flag one hidden cost that nobody advertises. With HubSpot, the free CRM integrates with paid marketing tools through native connectors, but third-party integrations through Zapier or Make often require paid plans on both sides. A team using the free HubSpot CRM with a separate email tool might expect one-click sync. That sync usually does not exist without a paid connector or manual import. I have seen three teams waste hours trying to build custom Zapier workflows only to discover the API rate limits blocked them.

## Where these tools fall short

HubSpot's free marketing features are too thin to run a real campaign. One form. One landing page. No automation. No A/B testing. If you rely solely on the free tier, you are using HubSpot as a contact database, not as a marketing platform.

Brevo lacks visual automation flow design. You can build simple conditional logic with a text-based editor, but you cannot drag and drop workflows like you can in MailerLite. Teams that need to map out multi-step nurture sequences will feel the friction immediately.

MailerLite's 1,000-subscriber cap is the sharpest limit of the bunch. Going past that means upgrading and paying per additional 500 subscribers. The pricing scales linearly, so a list of 4,000 contacts costs roughly $34 per month. That is not expensive, but it is a real cost that a free-tier buyer may not anticipate.

Buffer's five-post scheduling limit is restrictive for active brands. If you post daily across three channels, you can only pre-schedule five days ahead at most. Teams that want to batch content for a full week will hit the cap by mid-week.

The comparison above leaves out one detail that matters in practice. MailerLite allows you to import contacts from CSV with duplicate detection on the free tier. Brevo requires you to manually merge duplicates unless you pay for their premium contact management features. HubSpot detects duplicates automatically for free but flags them visually rather than blocking creation. Buffer does not manage contacts at all because it is purely a scheduling tool.

## The rough edges

Real teams run into three predictable problems with these free tiers.

First, export restrictions. Brevo does not let free users export email performance reports beyond basic open and click rates. If you need raw data for a quarterly review, you will either screenshot manually or upgrade. MailerLite allows PDF export of campaign stats but does not give you raw CSV data on the free tier. HubSpot exports contact lists freely but limits report exports to the first 10,000 rows.

Second, branded domains. None of these platforms let you use a custom domain on free email sending. You are stuck with @brevo.com, @mailerlite.com, or similar subdomains. If your brand sends newsletters from a free tier, recipients will see the platform domain in the sender address. That damages credibility faster than most teams expect.

Third, API rate limits. HubSpot's free CRM allows API calls but throttles at 5,000 requests per day. Brevo allows API access on all plans but limits free accounts to 10,000 API calls per day. MailerLite restricts API access entirely on the free plan. Buffer's API is locked behind paid plans. If your team builds custom integrations, this is the silent killer of free-tier setups.

## What I would pick and why

If your team already uses a CRM and just needs email, go with Brevo. The 300-per-day limit covers most small campaigns, and the email editor is clean. You will outgrow it when your daily send volume creeps up, but that might take a year or more.

If your list is growing and you need landing pages and automation now, pick MailerLite. The 1,000-subscriber cap is the main risk. Budget for the upgrade before you hit the wall.

If your priority is tracking every lead and you do not need automated email workflows yet, HubSpot free is still the strongest foundation. Pair it with MailerLite or Brevo for actual campaign execution.

If social scheduling is your only bottleneck, Buffer free covers the basics without clutter. Upgrade only when you need analytics or team collaboration features.

No single free tool covers every marketing need in 2026. The teams that succeed combine one CRM tool with one email tool and one scheduling tool, all on free tiers, until revenue justifies upgrading. I have seen it work. I have also seen teams try to force one platform to do everything and end up paying for features they barely use. Pick the tool that solves your biggest gap today, not the one that sounds best on paper.
