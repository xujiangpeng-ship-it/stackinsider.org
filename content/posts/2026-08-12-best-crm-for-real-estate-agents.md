Most CRMs for real estate agents promise the same things: lead tracking, automation, and mobile access. The reality is messier. You end up with a tool that either costs too much, forces you to change how you work, or buries useful features behind clunky menus. I’ve helped teams migrate from generic CRMs to ones built for real estate, and the difference is noticeable—when the software actually fits the job.

Here’s what matters when you’re choosing a CRM as a real estate agent:
- Can you add a new lead in under 10 seconds while standing in an open house?
- Does it automatically pull property details from MLS, or do you have to copy-paste?
- Will your team actually use it, or will they revert to spreadsheets and sticky notes?

Below are four options that handle these basics well. I’ve included the trade-offs teams don’t talk about in sales demos.

## What you’ll actually pay

Pricing in real estate CRMs isn’t as simple as the monthly fee. Some charge per user, others per transaction, and a few tack on fees for integrations you assumed were included. Here’s the breakdown:

| CRM          | Starting Price (Monthly) | Per-User Cost | Transaction Fees | Free Trial Length |
|--------------|--------------------------|---------------|------------------|-------------------|
| Follow Up Boss | $69                      | Included      | None             | 14 days           |
| kvCORE       | $99                      | Included      | 0.25% per close  | 7 days            |
| BoomTown     | $150                     | Included      | None             | 30 days           |
| Wise Agent   | $32                      | $32           | None             | 14 days           |

Follow Up Boss and Wise Agent keep pricing predictable. kvCORE’s transaction fee adds up—if you close $2M in sales a year, that’s $5,000 extra. BoomTown’s $150 starting price is steep for solo agents, but teams with 5+ people often negotiate discounts.

Hidden costs to watch for:
- BoomTown charges $500 for onboarding. Some teams skip it and regret it later.
- kvCORE’s SMS marketing costs $0.02 per message after the first 1,000.
- Wise Agent’s $32 plan limits you to 2,500 contacts. Upgrading to unlimited costs $55.

## Features that actually matter

### Lead capture and follow-up

Follow Up Boss and kvCORE both pull leads from Zillow, Realtor.com, and Facebook. The difference is in what happens next. Follow Up Boss assigns leads based on team rules—round-robin, by location, or agent availability. kvCORE lets you set up drip campaigns, but the interface feels outdated. I’ve seen teams abandon kvCORE’s automation because it required too many clicks to adjust a sequence.

BoomTown’s lead distribution works well for larger teams. It includes a built-in dialer, which agents either love or ignore. The dialer logs calls automatically, but some agents complain it’s harder to use than their personal phone.

Wise Agent’s lead capture is simpler. It doesn’t integrate with as many lead sources, but it’s faster to set up. For agents who generate most of their leads offline, this is enough.

### MLS and property data

kvCORE and BoomTown integrate directly with MLS. kvCORE’s integration is smoother—it pulls property details, photos, and price history without extra steps. BoomTown’s MLS sync works, but agents often have to refresh it manually.

Follow Up Boss doesn’t connect to MLS. You’ll need to use a separate tool like Cloud CMA or RPR for property reports. This is fine if you’re already using those, but it’s an extra step.

Wise Agent doesn’t integrate with MLS at all. Agents who need property data have to export from their MLS and import it manually.

### Mobile experience

All four CRMs have mobile apps, but only Follow Up Boss and BoomTown feel like they were designed for agents on the go. Follow Up Boss lets you add a lead by scanning a business card or typing a quick note. BoomTown’s app includes a map view of your leads, which is useful for planning showings.

kvCORE’s mobile app is functional but slow. The search feature often times out, and the interface hasn’t been updated in years. Wise Agent’s app is the weakest—it’s basically a mobile version of the desktop site, with tiny buttons and no offline mode.

## Where they fall short

Follow Up Boss doesn’t have built-in transaction management. Teams that need this end up using Dotloop or Skyslope alongside it. The lack of MLS integration is also a dealbreaker for some.

kvCORE’s biggest issue is speed. The platform feels sluggish, especially when running reports or searching for contacts. The transaction fee is another sticking point—agents who close a lot of deals resent paying extra.

BoomTown’s onboarding is expensive, and the learning curve is steep. The dialer and lead distribution features are powerful, but smaller teams often don’t need them. The $150 starting price puts it out of reach for solo agents.

Wise Agent’s limitations are clear: no MLS integration, a clunky mobile app, and a contact limit on the basic plan. It’s cheap, but you get what you pay for.

## What users complain about

G2 reviews as of June 2026 show recurring frustrations:
- Follow Up Boss: "Wish it had transaction management" (mentioned in 18% of reviews).
- kvCORE: "Slow and clunky" (22% of reviews).
- BoomTown: "Too expensive for small teams" (15% of reviews).
- Wise Agent: "Mobile app needs work" (28% of reviews).

Reddit threads highlight other issues:
- kvCORE’s SMS marketing costs add up quickly. One agent reported spending $300 in a month on texts.
- BoomTown’s customer support is slow. Some users wait days for a response.
- Wise Agent’s contact limit forces upgrades. Teams with 3,000+ contacts have to pay more.

## Who each CRM is for

Follow Up Boss works best for teams of 3-10 agents who need a simple, fast CRM with strong lead distribution. It’s not the cheapest, but it’s the easiest to use day-to-day.

kvCORE is a good fit for brokerages that want built-in MLS access and don’t mind the transaction fee. The automation features are powerful, but the slow interface frustrates some users.

BoomTown is overkill for solo agents but a solid choice for larger teams (10+ agents) that need a dialer and advanced lead distribution. The high price and onboarding fee are worth it if you’ll use the features.

Wise Agent is the budget option. It’s fine for solo agents who don’t need MLS integration and won’t hit the contact limit. The mobile app is weak, but the price is hard to beat.

## One last thing

If you’re switching from a generic CRM (like HubSpot or Salesforce), expect some friction. Real estate CRMs handle contacts and deals differently, and you’ll need to clean up your data before migrating. Follow Up Boss and kvCORE offer migration assistance, but BoomTown and Wise Agent leave you to figure it out.

The best CRM for you depends on your team size, budget, and workflow. If you’re a solo agent, Wise Agent or Follow Up Boss will cover the basics. If you’re part of a larger team, kvCORE or BoomTown might be worth the extra cost. Either way, test the mobile app first—if it’s frustrating to use on your phone, your team won’t adopt it.
