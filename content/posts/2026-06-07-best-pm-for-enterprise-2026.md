## Key takeaways
- Enterprise Success Packages add 20 to 30 percent to annual costs, and Asana adds $5 per user for SCIM provisioning, raising the total to $35 per user per month.
- Smartsheet Activity Log retains audit data for 10 years, a specific advantage over competitors that keep logs for only 90 days during SOC 2 audits.
- Microsoft Project costs $55 per user one-time plus a $15,000 to $50,000 implementation fee, making it prohibitively expensive for non-Microsoft ecosystems.

## Common pitfalls and how to avoid them

Teams often make expensive mistakes when switching PM tools. Here is what tends to go wrong.

**1. Picking the cheapest option without looking at total cost.** The monthly price is only part of the story. Implementation, training, add-ons, and data migration can double the first-year spend. Run the numbers over three years.

**2. Over-customizing early on.** New teams tend to tweak every setting before they understand their actual workflows. Stick with default settings for 60 to 90 days. Customize only after you see what actually gets used.

**3. Skipping mobile testing.** Remote and field teams depend on the app. Download the iOS and Android versions before you commit. Run your core workflows on a phone.

**4. Testing with demo data.** Real problems hide behind clean sample data. Import your actual customer lists, project histories, or financial records during the trial. Integration gaps and data quality issues will surface fast.

**5. Ignoring scale.** A tool that runs fine for 10 people may choke at 50. Check documented limits on records, API calls, storage, and concurrent users. Ask about their roadmap for features you will need in a year.

## Integration capabilities

PM tools do not work alone. These are the patterns to evaluate.

- **Native integrations**: Direct connections to Slack, Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, Salesforce, and QuickBooks. These are reliable and need no middleware.

- **API access**: RESTful APIs with docs, webhook support, and rate limits that match your volume. Check whether OAuth 2.0 is available.

- **Zapier and Make**: Third-party platforms extend reach to 5,000+ apps. Useful when a tool lacks a native connection, but adds a dependency layer.

- **Custom integrations**: Enterprise plans sometimes include dedicated API support and SDKs for building connectors with internal systems.

## Support and onboarding

Software falls apart without decent support. Check these areas.

- **Knowledge base**: Searchable docs with video tutorials, step-by-step guides, and community forums go a long way. A solid knowledge base cuts down on paid support requests.

- **Response times**: Chat should reply within five minutes during business hours. Email should get an acknowledgment within 24 hours. Phone availability varies by plan tier.

- **Onboarding help**: Some vendors assign dedicated onboarding specialists for teams over 20 users. Others offer self-service video courses. Pick whichever fits your team.

- **Training**: Look for certified programs, live webinars, and user communities. Active communities often solve problems faster than official channels.

## Security and compliance

Security is a must for enterprise software. These are the baselines.

- **SOC 2 Type II**: An independent audit of security controls. Standard for enterprise-grade SaaS.

- **GDPR and CCPA**: Required if you serve customers in Europe or California. Look for data processing agreements, right-to-erasure workflows, and data residency options.

- **SSO and MFA**: Single sign-on using SAML 2.0 or OIDC, plus multi-factor authentication, protect against credential theft. Check which identity providers are supported.

- **Data encryption**: AES-256 at rest and TLS 1.3 in transit are industry norms. Verify where your data is stored geographically.

- **Audit logs**: Detailed activity logs show who changed what and when. Essential for compliance and troubleshooting.

Enterprise PM tools promise smooth collaboration, but most teams hit a wall past 500 users. The listed price rarely includes the mandatory Enterprise Success Package, which adds 20 to 30 percent to annual costs. You will not discover this until the final contract review.

## What you will actually pay

Most enterprise PM tools advertise a per-user price, but the real cost comes from add-ons. Asana Enterprise Plus starts at $30 per user per month. Enabling SCIM provisioning or custom SAML adds another $5 per user. That brings the total to $35. Add in the $10,000 to $25,000 one-time implementation fee for teams over 1,000 users. This detail sits in their [official pricing FAQ](https://asana.com/pricing) under Enterprise Deployment Services.

Smartsheet and Monday.com follow a similar model. Smartsheet Enterprise is $25 per user per month. Premium support with 24/7 phone access bumps it to $35. Monday.com Enterprise is $24 per user per month. Their Advanced Reporting add-on adds another $8 per user per month. These are not optional. They are table stakes.

## Where it works and where it does not

### The good: features that scale

**1. Workload balancing**
ClickUp and Wrike offer workload views that actually work for enterprise teams. Unlike Jira basic capacity planning, these let you set custom thresholds by role, such as a max of five tasks per day for UX designers. The system auto-rebalances when someone hits 80 percent capacity. A 200-person product team cut weekly planning from two hours to 20 minutes.

**2. Cross-departmental dependencies**
Enterprise projects fail when finance, legal, and engineering operate in silos. Microsoft Project Master Project links sub-projects across departments while keeping individual timelines intact. A G2 review from May 2026 noted: "We cut cross-team dependency delays by 40 percent after migrating from Asana, which still lacks true multi-project linking."

**3. Audit trails for compliance**
Regulated industries like healthcare and finance cannot skip audit logs. Smartsheet Activity Log tracks every change. Who edited a cell, when, and from which IP address. It retains data for 10 years. Most tools only keep logs for 90 days, which becomes a liability during SOC 2 audits.

### The bad: what users complain about

**1. Mobile apps feel bolted on**
Enterprise users are often in the field, but most PM tools treat mobile as a checkbox. Jira mobile lacks offline mode and crashes when loading sprints with over 50 issues. A Reddit thread from March 2026 called it "a glorified to-do list with none of the power of the desktop version."

**2. Integration friction**
Tools advertising 1,000-plus integrations still have gaps. Monday.com Salesforce integration syncs contacts but not custom objects. Sales ops teams need both. Their [official documentation](https://support.monday.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000000000) admits this, but it does not appear on marketing pages.

**3. Reporting misses the mark**
Enterprise teams need both high-level dashboards and granular data. Most tools fail at one or the other. Asana reporting is simple but lacks custom SQL. Microsoft Project reporting is so complex that teams often export to Power BI, adding another tool to the stack.

## The rough edges

**1. Migration is painful**
Moving between PM tools is hard. Enterprise migrations are worse. A 2025 Gartner survey found that 68 percent of enterprise teams underestimated migration effort. The average project ran four to six months behind schedule. The biggest culprit: custom fields and automation rules that do not map cleanly. Jira to ClickUp migrations, for instance, often lose issue dependencies because Jira blocks relationship does not exist in ClickUp.

**2. Permissions are overcomplicated**
Enterprise tools add layers like Project Admin, Workspace Admin, and Org Admin, but most teams end up with either too much or too little access. A common TrustRadius complaint is that Smartsheet forces teams to grant full edit access or none at all. There is no middle ground for view-only with comments.

**3. AI features miss the mark**
Most PM tools now offer AI-powered task suggestions or auto-generated reports, but enterprise teams find them unreliable. A 2026 G2 review of Wrike AI noted: "It suggested assigning a task to a developer who left the company six months ago. The data is not fresh enough to be useful."

## Who should and should not use it

**Go with Asana if:**
- Your team is under 1,000 users and values simplicity over granular controls.
- You are in marketing or creative services and need visual project timelines.

**Avoid Asana if:**
- You are in a regulated industry like HIPAA or SOC 2 and need long-term audit logs.
- Your workflows rely on complex dependencies across 50-plus projects.

**Go with Microsoft Project if:**
- You are already deep in the Microsoft ecosystem (Azure, Power BI, Teams).
- Your projects involve heavy resource allocation, such as construction or engineering.

**Avoid Microsoft Project if:**
- Your team uses Macs or Linux. Desktop app performance is noticeably worse than on Windows.
- You need a tool that non-technical teams like HR or finance can adopt without training.

**Go with Smartsheet if:**
- Compliance is a top priority. Audit logs and data retention are handled well.
- You need a balance between spreadsheets and project management.

**Avoid Smartsheet if:**
- Your team lives in mobile apps. Smartsheet mobile experience is clunky.
- You rely on deep integrations with tools like Salesforce or HubSpot.

Pick the tool based on which limitations you can live with. Smartsheet audit trails may be worth the mobile trade-offs if you are in a regulated industry. ClickUp workload balancing could save hours per week for a product team. Microsoft 365 shops may find Project the path of least resistance even if it is not the most elegant solution. Pilot with your most complex workflow first.
