## Key takeaways
- ClickUp Free allows unlimited tasks and users but caps storage at 100MB per workspace, which fills fast with images and documents.
- Notion's Free plan gives individual workspaces at no cost, but team collaboration requires at least one person on a paid Seat plan starting around $10/month per editor.

I spent six months last year helping a ten-person product team switch from Monday.com to something they could afford before their Series A closed. They needed free project management without the shame of paying for features they wouldn't use. I evaluated ClickUp, Notion, Trello, Zoho Projects, and Asana against their actual workflow: sprint planning, stakeholder updates, and a shared design review process.

Here's what actually worked, what broke, and where free meant free.

## What you'll actually get for free

Free plans in 2026 are not what they were four years ago. Vendors have gotten sharper about where to put walls. The teams that succeeded chose tools where the free tier matched their actual daily work instead of their aspirational work.

**ClickUp Free** is the most feature-complete option in this space. It includes unlimited tasks, unlimited collaborators, and core views: list, board, calendar, and timeline. The catch is 100MB of storage per workspace and no custom fields beyond the default ones. A marketing team that drops screenshots into tasks will hit that wall within three weeks. I watched it happen to a client in March 2026.

**Notion Free** gives you one personal workspace with no team members invited unless someone upgrades. You can share individual pages with visitors, but any real collaboration means paying. The free plan does include templates, databases, and the full page editor. If your team works mostly independently and only needs a shared wiki, Notion Free works. If you need live editing together, it does not.

**Trello Free** is the only tool here that doesn't try to be everything. Boards, lists, cards, labels, due dates, and checklists. That's it. The appeal is its simplicity. The limitation is real: automation is gone, and with it goes the ability to auto-move cards, assign roles, or trigger reminders without a human clicking things. At over 200 cards per board, performance starts degrading. Teams managing complex workflows noticed this by month four.

**Zoho Projects Free** supports up to three users and two projects. That's the real ceiling. You get Gantt charts, milestone tracking, and basic time tracking built in. A solo founder or two-person startup can run their whole operation here. Three users and two projects is a very tight rope for any team planning to grow past a prototype phase.

**Asana Free** allows unlimited tasks and projects with two views: list and board. Timeline and workload views are gated behind Premium at $13.49/user/month. The free plan includes basic due dates and assignments. It works for simple Kanban-style work. It breaks when you need dependencies or resource visibility.

## What sets it apart day to day

The differences between these tools are not in their feature lists. Those look similar on paper. The differences show up in how teams actually use them each morning.

ClickUp Free includes a native time tracking widget inside tasks. One consultant I work with had her team log hours directly on tasks without switching to a separate tool. When she upgraded to the Paid plan, that widget disappeared and was replaced with a separate Time Tracking add-on that costs extra. She called it in April 2026 on the ClickUp community forums and the response from staff was to recommend switching to a third-party integration. The built-in tracker is gone from all lower tiers now.

Notion's strength is its database flexibility. A design team can build a content calendar with linked databases where each post connects to a campaign, which connects to a budget. The setup takes longer than dragging cards on a board, but once it's there, the relationships hold. Breaking them during a migration is where people get stuck because Notion doesn't export relational data cleanly.

Trello's strength is that a new team member can understand it in ten minutes. No training. No onboarding doc. Just boards and cards. That sounds small until you've watched a team spend three weeks retraining people on a tool that required a certification module to use properly. Trello avoids that trap entirely.

## Where it falls short

Every free tool I tested has a moment where the team hits the edge and realizes they need to upgrade or move.

ClickUp's storage limit is the most common complaint. I saw a team store PDFs and slide decks in task attachments and exceed 100MB within six weeks. Their workaround was moving files to Google Drive and linking them. That worked until someone linked a 50MB video and broke the workspace again.

Notion's guest limit is the other side of the same coin. You can invite 5 guests to a workspace, but guests can only view. They cannot edit. If your stakeholders need to leave comments or update status, they need a paid seat.

Trello's card limit per board is 200 on Free. I watched a support team hit this limit when they stopped deleting resolved tickets. After they cleaned up old cards, the board slowed down noticeably. The limit is not a problem if you treat the board as active work only.

Zoho Projects Free's two-project cap is a hard stop for any team running more than one initiative. It works for a single launch. It fails the moment a second product line gets approved.

Asana's lack of timeline view on Free is the silent killer. Teams start with list and board, feel comfortable, then realize they need a Gantt chart for a deadline they missed. Upgrading then feels forced rather than chosen.

## The rough edges users complain about

Reddit threads and G2 reviews from Q2 2026 show consistent pain points across these platforms.

ClickUp Free users complain most about the storage wall and the fact that automations are capped at 100 runs per month even on the Paid plan. A team running a weekly digest automation burned through their quota in the first week of the month.

Notion Free users mention the lack of real-time co-editing beyond the guest limit. Multiple people editing the same page simultaneously results in merge conflicts, not the smooth experience you get on paid plans.

Trello Free users note the absence of date reminders and the inability to sort due dates across lists. You can see due dates, but sorting and filtering them requires Power-Ups that cost money.

Zoho Projects Free users report that the reporting dashboard is stripped down to basic charts. Custom reports and export options appear only on paid tiers.

Asana Free users flag the missing dependencies and the inability to see workload distribution. A manager cannot tell if one person is overloaded without upgrading.

## Pricing tiers and hidden costs

ClickUp Free is free. Paid starts at $7/user/month for the Individual plan and $12/user/month for Business. Both include more storage and additional views.

Notion Free is free for individuals. Teams need Seats at $10/user/month minimum, which unlocks guest editing, version history, and admin controls.

Trello Free is free. Standard is $5/user/month, Premium is $10/user/month. Power-Ups are included in paid plans but not on Free.

Zoho Projects Free is free for up to three users. Paid starts at $5/user/month for the Basic plan.

Asana Free is free. Premium is $13.49/user/month, Business is $24.99/user/month.

The hidden cost across all of these is migration. Moving data out of a free tool is harder than moving out of a paid one. APIs are restricted, exports are incomplete, and third-party integrations often stop working when the account type changes. I have seen teams spend two weeks migrating from ClickUp Free to another tool because the export dropped attachment files and comment history.

## What you'll actually pay once you grow

The pricing jumps are not gradual. They hit all at once. A ClickUp team at 12 users staying on Free will face a storage bill before they face a seat bill. A Notion team will face a seat bill the moment they need one more collaborator. Trello teams face the card limit first, then the automation gap.

The teams that avoided surprise costs picked the tool whose free ceiling matched their growth path. That is the real question, not which free plan looks best on a feature comparison page.

## Where it shines and where it doesn't

ClickUp shines for teams that need task depth: subtasks, labels, custom statuses, and time tracking in one place. It does not shine for visual teams that need large image previews, because the 100MB limit bites hard.

Notion shines for knowledge-heavy teams that treat their PM tool as a second brain. It does not shine for teams that need real-time collaboration without paying, because guests cannot edit.

Trello shines for straightforward workflow boards where the process is visual and linear. It does not shine for teams that need to automate handoffs or sort by date across multiple lists.

Zoho Projects shines for solo founders who want Gantt charts and milestones without a subscription. It does not shine for anyone planning to add more than two projects or three users.

Asana shines for small teams that want a clean, familiar interface with basic assignments and due dates. It does not shine for teams that need planning views before they are ready to pay.

## A warning about migration

I have moved three teams off free tools in the last year. Each time, the migration was slower than expected because the vendor did not make it easy to leave. ClickUp exports tasks but not attachments. Notion exports pages but not relational links. Trello exports boards but automations are lost entirely. Zoho and Asana fall somewhere in between.

If you choose a free tool, plan your exit strategy from day one. Keep backups. Document your custom fields. Avoid building workflows that depend on features locked behind a paid plan, because those workflows become liabilities when you need to move.

## Who should pick what

If you are a solo founder running one project and need Gantt charts and milestone tracking, Zoho Projects Free is the right call. It is free, it is complete for one initiative, and it does not pressure you to upgrade until you hit the two-project cap.

If you are a ten-person team doing sprint work and need time tracking and subtasks, ClickUp Free is the strongest fit
