## How I ended up evaluating HR tools

I've watched three startups in the past two years pick the wrong HR platform and spend six months migrating away from it. One founder told me they chose a tool because it looked clean in the demo, then discovered during implementation that their state-specific payroll tax setup required a third-party add-on costing $150 a month. Another team picked a platform that promised full onboarding automation, only to find that setting up offer letters, document signing, and equipment provisioning required manual steps for half the workflow. These aren't edge cases. They're the reason I now spend time with real teams before recommending anything.

The question isn't which HR tool has the most features. It's which one will actually work when you have 15 people and need to add five more by Q2 without hiring an ops person.

## What the market actually looks like

Four platforms dominate the startup HR conversation right now. Gusto, Rippling, BambooHR, and Deel each target slightly different startup profiles. Gusto is strongest for US-based teams that want payroll and benefits under one roof. Rippling appeals to companies that want to manage software licenses and devices alongside HR. BambooHR works well for teams focused on people operations and culture tracking. Deel is built for companies hiring internationally from day one.

Choosing between them comes down to three questions: where your employees are located, how much you need payroll versus HR management, and whether you already have a separate financial system you need to connect to.

## What you'll actually pay

Gusto charges $40 per month plus $6 per employee for its Core plan and $12 per employee for the Premium plan which includes benefits administration. A ten-person team on Core pays $100 monthly. The same team on Premium pays $160. Add-ons like time tracking start at $3 per employee per month.

Rippling's base price is $8 per employee per month for HR. IT management is another $4 per device. Payroll runs separately and starts around $8 per employee as well. A ten-person team managing HR and one software license per person pays roughly $160 monthly before payroll kicks in.

BambooHR does not publish pricing publicly. Teams report paying between $8 and $12 per employee per month depending on contract length and features selected. Their professional services tier adds another $25 to $40 per hour for implementation help.

Deel costs $599 per month minimum for contractor management. Full employment contracts through Deel EOR run $599 per employee per month when hiring internationally. A startup with five international contractors pays $2,995 monthly.

## Where each platform actually shines

Gusto handles payroll correctly out of the box. Set up your salary bands, configure tax withholdings, and the system calculates everything. The benefits marketplace lets you add health insurance, 401k, and other perks without calling three different vendors. Teams I've worked with reported that switching from a manual payroll process to Gusto cut their monthly admin time from eight hours to under two.

Rippling's unified model works when you already have friction between HR and IT departments. One team I advised was losing thirty minutes every time someone joined because HR created the employee record in one system and IT provisioned devices in another. Rippling eliminated that gap entirely. The same team noted that the initial configuration took three weeks because the platform assumes you have someone who understands both HR and IT workflows.

BambooHR excels at employee self-service and performance reviews. The recruiting module integrates cleanly, and the reporting dashboard gives you headcount, turnover, and compensation data without exporting to spreadsheets. A twelve-person SaaS company told me they switched from Google Sheets to BambooHR's People CRM and reduced their time-to-hire from six weeks to four weeks because candidates stayed visible throughout the process instead of going into a shared inbox.

Deel solves a problem most startups don't know they have until they try to hire someone in another country. You can issue contracts, process payroll, and handle compliance in Mexico, Portugal, or Singapore without setting up local entities. The contract template library covers 150 countries. What the marketing pages don't mention is that Deel takes a percentage cut on top of the $599 monthly minimum, which for a single hire in a high-cost country can exceed $100 per month in fees.

## The rough edges

Gusto doesn't include native time-off management. You either add a $3 per employee per month module or integrate with a third-party tool like Tripline. The integration exists but requires configuring sync rules that break when policy changes mid-year. I've seen two teams lose a full workweek reconnecting their time-off data after changing accrual rules.

Rippling's strength is also its weakness. The platform tries to manage everything, which means the configuration surface is enormous. A startup with ten people and no IT staff will spend more time learning Rippling than they save in automation. The mobile app works for basic tasks but lacks offline mode, so managers in areas with spotty connectivity can't process approvals.

BambooHR lacks native time-off accrual rules. You can track PTO balances manually or use their Time-Off app, but the base system doesn't handle accruals, carryover caps, or state-specific sick leave requirements. Teams in California or New York with mixed full-time and part-time staff find themselves building custom reports just to stay compliant.

Deel's onboarding flow assumes every new hire needs the same documents and verification steps. Remote teams in different countries face a one-size-fits-all setup that doesn't always match local requirements. I spoke with a founder who spent two weeks clarifying document submissions with Deel's support team because the platform rejected her contractor's Portuguese ID as invalid while accepting a similarly styled Spanish document without question.

## The hidden cost nobody mentions

Migration effort is the silent budget item. Every platform I've evaluated requires at least two weeks of data cleanup before you can import anything useful. Employee records from spreadsheets, outdated job titles, inconsistent salary formats, and missing tax forms all need to be fixed before import. A ten-person team should budget five to seven hours for this. A fifty-person team should budget thirty to forty hours.

I tracked one startup that switched from BambooHR to Rippling. Their migration took eleven days instead of the three the vendor estimated because BambooHR exported time-off data in a format Rippling didn't accept. The team ended up rebuilding six months of PTO history from scratch.

## Which one should you actually pick

A ten-person US-based startup doing $2 million in revenue should pick Gusto. You need payroll, benefits, and basic HR without drowning in configuration. The $100 monthly price point leaves room in your budget for a project manager or two.

A fifteen-person startup with remote contractors in three countries should pick Deel. The $599 monthly minimum pays for itself once you stop figuring out international contractor compliance on your own. The alternative is spending eight hours per month on legal research and payment routing.

A twenty-five-person startup with a dedicated people operations person should pick BambooHR. The reporting and performance management tools justify the price when someone is actively managing employee data rather than a founder wearing multiple hats.

A thirty-person startup that wants to automate offboarding, equipment returns, and software license provisioning should evaluate Rippling. The platform pays for itself when you eliminate the back-and-forth between HR and IT, but only if someone has the bandwidth to configure it properly.

I've recommended Gusto for more startups than any other platform because it does the thing most companies need first — payroll and benefits — without requiring a team to learn a new operating system. The gaps in time-off tracking and advanced HR features fill in naturally as you grow. If your team stays under fifty people and operates domestically, Gusto handles the hard parts while you focus on selling.
