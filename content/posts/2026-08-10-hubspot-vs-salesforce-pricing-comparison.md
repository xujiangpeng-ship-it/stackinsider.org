Most teams pick HubSpot because the sticker price looks friendlier. Then they hit 50 users and realize Salesforce’s $25 seat suddenly costs $150 when you add the tools you actually need. The pricing pages won’t tell you that.

Here’s what I’ve seen working with teams from 10 to 2,000 people.

## What you’ll actually pay

HubSpot’s pricing starts at $20 per user per month for the Starter CRM Suite. That includes marketing, sales, and service hubs. The catch: you’re limited to 1,000 marketing contacts. Once you exceed that, the price jumps to $800 per month for 2,000 contacts. Each additional 5,000 contacts adds $250 per month. If you have 20,000 contacts, you’re looking at $1,550 per month just for the contact tier.

Salesforce’s pricing starts at $25 per user per month for the Sales Cloud Professional edition. That’s the base price. To get marketing automation (Pardot) or customer service (Service Cloud), you’ll pay an additional $1,250 per month for up to 10,000 contacts. Each user who needs access to multiple clouds pays for each one. A sales rep who also handles support tickets could cost $75 per month.

Neither platform includes implementation. HubSpot’s onboarding starts at $3,000 for a 30-day kickoff. Salesforce’s implementation partners typically charge $15,000 to $50,000 depending on complexity. I’ve seen mid-market companies spend $80,000 on Salesforce setup because they needed custom objects and integrations.

HubSpot’s Professional tier includes a free sandbox for 30 days during onboarding. After that, it’s an extra $750 per month. Salesforce includes a sandbox in the Enterprise edition.

Both platforms charge for storage. HubSpot gives you 10 GB included in Professional. Each additional GB is $4 per month. Salesforce starts with 10 GB for the org, plus 20 MB per user. Additional storage is $125 per GB per month. If you store a lot of attachments or emails, this adds up quickly.

## Where the money goes after the first year

HubSpot’s annual contract includes a 10% discount. Salesforce offers 20% for three-year commitments. But the real cost creep comes from add-ons.

HubSpot’s Operations Hub starts at $50 per month for 2 users. If you need custom code actions or data sync, you’ll pay $800 per month for the Professional tier. Salesforce’s MuleSoft Composer for Salesforce starts at $1,000 per month for 10,000 API calls. Teams that integrate with ERP systems often need the full MuleSoft Anypoint Platform, which starts at $10,000 per year.

Training is another hidden line item. HubSpot Academy offers free courses, but advanced certifications for admins cost $1,500 per person. Salesforce Trailhead is free, but the recommended instructor-led training runs $4,500 per week. I’ve seen companies budget $20,000 for initial training alone.

## What sets it apart

HubSpot’s strength is speed. You can spin up a campaign in 30 minutes without IT. The drag-and-drop email builder and landing page editor work out of the box. Teams under 50 people rarely need a dedicated admin. The mobile app lets reps log calls and update deals offline, which Salesforce only offers in the Enterprise edition.

Salesforce’s advantage is depth. Custom objects let you model complex sales processes. The reporting engine handles large datasets without slowing down. If you need to track renewals, upsells, and support cases in one view, Salesforce can do it. The platform also scales to thousands of users without performance hits. HubSpot’s UI starts to lag with 500+ active users.

## The rough edges

HubSpot’s reporting is limited. You can’t build cross-object reports without the Operations Hub. The dashboard filters reset every time you refresh, which frustrates sales managers. The API rate limits can bottleneck data syncs if you integrate with multiple tools. I’ve seen teams hit the 100,000 call limit in the Professional tier and have to upgrade to Enterprise just for API access.

Salesforce’s complexity is its biggest weakness. The setup requires a dedicated admin. The permission structure is powerful but confusing. I’ve seen companies spend months cleaning up profiles and roles after a botched implementation. The mobile app is clunky compared to HubSpot’s. Offline mode is only available in the Enterprise edition, which costs $165 per user.

## Who should pick which

Pick HubSpot if you’re under 200 users, need to move fast, and don’t have a dedicated IT team. The pricing is predictable, and you won’t need a full-time admin. It’s a good fit for SaaS startups, agencies, and professional services firms that rely on inbound marketing.

Pick Salesforce if you’re over 200 users, have complex sales processes, or need deep customization. The platform handles enterprise needs like territory management and advanced forecasting. It’s the default choice for manufacturing, healthcare, and financial services companies that need compliance and audit trails.

If you’re in the middle—100 to 200 users—run a pilot with both. HubSpot’s Professional tier will cost about $10,000 per year for 50 users. Salesforce’s Enterprise edition will run $100,000 for the same team size. The difference isn’t just the license cost. It’s the admin overhead, training, and integration work. Most teams in this range end up with HubSpot because the total cost of ownership is lower. But if you need the customization, the extra spend is worth it.
