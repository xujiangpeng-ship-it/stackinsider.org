Most CRMs promise to organize your client work. Most fail. The ones that look simple on a demo video often turn into a mess when you try to use them for actual consulting projects. You don’t need another dashboard to stare at—you need a tool that handles proposals, invoices, and follow-ups without forcing you to change how you work.

Here’s what I’ve learned after helping teams migrate between these tools. None are perfect, but some fit consulting work better than others.

## What consultants actually need from a CRM

Consultants don’t need enterprise sales pipelines. You need:

- A way to track project status without switching between spreadsheets and email.
- Invoicing that doesn’t require exporting data to another tool.
- Client portals or shared docs so you’re not emailing contracts back and forth.
- Mobile access that works offline, because client sites rarely have good Wi-Fi.
- Integrations with tools you already use (QuickBooks, Zoom, Google Workspace).

Most CRMs focus on sales teams. Consultants end up paying for features they don’t use, like lead scoring or territory management. The tools below avoid that trap.

## The 4 options that fit consulting work

### 1. HoneyBook
HoneyBook is built for service providers, not sales teams. It handles contracts, payments, and client communication in one place. The interface is clean, and the mobile app works well offline.

**Pricing:** Starts at $19/month (billed annually) for the Starter plan. The Essentials plan at $39/month includes automation and client portals. Teams of 3+ need the Premium plan at $79/month.

**What works:**
- Contracts and invoices are built-in. You can send a proposal, get it signed, and collect payment without leaving the app.
- Client portals keep all project files and messages in one place. No more digging through email threads.
- Automations handle reminders and follow-ups. You can set it to send a thank-you note after payment or a check-in before a project starts.

**What doesn’t:**
- Reporting is basic. If you need detailed financial tracking, you’ll still need QuickBooks.
- No native time tracking. You’ll need to integrate with Toggl or Harvest.
- The mobile app lacks some features, like editing contracts.

**Best for:** Solo consultants or small teams who want an all-in-one tool for client management and payments.

### 2. Dubsado
Dubsado is similar to HoneyBook but with more customization. It’s a good fit if you need to tailor workflows for different types of projects.

**Pricing:** $20/month for the Starter plan (billed annually). The Premier plan at $40/month adds automation and unlimited projects. Both plans include all features—no upsells for basic functionality.

**What works:**
- Customizable forms and workflows. You can create different onboarding sequences for coaching clients vs. design projects.
- Scheduling tool integrates with Google Calendar. Clients can book calls without back-and-forth emails.
- Invoices and payments are built-in. You can set up recurring payments for retainers.

**What doesn’t:**
- The interface feels dated. It’s functional but not as polished as HoneyBook.
- No offline mode on mobile. If you’re on a client site with bad reception, you’re stuck.
- Learning curve is steeper. The customization options can be overwhelming if you just want something simple.

**Best for:** Consultants who need to tailor workflows for different client types or industries.

### 3. Pipedrive
Pipedrive is a sales CRM, but it works for consultants who need to track leads and projects in one place. It’s less service-focused than HoneyBook or Dubsado but more flexible.

**Pricing:** Starts at $14.90/user/month (billed annually) for the Essential plan. The Advanced plan at $27.90/user/month adds automation and email sync. The Professional plan at $49.90/user/month includes revenue forecasting.

**What works:**
- Pipeline view is intuitive. You can see where every project stands at a glance.
- Integrates with Zapier, so you can connect it to tools like QuickBooks or Slack.
- Mobile app is solid. You can update deals and log calls on the go.

**What doesn’t:**
- No built-in contracts or invoicing. You’ll need to integrate with DocuSign or QuickBooks.
- Client portals aren’t included. You’ll need to use a third-party tool like Google Drive or Dropbox.
- Pricing adds up quickly for teams. The per-user cost is low, but it’s easy to outgrow the lower tiers.

**Best for:** Consultants who also do sales and need a flexible tool for tracking leads and projects.

### 4. Zoho CRM
Zoho CRM is the most customizable option here. It’s overkill for solo consultants but a good fit for teams that need to track complex projects.

**Pricing:** Free for up to 3 users. The Standard plan is $14/user/month (billed annually). The Professional plan at $23/user/month adds automation and inventory management. The Enterprise plan at $40/user/month includes AI features.

**What works:**
- Highly customizable. You can tailor it to fit almost any workflow.
- Integrates with the rest of Zoho’s suite (Books for invoicing, Projects for task management).
- AI features (like lead scoring) can be useful if you’re juggling multiple clients.

**What doesn’t:**
- Steep learning curve. The customization options can be overwhelming.
- Mobile app is clunky. It’s usable but not as smooth as Pipedrive’s.
- Support is hit-or-miss. Some users report slow response times.

**Best for:** Teams that need a customizable tool and are already using Zoho’s other products.

## What you’ll actually pay

HoneyBook and Dubsado have straightforward pricing. You’ll pay $20–$40/month for most solo consultants. Teams of 3+ will need the higher tiers, which can run $80–$100/month.

Pipedrive and Zoho CRM charge per user. A team of 5 on Pipedrive’s Advanced plan will pay $1,674/year. Zoho CRM is cheaper at first, but costs add up if you need integrations or support.

Hidden costs to watch for:
- Payment processing fees. HoneyBook and Dubsado charge 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction.
- Integrations. Pipedrive and Zoho CRM may require paid add-ons for invoicing or contracts.
- Training. Dubsado and Zoho CRM have steeper learning curves, so you might need to budget for onboarding.

## The rough edges

HoneyBook’s reporting is weak. If you need to track revenue by client or project, you’ll need to export data to a spreadsheet.

Dubsado’s mobile app is limited. You can view client info but can’t edit contracts or forms offline.

Pipedrive lacks built-in invoicing. You’ll need to integrate with QuickBooks or another tool, which adds complexity.

Zoho CRM’s interface feels cluttered. The customization options are powerful but can make the tool harder to use.

## Which one should you pick?

If you’re a solo consultant or small team, start with HoneyBook. It’s the easiest to set up and covers the basics (contracts, invoices, client portals) without extra tools.

If you need more customization, try Dubsado. It’s a good fit for consultants who work with different types of clients and need tailored workflows.

If you’re also doing sales and need a flexible tool, Pipedrive is a solid choice. It’s not built for service providers, but it’s adaptable enough to work for consulting projects.

If you’re part of a larger team or need deep customization, Zoho CRM is worth the effort. It’s overkill for most solo consultants but a good fit for teams that need to track complex projects.

Avoid tools that force you to change how you work. The best CRM for consultants is the one that fits your existing workflows—not the one with the most features.
