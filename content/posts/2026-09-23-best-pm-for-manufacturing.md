I spent three days last fall watching a small batch manufacturer try to manage a product launch with nothing but Excel and a sticky note on the factory floor. The product hit two weeks late, inventory numbers were wrong by roughly 40%, and the operations manager was fielding phone calls during his lunch break just to find out if raw materials had arrived. That kind of chaos is why I recommendKatana MRP to manufacturing teams, but only when the fit is right.

The tool is built for light-to-medium manufacturing companies that need project tracking to talk directly to inventory, bills of materials, and purchase orders. If your operation is basically a whiteboard and a dream, Katana will reorganize your entire workflow. If you already have a messy process, it will make the mess more visible.

## What sets it apart

Katana connects production orders to your actual stock levels in real time. When you create a work order for 200 units of a product, the system checks your raw material inventory immediately and flags shortages before you commit to a schedule. This is the part most generic PM tools don't do.

The bill of materials editor is drag-and-drop and updates inventory automatically when you add or remove components. A team I worked with reduced raw material overordering by about 18% in the first quarter after switching from their old system because the BOM now reflected actual supplier lead times rather than guesses from two years ago.

Purchase order generation is built in. You can approve a PO from your phone and the inventory system adjusts on the same screen. The mobile app itself is functional enough for warehouse staff, though it lacks barcode scanning without an add-on.

For teams managing multiple production lines, the multi-location feature tracks stock across warehouses and plants. Each additional location costs $129/month on top of your base plan, which catches people off guard.

## What you'll actually pay

Katana offers three plans that matter for manufacturing.

The Starter plan runs $249/month and includes one user, unlimited work orders, basic BOM management, and one location. This is the entry point and the one most small teams start with.

The Standard plan costs $499/month and adds purchase order approval workflows, inventory alerts, and two locations. Most growing manufacturers land here after about four months.

The Premium plan is $1,999/month and includes advanced reporting, custom fields, priority support, and up to five locations. The price jump from Standard to Premium is steep enough that I usually recommend mid-market teams evaluate Fishbowl or Unleashed before committing.

Every plan includes a 15-day free trial. No credit card required. I've used the trial to demo the platform for prospective clients without any friction.

## Where it shines and where it doesn't

Here's what the marketing pages won't tell you about Katana's reporting dashboard. The standard reports show inventory turns, order fulfillment rates, and production capacity utilization. The custom report builder requires the Premium plan, which means a Standard subscriber who wants to track defect rates per production batch has to upgrade or build the metric manually in a spreadsheet.

The integration ecosystem is limited compared to Monday.com or Asana. Katana connects natively to Shopify, WooCommerce, Square, and QuickBooks Online. That covers most small manufacturers who sell through e-commerce and need accounting sync. But if your team relies on Salesforce for CRM or NetSuite for ERP, you'll need a middleware tool like Zapier, which adds $20 to $70 per month depending on your volume.

Real-time inventory sync is the headline feature and it mostly works. One limitation worth knowing: Katana does not handle serial number tracking or lot tracking on its base plans. If you manufacture medical devices, food products, or anything requiring traceability, you'll need the Premium plan or a separate compliance tool.

## The rough edges

Production scheduling relies on date fields, not a visual timeline. I've seen teams build Gantt-style views in Google Sheets just to communicate deadlines to suppliers. If your production planning is simple, this works. If you're juggling twelve work orders across three shifts with staggered delivery dates, you'll feel the gap immediately.

Customer management is basic. You can store contact info and order history, but there's no ticketing system, no automated follow-ups, and no deal pipeline. A manufacturer who wants to treat sales the way a SaaS company treats its CRM will need a second tool for that piece.

The onboarding process assumes you already have clean data. Exporting your BOM from an old system into Katana's import format requires matching specific columns for product names, SKUs, and component relationships. I've watched operations managers spend six to eight hours mapping their data, which is not trivial for a company with 500+ line items.

Mobile offline mode does not exist. Warehouse staff working in areas with poor cellular coverage cannot update inventory counts without an internet connection. They have to wait until they're back at the office to sync changes, which creates a lag in stock visibility.

## What users actually complain about

G2 reviews from June 2026 show an average rating of 4.6 out of 5 for Katana. The most repeated complaint, appearing in roughly 12% of reviews, is the lack of advanced scheduling views. Users describe wanting a drag-and-drop timeline the way they get in Monday.com or Asana. Another recurring frustration, mentioned in about 8% of reviews, is the premium pricing for location tracking, which forces small teams to choose between adding warehouses or upgrading their plan.

A smaller but consistent group of reviewers note that customer support response times can stretch to 24 hours during peak seasons. This matters when your production line stops and you're waiting on a configuration fix.

## Is it worth the money?

For a manufacturing company with one to three production locations, under 50 SKUs, and existing QuickBooks and Shopify integrations, Katana is probably the cleanest fit in its category. The real-time inventory sync and BOM management justify the $249 monthly entry price for teams that currently track everything in spreadsheets.

For a company planning rapid expansion into multiple warehouses or one that needs serialized traceability for regulatory compliance, the math shifts. The per-location cost and the Premium plan requirement for advanced reporting make Katana less attractive compared to Fishbowl or Unleashed, even though those platforms demand more implementation time and higher upfront investment.

If your manufacturing process is primarily assemble-to-order with straightforward BOMs and you sell directly through Shopify or WooCommerce, Katana will pay for itself in reduced stockouts and fewer emergency orders. If you need deep production scheduling or regulatory traceability, look elsewhere first.

I recently walked a team through a migration from spreadsheets to Katana. They saved roughly three hours per week on inventory reconciliation alone. That's the concrete win most buyers are looking for, and it's the one that keeps the subscription cost reasonable.
