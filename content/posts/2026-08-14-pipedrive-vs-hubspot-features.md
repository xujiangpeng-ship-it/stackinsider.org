Sales teams I've worked with usually start with one question: "Which CRM will let us close deals without fighting the tool?" Pipedrive and HubSpot both promise that. The reality is messier.

Pipedrive is built for salespeople who want a simple pipeline view and don’t need marketing automation. HubSpot is built for companies that want sales, marketing, and service tools in one place. That difference shows up in every feature.

## What you get out of the box

Pipedrive opens to a kanban-style pipeline. You see deals as cards, drag them between stages, and that’s it. The interface loads fast, even with 10,000 deals. Custom fields are easy to add, but you won’t find built-in email sequences or landing pages.

HubSpot opens to a dashboard with sales, marketing, and service modules. The pipeline view is there, but it’s one of many tabs. You get email templates, a basic website builder, and ticketing without extra setup. That breadth comes with complexity. New users often spend a week just figuring out where to click.

Both CRMs sync with Gmail and Outlook. Pipedrive’s sync is one-way (inbox to CRM) unless you pay for an add-on. HubSpot’s sync is two-way by default, but it can create duplicate contacts if you’re not careful.

## Features that actually change daily work

Pipedrive’s workflow automation is limited to deal stages and basic tasks. You can set up a rule like “when a deal moves to ‘proposal sent,’ create a follow-up task in 3 days.” That’s useful, but you can’t automate emails or update external tools without Zapier.

HubSpot’s automation includes emails, internal notifications, and updates to third-party apps. You can build a sequence that sends a contract, waits for a signature, then notifies finance—all without leaving HubSpot. The catch: automation is locked behind higher pricing tiers.

Reporting is where the gap widens. Pipedrive gives you pre-built reports on deal velocity and win rates. You can filter by team or product, but custom reports require exporting to Excel. HubSpot lets you build custom dashboards with sales, marketing, and service data. You can track how many leads from a campaign turned into closed deals, but the interface is cluttered.

Mobile apps tell the same story. Pipedrive’s app lets you update deals, log calls, and add notes. HubSpot’s app does that plus lets you edit marketing emails and view service tickets. Neither app works offline.

## What you’ll actually pay

Pipedrive’s pricing starts at $14.90 per user per month when billed annually. That includes the pipeline view, basic automation, and reporting. Email sync costs extra. The next tier ($27.90) adds workflow automation and custom fields. Enterprise ($99) adds revenue forecasting and advanced permissions.

HubSpot’s sales hub starts at $15 per user per month, but that tier is bare. You get contact management and email tracking, but no automation or reporting. The next tier ($90) adds automation and custom reports. Enterprise ($150) adds predictive lead scoring and playbooks.

Both CRMs charge for extra contacts. Pipedrive’s limit is 10,000 contacts on the lowest tier. HubSpot’s limit is 1,000 contacts. After that, you pay $25–$50 per month for each additional 1,000 contacts.

## Where each CRM falls short

Pipedrive’s biggest limitation is its lack of built-in marketing tools. If you want to send newsletters or run ads, you’ll need to integrate with Mailchimp or another tool. The reporting is also basic. Teams that need to track marketing ROI or service metrics will hit walls.

HubSpot’s biggest limitation is cost. The starter tier is cheap, but you’ll outgrow it fast. The professional tier is $90 per user per month, which adds up for a team of 10. The interface is also slow. Pages take 2–3 seconds to load, which frustrates salespeople who just want to update a deal.

Both CRMs have quirks. Pipedrive’s search is weak—you can’t search for deals by contact phone number. HubSpot’s duplicate contact problem is well-documented. Neither CRM lets you merge duplicate companies easily.

## Who should pick which

Pipedrive fits small sales teams that want a simple pipeline and don’t need marketing tools. It’s also a good fit for teams that use separate tools for marketing and service. The pricing is predictable, and the interface is fast.

HubSpot fits companies that want one tool for sales, marketing, and service. It’s also a good fit for teams that need advanced automation and reporting. The pricing is higher, but you get more features in one place.

If you’re a team of 5–10 salespeople who just need to track deals, Pipedrive is the better choice. If you’re a growing company that wants to align sales and marketing, HubSpot is worth the cost.

The next update to watch is HubSpot’s AI features. They’re rolling out predictive lead scoring and automated email replies. Pipedrive is adding AI too, but their focus is on deal insights, not marketing. Neither will replace a good salesperson, but both will change how teams work.
