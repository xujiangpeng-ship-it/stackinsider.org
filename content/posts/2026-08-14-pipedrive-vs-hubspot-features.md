Sales teams usually start with one question: which CRM will let them close deals without fighting the tool? Pipedrive and HubSpot both promise that. The reality is messier.

Pipedrive caters to salespeople who prefer a simple pipeline view and do not need marketing automation. HubSpot targets companies that want sales, marketing, and service tools in one place. This distinction affects every feature.

## What you get out of the box

Pipedrive loads a kanban-style pipeline. You see deals as cards and drag them between stages. The interface loads fast, even with 10,000 deals. Custom fields are easy to add. Built-in email sequences and landing pages are absent.

HubSpot opens to a dashboard with sales, marketing, and service modules. The pipeline view is one of many tabs. Email templates, a basic website builder, and ticketing are available without extra setup. That breadth creates complexity. New users often spend a week figuring out where to click.

Both CRMs sync with Gmail and Outlook. Pipedrive sync is one-way (inbox to CRM) unless you pay for an add-on. HubSpot sync is two-way by default, but it can create duplicate contacts if you are not careful.

## Features that affect daily work

Pipedrive workflow automation covers deal stages and basic tasks. You can set a rule like this: when a deal moves to "proposal sent," create a follow-up task in 3 days. That is useful. You cannot automate emails or update external tools without Zapier.

HubSpot automation includes emails, internal notifications, and updates to third-party apps. You can build a sequence that sends a contract, waits for a signature, then notifies finance. All of this happens inside HubSpot. Automation features are locked behind higher pricing tiers.

Reporting shows where the gap widens. Pipedrive provides pre-built reports on deal velocity and win rates. You can filter by team or product. Custom reports require exporting to Excel. HubSpot lets you build custom dashboards with sales, marketing, and service data. You can track how many leads from a campaign turned into closed deals. The interface is cluttered.

Mobile apps tell the same story. The Pipedrive app lets you update deals, log calls, and add notes. The HubSpot app does that plus lets you edit marketing emails and view service tickets. Neither app works offline.

## What you will actually pay

Pipedrive pricing starts at $14.90 per user per month when billed annually. That includes the pipeline view, basic automation, and reporting. Email sync costs extra. The next tier ($27.90) adds workflow automation and custom fields. Enterprise ($99) adds revenue forecasting and advanced permissions.

HubSpot sales hub starts at $15 per user per month. That tier is bare. You get contact management and email tracking. No automation or reporting is included. The next tier ($90) adds automation and custom reports. Enterprise ($150) adds predictive lead scoring and playbooks.

Both CRMs charge for extra contacts. Pipedrive allows 10,000 contacts on the lowest tier. HubSpot allows 1,000 contacts. After that, you pay $25–$50 per month for each additional 1,000 contacts.

## Where each CRM falls short

Pipedrive lacks built-in marketing tools. If you want to send newsletters or run ads, you need to integrate with Mailchimp or another tool. Reporting is also basic. Teams that need to track marketing ROI or service metrics will hit walls.

HubSpot is expensive. The starter tier is cheap, but you outgrow it fast. The professional tier costs $90 per user per month. That adds up for a team of 10. The interface is also slow. Pages take 2–3 seconds to load. Salespeople who just want to update a deal find this frustrating.

Both CRMs have quirks. Pipedrive search is weak. You cannot search for deals by contact phone number. The HubSpot duplicate contact problem is well-documented. Neither CRM lets you merge duplicate companies easily.

## Who should pick which

Pipedrive fits small sales teams that want a simple pipeline and do not need marketing tools. It also works for teams that use separate tools for marketing and service. Pricing is predictable. The interface is fast.

HubSpot fits companies that want one tool for sales, marketing, and service. It also works for teams that need advanced automation and reporting. Pricing is higher, but you get more features in one place.

If you are a team of 5–10 salespeople who need to track deals, Pipedrive is the better choice. If you are a growing company that wants to align sales and marketing, HubSpot is worth the cost.

Watch for HubSpot’s AI features next. They are rolling out predictive lead scoring and automated email replies. Pipedrive is adding AI too. Their focus is on deal insights, not marketing. Neither will replace a good salesperson. Both will change how teams work.
