The first thing you notice about Zoho CRM is the price. $14 per user per month for the Standard plan sounds cheap until you realize automation, custom reports, and extra storage cost extra. Teams I’ve worked with often hit the limits faster than expected, especially if they import historical data or run frequent email campaigns.

Zoho CRM is built for sales teams that need basic pipeline tracking without heavy customization. It works well for small businesses and mid-sized companies that don’t require deep integrations with niche tools. But if you’re coming from Salesforce or HubSpot, the differences in workflow and reporting will frustrate your team.

## What you’ll actually pay

Zoho CRM pricing starts at $14 per user per month for the Standard plan, billed annually. The Professional plan jumps to $23, Enterprise to $40, and Ultimate to $52. These prices exclude add-ons, which most teams end up needing.

Here’s what you’ll pay beyond the base cost:

- Automation: $10 per month for 10 workflows (Professional plan and below). Enterprise and Ultimate include 100 and 200 workflows, respectively.
- Storage: $4 per month for 5GB (Standard and Professional). Enterprise includes 100GB, Ultimate 1TB.
- Advanced analytics: $25 per month for Zoho Analytics integration (required for custom dashboards beyond basic reports).
- Telephony: $12 per user per month for Zoho Voice (if you need call logging and recording).
- Email parsing: $10 per month for Zia, the AI assistant that reads and categorizes emails.

A 10-person team on the Professional plan with automation, extra storage, and telephony will pay around $450 per month, not $230. That’s still cheaper than Salesforce but closer than the sticker price suggests.

## What sets it apart

Zoho CRM integrates tightly with the rest of the Zoho ecosystem. If you already use Zoho Books, Desk, or Campaigns, the data flows between apps without manual exports. The interface is consistent, so teams don’t waste time relearning basics.

The mobile app is functional. It’s not as polished as HubSpot’s, but it lets reps update deals, log calls, and view dashboards offline. That’s more than Salesforce offers without a separate license.

Zia, the AI assistant, automates simple tasks like lead scoring and email categorization. It’s not as advanced as Einstein AI, but it’s included in the Enterprise and Ultimate plans. For teams that don’t need predictive analytics, it’s enough to reduce manual data entry.

## Where it falls short

The reporting tools are weak. Custom reports require Zoho Analytics, which is an extra $25 per month. Even then, the interface feels clunky compared to HubSpot or Pipedrive. Teams that rely on detailed sales forecasts or pipeline velocity reports will struggle.

Integrations with non-Zoho tools are hit or miss. The native Slack integration is basic—it only posts notifications, not two-way sync. Zapier fills some gaps, but it adds another layer of cost and complexity. If your stack includes tools like Gong, Outreach, or Chili Piper, expect friction.

The automation builder is limited. You can’t trigger workflows based on field changes in related modules (e.g., updating a contact when a deal stage changes). This forces teams to use workarounds or pay for more workflows.

User adoption can be a problem. The interface feels dated compared to modern CRMs. New users often complain about the learning curve, especially if they’ve used HubSpot or Salesforce before. Zoho’s training materials help, but they’re not as polished as what you get from larger vendors.

## The rough edges

Zoho CRM’s support is inconsistent. The free plan includes only email support, with a 24-hour response time. Paid plans get phone support, but hold times can be long. Enterprise and Ultimate customers get a dedicated account manager, but smaller teams are left with community forums and documentation.

Storage limits are strict. A 10-person team on the Professional plan gets 50GB total. That’s enough for basic CRM data, but if you attach large files (contracts, presentations) or run email campaigns, you’ll hit the limit quickly. Extra storage costs $4 per 5GB, which adds up.

Migration from other CRMs isn’t seamless. Zoho provides a migration tool, but it doesn’t handle custom fields or automation rules well. Teams moving from Salesforce or HubSpot often need to rebuild workflows manually. I’ve seen migrations take weeks longer than expected because of this.

## Who it’s for

Zoho CRM fits small to mid-sized sales teams that:
- Need basic pipeline tracking and reporting.
- Already use other Zoho apps (Books, Desk, Campaigns).
- Don’t require deep integrations with niche tools.
- Want to avoid Salesforce’s complexity and cost.

It’s not a good fit for:
- Teams that rely on advanced reporting or predictive analytics.
- Companies using tools outside the Zoho ecosystem (e.g., Outreach, Gong).
- Large enterprises with complex sales processes.

If you’re considering Zoho CRM, start with the Professional plan and add automation and storage as needed. The 15-day free trial doesn’t require a credit card, so test it with real data before committing. Pay attention to storage limits and workflow restrictions—those are the hidden costs that catch teams off guard.

Zoho CRM is improving. The 2026 roadmap includes better reporting and deeper Slack integration. But for now, it’s a solid, affordable option for teams that don’t need the bells and whistles of more expensive CRMs. Just don’t expect it to do everything.
