## Key takeaways
- Freshsales starts at $15 per user per month on the Starter plan, while HubSpot's Sales Hub Professional begins at $900 per month minimum regardless of seat count.
- HubSpot's free CRM remains genuinely usable for small teams, but Freshsales free tier caps you at 1,000 contacts with no email tracking.
- Migration between the two platforms costs roughly 40 hours of team time for a mid-sized pipeline, including data cleanup and retraining.

---

I spent three weeks migrating a 25-person sales team from Freshsales into HubSpot last year. Half of them pushed back because Freshsales was cheaper and faster to set up. The other half agreed because they were drowning in manual data entry and needed marketing automation that Freshsales simply doesn't offer well. That tension comes up constantly when teams ask me which CRM to pick.

The short answer depends on whether you need a lean sales tracker or a full growth platform. Here's what actually matters day to day.

## What you'll actually pay

Freshsales pricing is per user. Starter runs $15 per user per month, Growth is $39, and Pro hits $69. All paid plans include a 14-day free trial and you get a functional free tier that covers basic pipeline management for up to 1,000 contacts.

HubSpot's model is different. The free CRM has no per-seat limit, but Sales Hub Starter starts at $15 per user per month with a 5-seat minimum, and Sales Hub Professional jumps to $80 per user per month. Here's the thing that catches people off guard: Marketing Hub Professional starts at $800 per month minimum and requires at least 10 seats even if you only need sales tools. You also can't layer individual HubSpot products as cheaply once you go past the free tier.

For a team of ten people running just sales, Freshsales at the Growth plan costs $390 monthly. The equivalent HubSpot setup with Sales Hub Professional runs $800 monthly. If marketing or service tools enter the picture, that gap widens fast.

## Features that actually matter

Both platforms handle the basics: contact records, deal pipelines, task assignments, and basic reporting. Where they diverge is in the tools teams use every single day.

Freshsales includes caller ID through its Freshcaller integration natively. A rep can see company context before a call rings. The built-in email tracking shows opens and clicks without third-party tools. Its IQ bot handles data enrichment automatically, pulling company info from public records into contact records. These features come standard on paid plans.

HubSpot's strength is breadth. The free CRM includes email templates, meeting scheduling, and basic pipeline boards. Paid tiers add sequence automation, smart reporting, and quote generation. The app marketplace has over 2,000 integrations compared to Freshsales' roughly 300 native and partner connections. If your team already uses Salesforce, Marketo, or Shopify, HubSpot tends to have a tighter native integration.

I watched a team lose about 20 minutes per rep per day switching between Freshsales and their email client because Freshsales email sync was unreliable with their Microsoft 365 setup. They moved to HubSpot partly for that reason. Email tracking reliability varies by email provider and IT policy, and this is one area where platform choice matters more than features on paper.

## Where Freshsales shines and where it stumbles

Freshsales excels at speed. New reps get productive in a day or two. The interface is clean, pipelines are intuitive, and the IQ data enrichment means less manual entry. The mobile app works well for field reps who need to log calls and update deals from their phone.

The limitation most buyers miss is that Freshsales does not have a true marketing automation engine. You can send drip emails through sequences, but you cannot build complex multi-channel workflows, run A/B tests at scale, or manage lead scoring beyond basic rule-based triggers. If your sales team relies on marketing-qualified leads generated through nurture campaigns, you will hit a wall.

HubSpot handles that end to end. Marketing, sales, and service hubs connect under one platform. The downside is complexity. A team that only needs CRM and basic pipeline management will spend more time configuring HubSpot than they save. The platform rewards deep investment but punishes light usage with a steeper learning curve.

## The rough edges

Freshsales gets complaints about its reporting. Custom reports require workarounds and the dashboard builder feels rigid compared to HubSpot's drag-and-drop approach. Several teams I advised asked for cohort analysis and went unsupported.

HubSpot's biggest user complaint is pricing transparency. Sales reps and managers often see different cost estimates depending on which hub they browse. The switch from free to paid can surprise teams that grew organically on the free tier. One prospecting team of 12 discovered their bill would nearly triple when they added a second sales rep, because HubSpot counts seats across all hubs when you upgrade.

Data migration is another friction point. Freshsales allows easier export to CSV but formatting can be inconsistent. HubSpot's import tool is more forgiving but validates strictly, so bad data gets rejected rather than imported with errors. Both platforms require data cleanup before migration, and that step gets overlooked every time.

## Comparison at a glance

| Factor | Freshsales | HubSpot |
|---|---|---|
| Starting paid price | $15 per user/month | $15 per user/month (5-seat minimum) |

| Marketing automation | Basic email sequences only | Full multi-channel workflows |

| Migration effort | Low to moderate | Moderate to high |

## What the vendor pages won't tell you

Freshworks acquired ZoomPhone and folded VoIP capabilities directly into Freshsales. This is a genuine differentiator that Freshsales marketing highlights less than it should. Teams that handle high call volumes save on a separate VoIP subscription. HubSpot requires a third-party phone integration for the same capability.

On the other side, HubSpot's community and certification program is real infrastructure. The HubSpot Academy offers free training that actual translates into faster onboarding. Freshsales has documentation but no equivalent structured learning path. When a company hires five new reps in a quarter, HubSpot's training resources reduce time to productivity noticeably.

## Who should pick what

If you have fewer than 25 salespeople, rely primarily on cold calling and email outreach, and need CRM cost predictability, Freshsales delivers more functionality per dollar. I've seen startup sales teams run lean on Freshsales Growth for under $500 monthly while covering CRM, email tracking, and basic phone integration.

If your team spans sales, marketing, and customer support, or if you plan to scale past 50 users within two years, HubSpot's integrated approach saves time on integration management even if the monthly bill is higher. The platform compounds value as you add hubs, and the app marketplace reduces custom development needs.

A three-person e-commerce team selling through Shopify should probably start with Freshsales and migrate later if they outgrow it. A B2B services firm managing complex proposals, multi-stage pipelines, and inbound marketing should evaluate HubSpot first even if the initial cost is higher.

I recommend running a 30-day pilot on both platforms with your actual deal data before committing. Both offer free trials and the free CRM tiers are functional enough to test real workflows. The teams that skip this step usually discover pain points three months into a paid subscription.
